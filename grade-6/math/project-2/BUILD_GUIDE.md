# Grade 6 Math – M2 Coordinate Rescue: Lesson Build Guide

Project 2 of 4 · four weeks · eight 90-minute lessons (one individual, then one group, each week).

Built from the [M2 teacher plan](../../../standards/scope-and-sequence/math-projects/M2_COORDINATE_RESCUE.md). Rerun `standards/curriculum-build/project-build-guides/build_math_guides.py` after that plan changes; do not edit this file by hand.

## Project overview

**Essential question:** How can a map and a set of math rules show that a rescue plan will work?

**What students do:** Plot rescue sites on a grid, plan a route, and explain the supply rules with numbers, letters and graphs. Revise the plan when a route closes.

**Final product:** One two-page route file: a labeled coordinate map, horizontal/vertical distance calculations, a supply-rule table/graph/equation, and a constraint explanation. Attach short individual algebra annotations and a revised route after a closure.

**What good work shows:** Accurate coordinates and permitted distances; variables defined with units; expressions and equations explained; constraints represented correctly; a defensible route revision with individual reasoning.

## Project data

Use these exact numbers when building cards, keys and examples.

Fictional training map, not emergency advice: base O=(0,0); sites A=(-4,3), B=(2,3), C=(2,-2), D=(-4,-2). Travel only along horizontal/vertical segments; one grid unit is one km. A valid route O -> (-4,0) -> A -> B -> C -> D -> (-4,0) -> O has length 30 km. Supplies are 3 kits per person; y=3x. Transport capacity is 24 kits. Final case: sites A=(-3,2), B=(3,2), C=(3,-2), D=(-3,-2); initial perimeter-style base route length 26 km. Close the top segment A-B; students may return through base-row y=0 (one feasible full route is 30 km). No diagonal-distance formula, general signed arithmetic or two-step equations is required.

## Materials to make

None of these exist yet; each must be built and answer-checked before teaching. All project numbers are in **Project data** above; no outside website login or paid reading is required.

Produce quadrant maps including half-unit coordinates, horizontal/vertical number lines, variable cards, expression tiles, equation balance drawings, candidate-value cards, inequality lines, and linked table/graph/equation templates. ## How to run every lesson

**Reading and access:** Use the brief, short problem cards, labeled diagrams and tables listed in each lesson. Cap new prose at about 150-250 words per block, usually less; read the first complex prompt together. These are production limits, not measured reading levels. Explain vocabulary with a model, retain mathematical notation, offer enlarged/tactile grids and oral/text-to-speech access to directions. Keep the assessed reasoning the learner's own. Fade sentence frames and partially labeled diagrams before independent evidence; record continuing accommodations. Step 6 must inspect the actual wording, legibility and screen-reader order.

**Recording evidence:** Use the lesson-specific criteria below. Record each component as secure in this response, developing, or not yet evidenced, with date and support conditions; these descriptive records are not a school grading scale. Practice gets feedback. The final product integrates revised work. Keep group quality separate from each learner's fresh explanations. Sample fluency repeatedly without imposing an invented speed cutoff. Calculator checks follow selected written algorithms; use required accommodations and record conditions.

**Support and extension:** Follow the specific signal/action/recheck in each lesson. Use the budgeted feedback or workshop time; do not add hidden minutes. If the fresh recheck is unsuccessful, carry the exact component and response to the next named support window. Learners ready to extend may compare a second method or construct a counterexample within the same work time; do not add a second major product.

**Outside work:** Optional consolidation, at most two 20-minute practice periods per week (40 minutes/week). The prior plan proposed this duration; it is not a confirmed homework requirement. Introductions, essential project work and required evidence are planned in class. No new instruction or indispensable material access is assigned outside class. Entry-skill gaps may require a calendar decision; homework does not conceal that shortage.

### M2-W1-I — Locate signed quantities

*Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Explain contextual zero, opposites and rational order; distinguish value from distance to zero.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: signed context, opposites, rational position/order and magnitude. Practice: site note. Check: independent number-line card. Builds on: fractions and positive number lines.

**Resources and reading:** Coordinate Rescue brief, signed elevation/temperature cards and number lines. Read 6 short quantities; define elevation, opposite and absolute value with diagrams.

**Teach and model:** Model -2.5 m and +2.5 m relative to sea level; opposite quantities cancel to zero. Place -3/2, -0.5, 0 and 1.25 on horizontal and vertical lines. Explain -1.5 < -0.5 but |-1.5| > |-0.5|; show opposite of opposite and zero's opposite.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Individually annotate the map legend and locate signed observations on both line orientations.
2. Rank four locations by elevation and separately by distance from zero.
3. Write a short site priority note explaining why larger absolute value does not mean higher elevation.
4. Recheck one M1 computation in the retrieval window.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect a fresh card: place -1.25 and 3/4; compare -2 and -5 in context and by absolute value; evaluate -(-4)=4 and explain why zero is its own opposite; describe +3/-3 balancing to zero.

**How the work is judged:** Require labeled scale, contextual inequality and nonnegative distances. Credit a clear zero reference and opposite explanation, not just correct symbols.

**If students struggle:** If -5 is called higher than -2, walk a line from zero and compare positions before distances. Recheck -0.75 and -1.5. If zero is described as no possible location, use sea level and recheck a zero-dollar balance.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare two line orientations and fractional/decimal labels. M2-W1-G opening 10 minutes rechecks order/opposites before moving to quadrant reflections.

**Mathematical practice:** MP2: explain what zero and sign mean in each setting.

### M2-W1-G — Build and measure the route

*Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Use quadrant signs/reflections and axis-aligned distances; define a variable before later algebra.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: quadrant/reflection and horizontal/vertical distance. Practice: route and supply table. Check: fresh plotted points and segment explanations. Builds on: signed position and M1 ratios.

**Resources and reading:** Training map in the brief plus a half-grid overlay. Read four coordinate pairs and a 2 km per 10 min travel card. Define quadrant and reflection.

**Teach and model:** Plot (-4,3), (4,3), (-4,-3), (4,-3) as reflections. Show horizontal distance from (-4,3) to (2,3) is 4+2=6 km and vertical distance from (2,3) to (2,-2) is 3+2=5 km. Define x as people and 3x as kits; contrast x as varying count with one unknown count.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Each learner plots a rational point and its reflections.
2. Teams draw an allowed base-to-sites route and total labeled segments.
3. Each student checks a segment independently.
4. Add a ratio note: 2 km/10 min implies 30 km/150 min; write a table and explain the rate. No diagonal shortcuts.

**Individual work to collect:** Individually plot (-1.5,2), reflect in each axis and both; find distances to (2.5,2) and (-1.5,-1). Add a three-kits-per-person table for 2,4,6 people, describe the ratio and percent of 24 kits used by 4 people, and convert 2 km to meters.

**How the work is judged:** Require quadrant/reflection accuracy; 4 and 3 units with absolute-distance reasoning; 6/12/18 kits, ratio 3:1, 50%, 2000 m. Recurring ratio checks cover named components only.

**If students struggle:** If coordinates are reversed, trace horizontal then vertical with two colors; recheck (2,-0.5). If cross-axis distance is subtracted as 4-2, count intervals and recheck -3 to +1 = 4. Use the same method for a vertical segment.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare equal-unit maps and a map example on different points. Bring each segment annotation into M2-W2-I; opening retrieval revisits rational positioning and variable meanings.

**Mathematical practice:** MP6: distinguish coordinates from distances and include units.

### M2-W2-I — Write rules and explain expression structure

*Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Translate and evaluate expressions; distinguish a variable quantity from an unknown; begin equivalence reasoning.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: variable meanings, translation, expression parts and substitution. Practice: supply rule. Check: fresh expression/formula card. Builds on: M1 powers and distributive sums.

**Resources and reading:** Route file, supply-rule cards and expression tiles. Read four phrases, one formula and grouped-expression labels; teach coefficient, factor, term and quotient in context.

**Teach and model:** Define x as a count of people. Compare 3(x+2), 3x+2 and 3x+6; name outer product/factors and inner sum. In 3x+6 identify two terms and coefficient 3; name x/4 a quotient. Translate "5 fewer than x" as x-5. Evaluate 2x squared+3 at x=4 as 35 and V=lwh for l=2,w=3,h=4 as 24. Show 3(x+2)=3x+6 by distribution, not a single example.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Students label and translate four expressions, including a quotient and a subtraction phrase.
2. Build an expression describing a supply option and define its variable/domain.
3. Evaluate two candidate values with correct order.
4. Add a short rule annotation to the route file and compare two equivalent forms with tiles; retain the proof draft for the next lesson.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect an individual translation of twice the sum of n and 4; label 2(n+4), the coefficient/terms in 2n+8, and the quotient n/4; evaluate 2n squared+1 at n=3 and A=lw at l=2.5,w=4. Explain when n is an unknown versus a count that varies. Retrieve 18+30=6(3+5).

**How the work is judged:** Require 2(n+4), correct grouped parts, 19 and 10 square units; a variable definition must name the quantity and allowable values. A numerical example supports evaluation but does not prove equivalence.

**If students struggle:** If 2(n+4) becomes 2n+4, use two equal tile groups, then recheck 4(t+3). If substitution changes n squared to 2n, expand repeated multiplication and recheck 3n squared at n=2 =12.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare a small four-item card, not a long worksheet. Finish expression labels before independent evaluation. M2-W2-G opens with the proof draft; unresolved translation gets a 10-minute clinic there.

**Mathematical practice:** MP7: treat a grouped sum as one factor.

### M2-W2-G — Prove rules and solve addition/subtraction equations

*Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Justify equivalence structurally, test candidates, and solve the additive equation forms using balance.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: properties, all-input equivalence, candidate testing and additive equations. Practice: rule selection and supply revision. Check: short private annotations. Builds on: variable expressions and fraction arithmetic.

**Resources and reading:** Proof draft, tiles/balance drawings and candidate set {2,4,6}. Read 3 comparison cards and two short equations. This is the heaviest algebra lesson; limit each team to one proof and one route update.

**Teach and model:** Use swapping/regrouping to show commutative/associative properties; distribute 4(x+2)=4x+8 and collect x+x+x=3x. A counterexample disproves equivalence; one matching value does not prove it. Test x+2=6 and x<5 with {2,4,6}. Model x+2.5=7 and x-1.5=4 by equal changes to both sides.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Each student prepares one structural equivalence explanation.
2. Teams select equivalent supply rules and reject a look-alike with a counterexample.
3. Solve the two additive unknowns and update the route supply note.
4. Individually annotate a property and verify a solution; use a brief fraction/division retrieval strip during the opening window.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect during work: justify 2(x+5)=2x+10 by distribution, (x+2)+3=x+(2+3) by association, and x+5=5+x by commutation. Closing check: test {1,3,5} in x+2=5 and x<4; solve x+1.25=4 and x-2=3.5 with substitution. Opening retrieval: (3/4)/(1/2)=1.5 with inverse check and 816/24=34.

**How the work is judged:** Require general property reasoning; candidate sets {3} and {1,3}; solutions 2.75 and 5.5 checked in the originals. Mark practice supported if teacher supplies a step; take a fresh solo item later.

**If students struggle:** If one successful input is offered as proof, compare x+4 and 2x at x=4 then x=5. Recheck 5(x+1)=5x+5 with a structural explanation. If only one side changes, use a balance drawing and recheck x+3=8.

**Prepare and hand off:** Pre-sort cards so no copying consumes work time. If two concepts remain unresolved, use M2-W3-I opening clinic and W4-I workshop; retain separate equivalence, candidate and equation evidence.

**Mathematical practice:** MP3: justify a general rule and challenge a false one.

### M2-W3-I — Solve multiplicative needs and graph constraints

*Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Solve multiplication/division equation forms and distinguish strict from inclusive limits.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: multiplicative equations and four inequality forms. Practice: capacity note. Check: fresh equations and number-line constraints. Builds on: substitution, balance and signed order.

**Resources and reading:** Capacity card: at most 24 kits; equation cards with nonnegative fractions/decimals; number lines. Read 4 short constraints, explaining at most/at least.

**Teach and model:** Model 0.75x=6 -> x=8 and x/1.5=4 -> x=6 with inverse operations and substitution. Graph x<4, x>4, x<=4, x>=4 with open/closed endpoints. Explain infinitely many numerical solutions but only whole-number people counts in the route setting.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Students calculate two unknown quantities for the route.
2. Write each strict/inclusive inequality from a context and graph it.
3. Use 3 kits per person to identify the people capacity of 8; show the constraint and interpret it.
4. Add a half-page capacity explanation to the existing route file.

**Individual work to collect:** Individually solve (2/3)x=4 and x/0.5=7, verify each; write/graph t>2, t<5, t>=3 and t<=6 from four phrases. Give three solutions and explain why the unrestricted number-line solution set is infinite.

**How the work is judged:** Require x=6 and 3.5 with inverse checks, correct endpoints/rays, and distinction between numerical solutions and possible whole people. Do not require solving compound or two-step inequalities.

**If students struggle:** If an inclusive limit is open, ask whether the endpoint itself is allowed; recheck "at least 5". If x/0.5=7 is answered 14, substitute to expose the mismatch; use half-unit groups and recheck x/0.25=8 -> 2.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare only one example per equation form before independent practice. Send unresolved candidate, endpoint or inverse-operation evidence to the opening of M2-W3-G and W4-I workshop.

**Mathematical practice:** MP2: interpret the solution in its context.

### M2-W3-G — Link quantities and adjust the plan

*Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Link independent/dependent quantities through y=px, a table and a graph; revisit constraints.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: linked variables, equation, table and graph. Revisit: inclusive constraints. Practice: supply revision. Check: fresh relationship. Builds on: M1 ratios and M2 expressions.

**Resources and reading:** Supply rule y=3x and capacity 24 kits; table/axes and decimal arithmetic retrieval strip. Read one rule and two capacity statements.

**Teach and model:** Define x=people, y=kits; make rows x=0,2,4,6,8 and graph y=3x. Explain x chosen first and y determined by it. Compare y=2x and interpret the multiplier; keep this lesson at proportional nonnegative relationships.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Teams build linked table, graph and equation for their supply rule.
2. Test the capacity limit and revise one route supply count.
3. Each learner explains one row and the corresponding point.
4. On a short retrieval strip, revisit decimal costs and GCF/LCM/factored sums needed for pack scheduling; place this strip with individual evidence.

**Individual work to collect:** Individually represent y=2.5x for x=0,2,4 with labeled table/graph and independent/dependent explanation. Graph y<=10 as a numerical constraint on y. Retrieve GCF(24,40)=8, LCM(4,6)=12, 24+40=8(3+5), and 2.75x4=11 with a written algorithm.

**How the work is judged:** Require table y=0,5,10, correct graph scaling and explicit meaning of the multiplier. Graphing a line alone is insufficient. Recurrence evidence checks the stated arithmetic/number-structure components.

**If students struggle:** If axes are swapped without changing the rule, ask which quantity is selected first and trace one row into the graph; recheck y=4x at x=3. If inclusive capacity excludes 10, substitute the endpoint into y<=10.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare graph scales with space for fractional values. M2-W4-I uses the evidence record to target one missing algebra/coordinate family per learner before final file completion.

**Mathematical practice:** MP8: identify a repeated multiplicative relationship.

### M2-W4-I — Assemble and independently explain the route

*Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Integrate coordinate, expression, equation and relationship reasoning while closing specific evidence gaps.

**Skills taught and checked:** Revisit: coordinate distance, expression meaning, equations and relationships. Practice: completed route file. Check: fresh four-family card. Builds on: W1-W3 evidence.

**Resources and reading:** Existing two-page route file and one fresh four-part annotation card. Read map/rule labels and brief prompts; no added report.

**Teach and model:** Use the opening evidence sort and workshop for one demonstrated need: map distance, expression structure, inverse equation or linked variables. Work on a different example, then withdraw the scaffold for the private annotation.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Individually audit the file for labeled positions, distances, variables, rule and capacity.
2. Complete missing explanations in the existing space.
3. Exchange a focused peer query about one unclear line, then revise.
4. Complete the fresh four-family card; avoid repeating already secure component checks.

**Individual work to collect:** Fresh card: distance (-2,1) to (3,1); translate/evaluate 2(n+3) at n=4 and label its factors; solve x/2.5=4 and substitute; represent y=4x for x=0,1,2. Add only one targeted missing-component recheck per learner during the workshop.

**How the work is judged:** Require 5 units using axis distance, 14 with correct grouping, x=10 verified, and linked 0/4/8 outputs. Preserve prior evidence for all other components; absence of earlier evidence stays open.

**If students struggle:** If a learner copies another route explanation, request a private explanation on the fresh map. Use line counting or tiles as support, then recheck a different point pair/expression. Do not erase the support condition from the record.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare individual need cards from W1-W3 records. M2-W4-G reserves 15 minutes for outstanding components; later M3 retrieval continues support but does not certify omitted M2 teaching.

**Mathematical practice:** MP1: choose a strategy and verify each part of the plan.

### M2-W4-G — Respond to a route closure

*Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Apply a revised map, capacity condition and linked supply rule; defend a route change.

**Skills taught and checked:** Revisit: axis distances, constraints and linked quantities. Practice: changed-condition route. Check: private revision explanation. Builds on: completed route file.

**Resources and reading:** Final map and top-segment closure from the design brief; y=3x and kit capacity 24. Read four points and one closure; route file template.

**Teach and model:** Review the allowed segment rule and the distinction between distance and signed coordinate. Demonstrate how to record a change on an unrelated map; do not reveal the final route.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Teams prepare the final route.
2. Close A-B; teams choose allowed segments, recalculate travel and retain both maps.
3. Give a partner team a three-minute map/constraint defense while teacher samples explanations.
4. Every learner records a private changed segment and supply-rule implication.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect a solo distance calculation, a correctly graphed capacity inequality, and a new table/graph row for the supply rule. Use the 15-minute recheck window for the learner's flagged algebra or signed-number component, on different numbers.

**How the work is judged:** Require a continuous allowed route reaching all sites, accurate distance and a valid capacity argument. A longer feasible route is acceptable; optimization is not a new standard. Keep group route quality and individual evidence separate.

**If students struggle:** If a team uses a diagonal, trace it against the allowed road rule, replace it with perpendicular legs and recheck a fresh pair. If the supply graph is disconnected from the rule, ask the learner to compute one new point independently.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare final map, closure overlay and a feasible answer route. Transfer unresolved signed-number/algebra evidence into M3-W1-G or W3-I as appropriate. Record any components still without individual evidence.

**Mathematical practice:** MP3: defend and revise a mathematical plan.
