# Grade 6 Math – M4 Data Claims Hearing: Lesson Build Guide

Project 4 of 4 · four weeks · eight 90-minute lessons (one individual, then one group, each week).

Built from the [M4 teacher plan](../../../standards/scope-and-sequence/math-projects/M4_DATA_CLAIMS_HEARING.md). Rerun `standards/curriculum-build/project-build-guides/build_math_guides.py` after that plan changes; do not edit this file by hand.

## Project overview

**Essential question:** Which claims are supported by the data, and what would make us change our conclusion?

**What students do:** Investigate a question using a small set of data. Make different displays, decide what the numbers support, and defend a claim that you revise when a value changes.

**Final product:** One evidence brief of about two pages: a statistical question, labeled data, a chosen display, a center/spread explanation and a short claim with limits. Keep the other three required display types as practice attachments. Defend the claim in a small-group hearing and interpret a new dataset independently.

**What good work shows:** Question expects variation; observations, units and displays are accurate; center and range have contextual meanings; chosen measure fits the distribution; claim is bounded by the sample and revised when evidence changes.

## How to run every lesson

**Reading and access:** Use the brief, short problem cards, labeled diagrams and tables listed in each lesson. Cap new prose at about 150-250 words per block, usually less; read the first complex prompt together. These are production limits, not measured reading levels. Explain vocabulary with a model, retain mathematical notation, offer enlarged/tactile grids and oral/text-to-speech access to directions. Keep the assessed reasoning the learner's own. Fade sentence frames and partially labeled diagrams before independent evidence; record continuing accommodations. Step 6 must inspect the actual wording, legibility and screen-reader order.

**Recording evidence:** Use the lesson-specific criteria below. Record each component as secure in this response, developing, or not yet evidenced, with date and support conditions; these descriptive records are not a school grading scale. Practice gets feedback. The final product integrates revised work. Keep group quality separate from each learner's fresh explanations. Sample fluency repeatedly without imposing an invented speed cutoff. Calculator checks follow selected written algorithms; use required accommodations and record conditions.

**Support and extension:** Follow the specific signal/action/recheck in each lesson. Use the budgeted feedback or workshop time; do not add hidden minutes. If the fresh recheck is unsuccessful, carry the exact component and response to the next named support window. Learners ready to extend may compare a second method or construct a counterexample within the same work time; do not add a second major product.

**Outside work:** Optional consolidation, at most two 20-minute practice periods per week (40 minutes/week). The prior plan proposed this duration; it is not a confirmed homework requirement. Introductions, essential project work and required evidence are planned in class. No new instruction or indispensable material access is assigned outside class. Entry-skill gaps may require a calendar decision; homework does not conceal that shortage.

### M4-W1-I — Ask a statistical question and define the data

*Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Distinguish questions that expect variability; define observations, attribute, units and collection conditions.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: statistical question and variability. Practice: brief introduction. Check: new question/nonexample. Builds on: measurement, units and M1 division.

**Resources and reading:** Data Claims Hearing brief, question-sort cards and dataset A label (values revealed next lesson). Read 6 short questions and one data description. Explain observation and variability.

**Teach and model:** Contrast "How long did one specified person take?" with "How do completion times vary across these 12 attempts?" Explain that a repeated-measure question can also be statistical. Model a data record with person/attempt, minutes and conditions; retrieve 936/24=39 as a division algorithm.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Students sort question cards and justify using expected variation, not just plural wording.
2. Write a bounded question for the synthetic dataset.
3. Specify attribute, unit, observation count and what is unknown about collection.
4. Draft the brief's introduction; do not invent a real experiment or make classmates supply sensitive data.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect a new statistical question and explanation, plus a nonexample revision. Require a labeled description of 12 fictional completion times in minutes. Individually interpret 60 minutes/12 observations as 5 minutes per observation and compute 864/24=36 with a standard algorithm.

**How the work is judged:** The question must permit differing numerical responses. Data labels must distinguish synthetic practice from observed school data. Rate/division retrieval supplies component evidence, not a claim about the eventual distribution.

**If students struggle:** If "statistical" means any question with a number, compare one fixed date with times across repeated trials. Recheck "How many pages did each book have?" versus "How many pages in this named copy?" Require a variability explanation.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare cards with one repeated-measure example. M4-W1-G revisits any question/attribute confusion before constructing a dot plot; retain the bounded question.

**Mathematical practice:** MP2: define precisely what one observation measures.

### M4-W1-G — See the distribution in a dot plot

*Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Display every observation and describe center, range and overall shape without claiming more than the data show.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: distribution description and dot plots. Practice: tentative data claim. Check: new plot and range explanation. Builds on: statistical question and number-line scales.

**Resources and reading:** Dataset A (12 values) and dot-plot axis from 0 to 10. Read values and a short description; introduce distribution, cluster and range.

**Teach and model:** Place one dot per observation and stack equal values; verify 12 dots. Preview center near4-5 and range 10-2=8. Describe concentration around3-6 and a longer high end, using the picture rather than treating shape words as a vocabulary test.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Each student tallies and plots all values before team comparison.
2. Teams reconcile missing or duplicated dots.
3. Annotate center, spread and one visible feature; write a tentative claim that names this dataset.
4. Choose one calculation line for a by-hand decimal check.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect each annotated plot and a fresh six-value dot plot for [1,2,2,3,3,7], with count 6, range 6 and a description of the high isolated value. Retrieve 4.75+5.50=10.25 and 10.25-4.75=5.50 using written decimal methods.

**How the work is judged:** Every observation must appear exactly once, scale increments must match, and interpretation must cite a feature. Range measures spread, not the typical value. Assess the individual plot before accepting team consensus.

**If students struggle:** If repeated values are plotted once, pair each data item with a counter then move counters to the axis. Recheck [2,2,2,4] as four dots. If range is called the largest value, mark both endpoints and recheck [3,8] ->5.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare a plot with a deliberately missing duplicate. M4-W2-I opens with dot count/range repair before calculating measures; preserve the plotted A distribution.

**Mathematical practice:** MP6: represent repeated observations faithfully.

### M4-W2-I — Choose and explain measures; build a stem plot

*Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Explain mean, median, mode and range; construct a stem-and-leaf display with a key.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: center versus variation and stem plots. Practice: measure choice. Check: fresh measures/display. Builds on: dot distributions and division fluency.

**Resources and reading:** Dataset A, ordered list and stem template. Read12 values; explain median versus mean through a share model. Key: 0|4 means4 minutes, 1|0 means10 minutes.

**Teach and model:** Share total 60 among12 to find mean 5. Locate middle values4 and 5 -> median 4.5; mode 4 and range 8. Build stems0 and 1 with ordered leaves. Discuss how the high10 influences mean; do not use an IQR outlier rule as a TN requirement.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Independently calculate all measures and annotate their meanings.
2. Make the stem plot and verify the count.
3. Compare mean and median as a typical-time statement.
4. Add a chosen measure and reason to the existing brief; calculator checks follow sampled hand algorithms.

**Individual work to collect:** Fresh set [2,3,3,4,8]: mean 4, median 3, mode 3, range 6; create its stem plot and key. Explain center versus variation. Retrieve 1440/32=45 and 12.6/3=4.2 with standard algorithms.

**How the work is judged:** Require correctly ordered data, a meaningful stem key and contextual meanings. Mean is not simply the middle entry, and range cannot replace a center. Record computation and explanation separately.

**If students struggle:** If median is found before sorting, physically order cards and recheck [7,1,3] ->3. If mean divides by distinct values rather than observations, count all cards; recheck [2,2,8] ->4. If stem key changes units, translate one leaf aloud.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare sharing counters and a correct stem key. M4-W2-G opening retrieval rechecks center/range and ordering before quartiles.

**Mathematical practice:** MP5: choose a calculation or visual representation to verify a measure.

### M4-W2-G — Summarize the same data in a box plot

*Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Use a five-number summary to display distribution and compare what dot/box displays show.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: box plot and five-number representation. Practice: display comparison. Check: new independent box plot. Builds on: ordering and median.

**Resources and reading:** Sorted A data, dot plot and box-plot template. Read12 values and five labels; use the stated median-of-halves quartile convention consistently.

**Teach and model:** Find min2,Q1=3.5,median 4.5,Q3=6,max10; draw a box from 3.5 to 6, median line4.5 and whiskers to 2/10. Explain that plot length represents numeric distance, not number of observations in a section.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Every learner partitions the ordered list and calculates quartiles.
2. Teams create a box plot and compare it with their dot plot.
3. Identify a fact visible in the dot plot but hidden in the box plot (exact repeated values).
4. Revise the display choice in the brief with a reason.

**Individual work to collect:** Fresh ordered set [1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8]: summary1,2.5,4.5,6.5,8. Collect the independent box plot with scale and one comparison of what it does/does not show.

**How the work is judged:** Require correct five-number locations, proportional scale and no invented frequencies. Quartile calculation supports TN box-plot construction; this does not establish CC variability measures.

**If students struggle:** If the whole list is used again to find Q1/Q3, physically separate lower/upper halves and find their medians. Recheck [2,4,6,8] ->Q1=3,median 5,Q3=7. If a long whisker is called more observations, point back to the ordered list.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare a clear even-count example; odd-count convention, if added, must be stated. M4-W3-I rechecks any quartile/scale errors while introducing a different display purpose.

**Mathematical practice:** MP3: explain what a display supports and what it hides.

### M4-W3-I — Group the data and make a pie display

*Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Convert numerical intervals into mutually exclusive groups and explain proportions without losing the original context.

**Skills taught and checked:** Teach: pie display and contextual summary. Revisit: ratios and percents. Practice: evidence brief. Check: fresh grouped proportions. Builds on: M1 ratios and M4 displays/measures.

**Resources and reading:** Dataset A, group table 2-4/5-7/8-10 minutes and supplied circle sectors180/120/60 degrees. Read3 group boundaries plus data; explain frequency and percentage.

**Teach and model:** Count6/4/2 observations; write group:total ratios6:12,4:12,2:12 and percentages50%,33 1/3%,16 2/3%. Match supplied sectors; clarify slices show fractions of observations in time groups, not total minutes spent. Grouping hides individual values.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Individually classify each observation exactly once.
2. Label a pie display using the given sectors and make a title explaining the grouping.
3. Draft a 4-6 sentence contextual summary with count, units, center, range and pattern.
4. Choose which of the four displays best supports the hearing claim; keep the others as evidence attachments.

**Individual work to collect:** Fresh set of12 times grouped as counts3/6/3: give ratios/percentages25/50/25 and label supplied90/180/90 sectors. Find the whole when3 observations are25%. For A, explain why6 observations in the first group is50%, not6%.

**How the work is judged:** Groups must cover all observations with no overlap; percentages total 100 and labels name the measured attribute. Summary must distinguish center and range and relate the claim to these12 observations.

**If students struggle:** If percentages divide by the sum of times, physically count observation cards into groups and recheck2 of8=25%. If a boundary enters two groups, list explicit included values before rechecking a value at the boundary.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare accurate sectors; angle construction is not an added standard. M4-W3-G brings the same summary and chosen display to the correction exercise; first10 minutes retrieves percent/whole errors.

**Mathematical practice:** MP4: explain what a grouped display represents and loses.

### M4-W3-G — Correct the data and revise the claim

*Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Relate a center choice to distribution shape and context; revise a claim after a corrected observation.

**Skills taught and checked:** Revisit: shape, measures and contextual claims. Practice: corrected evidence brief. Check: private before/after explanation. Builds on: all A displays and arithmetic.

**Resources and reading:** Correction card: replace10 minutes by 7. Original and corrected lists, existing displays and brief. Read one change and the original claim; retain version labels.

**Teach and model:** Recalculate total 57/12=4.75, median 4.5, mode 4, range 8-2=6. Show why median stays fixed but mean changes. Compare a typical-time claim using median with an overall average-time claim using mean; neither is automatically always best.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Each learner predicts which measures change.
2. Teams update their selected display and brief, marking the changed observation.
3. Recalculate/interpret center and range and explain the measure choice.
4. Each member writes a private before/after explanation rather than relying on the team speaker.

**Individual work to collect:** Collect an independent corrected summary containing count 12, minutes, mean 4.75, median 4.5, range 6 and a justified center choice. Show 57/12 and 60/12; retrieve 1512/36=42 and 7.25x4=29 with written algorithms.

**How the work is judged:** Require arithmetic consistent with the revised list, a claim that fits the revised evidence, and an explanation of how the high-end change affects mean versus median. No causal conclusion or claim about all students.

**If students struggle:** If the learner changes every measure by 3, compare ordered middle values and sum separately; recheck [2,4,6] changing6 to 9: mean 5, median 4, range 7. If labels still say original, trace each number to its version.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare both lists and a versioned display example. M4-W4-I opening workshop targets measure selection or missing display evidence using recorded work.

**Mathematical practice:** MP3: change a claim when its evidence changes.

### M4-W4-I — Finish the brief and interpret a new distribution

*Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Transfer statistical reasoning to a new dataset; complete missing display evidence without redoing every display.

**Skills taught and checked:** Revisit: question, distribution, measures and summary. Practice: final evidence brief. Check: new B interpretation. Builds on: A investigations and four displays.

**Resources and reading:** Existing brief plus transfer dataset B from the design brief. Read12 new values and one question; no research task or outside data collection.

**Teach and model:** Use the workshop to address an observed need (statistical question, center calculation, range or measure choice) on different data. Show how a brief links a claim to a displayed feature without overstating the sample.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Learners finish the existing two-page brief, checking title, units, count and source label.
2. Select the one display most useful for B and construct it.
3. Independently describe B and justify a center choice.
4. Complete one missing display-type recheck only if earlier individual evidence is absent.

**Individual work to collect:** For B, pose a statistical question; report count 12/minutes; mean 5, median 4, modes2/3/4/5 and range 17; explain the long high end and the effect of18. Justify a typical-value choice. All four TN displays must already have individual evidence or receive a targeted recheck.

**How the work is judged:** Require correct values and distribution-based reasoning. Recognize multiple modes rather than force one. A chosen display must accurately represent B, and the interpretation must name the synthetic sample.

**If students struggle:** If equal means are taken to mean equal distributions, compare A and B ranges/dot patterns. Recheck two sets with mean 3: [2,3,4] and [0,3,6], ranges2 and 6. If a mode is forced, count frequencies explicitly.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare one concise B card and individual missing-evidence flags. M4-W4-G reserves15 minutes for final component rechecks; unresolved needs remain visible in the course record.

**Mathematical practice:** MP1: select and check a representation for unfamiliar data.

### M4-W4-G — Hold the data claims hearing

*Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes*

**Focus and project connection:** Defend a bounded claim, respond to a data correction and explain individual reasoning.

**Skills taught and checked:** Revisit: measure interpretation and contextual justification. Practice: hearing and revision. Check: private correction response. Builds on: final brief and transfer B.

**Resources and reading:** Completed brief and B hearing card; final correction changes18 to 6. Read one challenge and data update; use partner hearings rather than serial whole-class presentations.

**Teach and model:** Review claim/evidence/limit roles with an unrelated tiny dataset. Model a respectful challenge: which values support this measure choice? Clarify that revising a claim is evidence of reasoning, not a penalty.

**Activity steps:** 
1. Teams present their chosen claim and display in paired hearings with rotating speakers.
2. Issue the B correction; teams update center/spread and discuss whether the claim changes.
3. Each student submits a private revision.
4. Teacher uses the final targeted window for outstanding components; keep original and changed versions.

**Individual work to collect:** Private response after18 becomes6: mean 4, median 4, range 6, count 12; explain why the original high-end claim weakens. Include a context-based center choice. Recheck any missing question/display/measure component independently on new values.

**How the work is judged:** Require a calculation-backed revision, accurate units/count and a defensible measure choice. Grade the individual response separately from speaking confidence or group design; an unsupported broad claim needs revision.

**If students struggle:** If a learner repeats the original claim despite the correction, mark the altered endpoint and total, then ask what each measure now says. Recheck [1,3,8] with 8 changed to 5: mean 3, median 3, range 4.

**Prepare and hand off:** Prepare correction and answer key before use. Archive a component-by-component evidence record; identify any unfinished instruction/assessment for school scheduling rather than assert full mastery.

**Mathematical practice:** MP3: defend an interpretation and respond to a numerical challenge.
