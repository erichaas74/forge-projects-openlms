# Forge grades 4–6: project-map alignment review

The supplied projects provide useful formats, but the map does not yet establish complete coverage of the supplied Forge framework. The largest content changes are in grades 4–5 social studies and science. Math generally needs more explicit skill checkpoints. ELA needs named texts, grade-specific reading/writing requirements, and individual assessment evidence.

The accompanying workbook reviews **52 projects against 271 unique Forge standards**. It identifies **51 direct planned matches, 148 partial matches, and 72 gaps**. These are judgments about the written map, not evidence that students have mastered standards or that a course has been approved.

## Scope and method

Sources are the four DOCX files in [4-8_Possible_project_map](./4-8_Possible_project_map/) and the supplied CSV exports in [Forge_School_Master_Standards_Frameworks](./Forge_School_Master_Standards_Frameworks/). Only grade 4, 5 and 6 projects were rated. The separate, more detailed project plans referenced by the maps were not present in that map folder and were not assumed to contain missing requirements.

The four subject CSVs contain 270 standards. **FF.G6.MATH.29 — Summarize numerical data in context** also appears in the Grade 6 Framework, Progression & Acceleration, and Source Crosswalk exports, but is missing from Math 4-6. It is included in this review, giving 271 standards. The source files were not changed. All other grade-level framework rows match the subject exports.

The workbook preserves original Forge identifiers, standard statements, descriptions, external codes, suggested evidence, prerequisites, and review status. Its new project-alignment ratings are separate from the framework's existing Tennessee/national alignment labels. Those external crosswalk claims were not independently revalidated. The supplied framework is marked draft for Head of School review.

| Rating | Meaning | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | The map names the core target skill/content and a matching action or product. | Use as a planned anchor; still specify the complete rubric and individual proof. |
| Partial | A related topic or skill appears, but a required case, subskill, action or assessment detail is missing. | Adapt the project and add the missing teaching/evidence before claiming coverage. |
| Gap | No explicit grade-level target is documented. | Add or retheme required work. A suggested destination is not current alignment. |

A shared product can support several standards, but each must have its own evidence criteria. A project in a later grade does not fill an earlier-grade gap. Reading in a science project does not automatically complete an ELA standard, and a robot theme does not automatically assess forces.

## What should change

### ELA: retain the themes and specify the literacy sequence

All twelve titles can remain potential literacy contexts. The strongest explicit match is the shared draft–feedback–revision–final-product process: **FF.G4.ELA.21, FF.G5.ELA.21 and FF.G6.ELA.21**. Most other ELA matches remain partial because the map provides a common structure rather than unit-level texts and assessment tasks.

Add a text and evidence plan for each project: independent reading, literary analysis, informational analysis, the appropriate writing genre, language conventions, vocabulary, discussion and presentation. Require literary analysis from reading even when students also write an original story. Multimedia production needs an additional comparison task to assess **ELA.11**. Grade 4–5 oral fluency and all three grades' figurative-language/word-relationship expectations need explicit tasks.

Use a portfolio to revisit broad standards such as reading across genres and routine writing across purposes. Do not give one completion checkmark for an entire bundled reading standard after a single short response.

The ELA source currently describes **three weeks**, so the four-week design is an adaptation. Its grade 6 identity paragraph is interrupted by grade 7 checkpoint text; those grade 7 details were not credited as grade 6 requirements.

### Math: preserve the four projects and fill precise skill gaps

| Grade | Existing anchors | Required additions or clarifications |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Explorer Route, Energy Arcade, Fraction Trade, Hazard City Geometry | Add fraction-by-whole-number multiplication (**FF.G4.MATH.15**) and additive unknown-angle reasoning (**.25**). Specify number ranges, comparison models, fractional line plots, unit relationships and protractor tasks. |
| 5 | Expedition Budget, 3D Race Car, Robot Delivery, Mars BioDome | Add numerical expressions (**FF.G5.MATH.01–02**), powers of ten (**.05**), decimal rounding (**.07**), whole-number multiplication/division requirements (**.08–09**) and shape hierarchies (**.25**). Replace the broad label “fractions” with assessed operations and models (**.11–17**). |
| 6 | Mixture Lab, Atomic Motion, Temple Vault, Empire Power Index | Add whole-number division fluency (**FF.G6.MATH.05**), GCF/LCM/distributive reasoning (**.07**), exponents (**.12**), equivalent expressions (**.14–15**) and statistical questions (**.25**). Specify four-quadrant/absolute-value tasks, fractional prism dimensions, required data displays and contextual summaries (**.29**). |

These are additions to the curriculum, not merely extra labels on the final product. For example, a BioDome graph does not demonstrate fraction division unless the student actually models, calculates and explains that operation.

### Science: use the supplied framework to reset the grade sequence

The science map follows a different content sequence from the supplied Forge framework. Its broad NGSS connection labels do not establish alignment to every Forge/Tennessee target.

- **Grade 4:** Energy Arena, Wave Rescue, Fossil Detectives and Hazard City have useful anchors. Creature Feature emphasizes organism structures and senses, while the framework requires food webs, ecosystem roles, species changes and environmental effects (**FF.G4.SCI.07–10**). Expand the project to teach these targets. Add organism effects on landscapes and resource/human-impact work (**.16–18**), plus missing wave/light details.
- **Grade 5:** The map lists titles and refers to another plan; it does not provide the detailed science cards. Zero direct matches means insufficient documented detail, not that no science learning is possible. Add explicit conservation of matter, force investigations, animal responses, inheritance and variation (**FF.G5.SCI.02, .04, .06–11**). Specify each sky-system target in Cosmic Architects. The race-car math project offers partial science anchors for motion data, prototype testing and measurement tools, but needs separate science criteria.
- **Grade 6:** Weather and Climate Command Center and Natural Resources and Earth Data Challenge provide the closest current anchors. Restore ecosystem populations/interactions/energy/health, Tennessee invasive species and biodiversity (**FF.G6.SCI.03–08**), water cycle (**.12**), greenhouse effect (**.14**), biodiversity design (**.19**) and thermal-device engineering (**.20**). Matter and Particles can be adapted toward thermal/energy work. Earth History and Plate Tectonics currently have no explicit match to the supplied grade 6 targets; consider repurposing them or treating them as enrichment after required content is secured.

The map's preference against repeating science topics in adjacent years should be reconsidered wherever the framework expects a progression in the same domain. Revisit a domain at the required level of complexity rather than removing it from the assigned grade.

### Social studies: change historical content while reusing project formats

**Grade 4 needs a substantial retheme.** The exploration, encounters and New World exchange projects support inquiry and geography, but they do not cover the framework's twelve historical content standards (**FF.G4.SS.07–18**): sectional conflict, Civil War, Reconstruction, industrialization/reform, the world wars, Depression/New Deal, postwar change and civil rights. Reuse route maps, case files, exhibits and debate formats with the required historical content. A generic state-origin exhibit cannot stand in for this sequence.

**Grade 5 needs Tennessee-specific history and fuller geography.** Mystery Native Community can become a Tennessee evidence investigation (**FF.G5.SS.07**); Westward Trail can use Tennessee migration routes and frontier cases (**.08**). Add required statehood, leaders, Civil War/Reconstruction, twentieth-century history, civil rights/culture, economy/government and physical regions (**.09–16, .20**). Explicitly teach global geography (**.21**). The colonial/Revolution projects can be rethemed or retained as supporting contexts only after required coverage is scheduled.

**Grade 6 can retain its four formats with mandatory civilization cases.** The Civilization Comparison Tracker is useful, but students cannot choose a single civilization and thereby complete every civilization-specific standard. Assign required cases across Mesopotamia, Egypt/Nubia, Israel, India, China, Greece and Rome. Hammurabi alone only partially covers **FF.G6.SS.10**, a bundled standard containing government, religion, writing, architecture and literature as well as law. Add early-human chronology/Neolithic transition (**.07**), ancient Israel (**.13**) and Roman division/fall/Byzantine continuation (**.22**). Cover the remaining named cases through the map, exhibit, invention and government tasks.

## How this changes the four-week / eight-lesson plan

Develop each project backward from its assigned standards: **required content → observable individual evidence → teaching and practice → project checkpoint → progress rule**. Resolve the 72 gaps in the course map before describing the annual course as fully aligned. The eight lessons are one project's flow; they are not a plan to complete every annual standard in four weeks.

| Week | Instructional / individual session | Group session | Evidence and progress checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Teacher launches the problem and models the first required skill. AI gives each student a brief diagnostic tied to exact Forge IDs and identifies misconceptions. | Teams establish the plan. AI asks each student to explain an initial source, calculation or model; the teacher checks participation and understanding. | Individual baseline plus team plan. Require a specific prerequisite before a dependent task when the curriculum calls for it. |
| 2 | Teacher teaches the next target and re-teaches gaps. AI provides a short independent check after supported practice. | Teams build a draft/model. AI checks each student's reasoning about their contribution; teacher reviews the artifact and unresolved misconceptions. | Individual application plus a draft. An unmet prerequisite triggers targeted support and a new check before the related task unlocks. |
| 3 | Teacher connects concepts and models stronger evidence. AI tests a fresh example or transfer question. | Teams test, compare and revise. AI asks students to explain discrepancies and decisions; teacher verifies the quality of evidence. | Test/source-analysis record, revision and independent explanation. Review readiness before final synthesis. |
| 4 | Teacher addresses remaining required targets. AI administers a final individual check and compiles evidence against rubric components. | Teams present the final project. Each student defends a contribution or reasoning choice; teacher reviews the portfolio and final evidence. | Final product plus individual defense. Teacher confirms standards demonstrated, records remaining needs, and assigns reassessment where needed. |

For example, in **Mixture Lab**, a prerequisite gate could require an independently explained ratio and unit rate (**FF.G6.MATH.01–02**) before a student uses the scaling task for **.03**. The teacher defines the passing criteria and equivalent ways to demonstrate readiness. Passing that stage gate does not automatically complete every component of the broader standard.

Use a small set of observable targets in each session, with one useful artifact/contribution and a brief individual check. The workbook's linked-standard counts are **not a workload estimate**: one artifact may serve several targets, and one bundled standard may require several lessons. Determine pacing from the actual text, task and prerequisite demands; pilot it before setting an average completion quota.

The AI tutor's record should connect **student → Forge ID → rubric component → response/artifact → feedback → retry → teacher decision**. Distinguish “introduced,” “practicing,” “evidence submitted,” “AI recommends ready,” and “teacher confirmed.” A team submission, successful activity, chat length or AI-generated answer is insufficient for an individual standard checkmark.

For a student needing more time, diagnose the specific missing skill, offer a short model or scaffold, provide guided practice, then use a fresh independent check. Keep instruction and nondependent team participation available. An unmet gate should hold the dependent action, not block access to help or all group work.

For an advanced student, use side quests that deepen the same target—an added constraint, a counterexample, a comparison or a transfer situation. Secure required core evidence first. The supplied progression export keeps English sequential and allows other-subject acceleration through its stated readiness and approval process; an AI side quest is not placement approval.

The supplied NSQ alignment sheet concerns course design evidence, not additional student content standards or completed implementation. This crosswalk supports objective/activity/assessment planning, but individual checkmarks cannot demonstrate all NSQ institutional requirements.

## Recommended order of work

1. Reconcile the missing **FF.G6.MATH.29** in the maintained framework and review the draft source wording before final curriculum approval.
2. Retheme grades 4–5 social studies and restore the required science sequence.
3. Assign complete standard components, named texts/cases, and evidence to each project; use the workbook's row-level proposed actions.
4. Build the four-week lesson checkpoints and prerequisite rules from that revised map, then update the presentation with exact Forge IDs.

The original maps, standards exports, application and previously created presentation remain available as supplied; this review adds an alignment workbook and planning memo.
