# Step 1 — understand the grade's standards and learning connections

Rebuilt September 20, 2026. This step analyzes learning across all subjects in the grade. [Step 2](STEP_2_PROJECT_BACKWARD_DESIGN.md) then designs the coordinated four-project sequence for each subject.

## Purpose and boundary

Answer: **What must every student understand and be able to do, what learning depends on what, and which standards connect meaningfully across subjects?**

Keep the whole grade in view. Do not choose projects, project types, lessons, mini-module counts, screens or software here. The application build plan does not determine curriculum design.

## Inputs

- Grade, all participating subjects, learner prerequisites and known access/support needs.
- Complete supplied or verified standards for each subject, with exact wording, source and revision.
- Actual instructional calendar, subject time and existing commitments; distinguish confirmed constraints from proposals.
- Earlier/later grade expectations where supplied, and relevant existing planning records.

Preserve source wording separately from an author's interpretation. A supplied crosswalk identifier is not verified official wording. Record missing subjects, incomplete sources and uncertainty; do not invent standards or certify complete coverage from partial inputs.

## Work across all subjects

1. **Inventory every required standard.** Keep stable IDs and source references. Decompose bundled standards into assessable components without losing required content or changing their depth.
2. **Describe the learning.** For each component, state what students must understand, the action/reasoning they must perform, and what individual evidence could demonstrate it. Identify prerequisites, misconceptions, support needs and repeated practice needs.
3. **Map progression.** Identify learning dependencies within and across subjects, sensible concept groupings, earlier knowledge and skills needing sustained recurrence. Group by learning relationships; do not force four equal piles yet.
4. **Find meaningful cross-subject connections.** Name the exact components on both sides, how using one helps students think or act in the other, a plausible student use, evidence possibilities and timing dependencies. Generic labels such as "uses math" or "includes writing" are insufficient.
5. **Separate academic requirements from thematic fit.** Every required component remains required. Mark content that may need separate teaching; it does not need an artificial story connection. Connections are candidates for Step 2, not claims of coverage.
6. **Review the complete grade map.** Reconcile all supplied subjects and components, check prerequisite order and source gaps, and identify likely workload pressure before project selection.

## One standards-and-learning map

Use one linked grade planning packet. It may have subject views, but they share stable component IDs and one revision.

| Record | Required fields |
| --- | --- |
| Scope and sources | Grade, participating subjects, source/version, supplied or independently verified status, calendar constraints, missing inputs |
| Learning component | Standard/component ID, exact source wording, interpretation, knowledge/action/depth, prerequisites, recurrence/support needs, possible individual evidence |
| Cross-subject connection | Exact linked components and subjects, meaningful student use, reason the connection improves learning, possible evidence, prerequisite/timing constraints |

Core/supporting/recurring describes the learning's role; it never makes a required component optional. A team result, graph, essay or completion badge does not establish every standard associated with it.

## Exit check

- Every supplied required standard/component is accounted for, with source wording and interpretation distinguishable.
- Expected student understanding and reasoning are clear.
- Prerequisites and recurrence are explicit across the grade.
- Cross-subject connections identify exact learning, purposeful use and timing dependencies.
- Missing sources, subjects or calendar information are visible; affected planning is provisional.
- No project assignment, template, module structure or software choice has been silently treated as settled.

Hand the complete map to [Step 2](STEP_2_PROJECT_BACKWARD_DESIGN.md). Preserve useful existing inventories as inputs; a previous coverage review does not prove that the new grade-wide planning review has occurred.
