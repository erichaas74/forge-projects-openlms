## Standards walk and four-cluster maps

Standards were grouped by conceptual dependence and evidence demands, rather than divided into equal sets. Project numbers indicate a learning sequence within a subject. They do not imply that ancient civilizations occurred one after another, or that every subject must run its same-numbered project simultaneously.

### Mathematics

The progression is **quantitative relationships → symbolic and spatial models → geometric design → evaluation of data claims**. Number fluency supports ratio reasoning and later formulas. Factoring precedes equivalent expressions. Signed number order precedes coordinate distance and inequalities. Coordinates and expressions then support geometry. The final project makes variability and statistical reasoning explicit.

| Cluster | Primary-home standards | Recurring standards from earlier projects | Prerequisite sequence and major skills | Context and demand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | MATH.01–07, .12 | Grade 5 operations and measurement, retaught as needed | Fraction/decimal operations support ratios and unit rates; build ratio tables, percent/conversion models, factor groupings, and numerical powers | Supply lots, rates, packaging batches. Separate computation from success in trading. |
| M2 | MATH.08–11, .13–20 | MATH.01–07, .12 | Signed quantities → order/absolute value and coordinates; variable quantities → expressions → equivalence/substitution → one-step equations and inequalities → tables/graphs/equations | Heaviest mathematics cluster: 12 home standards. Requires more teaching time than the geometry cluster. |
| M3 | MATH.21–24 | MATH.03–04, .06, .09–15, .17–18, .20 | Area decomposition precedes nets/surface area; coordinate distance precedes polygon sides; fraction operations precede fractional-edge prism volume | Container footprint, capacity, and material are separate constraints. All four geometry targets are required. |
| M4 | MATH.25–29 | MATH.01–03, .05–06 | Statistical versus nonstatistical questions → variable data → displays → center/variation/shape → justified contextual summary | Data hearings require distribution reasoning, not only attractive graphs. Include MATH.29 explicitly. |

M1 teaches whole-number powers in a separate SHOEHORN number-structure block; nested packing is no longer required as a thematic justification. M2 must distinguish expressions from equations and inequalities. It cannot award expression equivalence merely because two expressions agree at one value. M3 requires fractional edge lengths and both rectangle and triangle nets. M4 preserves the supplied dot, box, pie, and stem-plot requirements pending external wording verification.

### English Language Arts

The progression is **interpret literature → transform narrative meaning → explain from research → construct and evaluate arguments**. Reading analysis and original writing are separate evidence responsibilities. Literary and informational reading continue through the year.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | ELA.01–02, .04, .07–10, .15–16, .21, .23 | Grade 5 reading, discussion, and writing processes | Locate evidence before inferring; trace events before analyzing theme and viewpoint; establish grammar, editing, discussion, and independent-reading routines | Five focus standards plus six annual routine owners. Home assignment does not compress a year of reading/writing into E1. |
| E2 | ELA.03, .05, .11, .20 | ELA.01–02, .04, .07–10, .15–16, .21, .23 | Literary interpretation supports narrative choices; compare reading with a live/audio representation and compare different forms/genres | Both an original narrative and analysis of a text by another author are required. |
| E3 | ELA.06, .12–13, .19, .22 | ELA.01–05, .15–16, .21, .23 | Informational evidence/central idea → structure/purpose → source evaluation and research → explanatory organization | Topic knowledge strengthens research but does not replace assessed literacy. |
| E4 | ELA.14, .17–18 | ELA.01–08, .10–13, .15–16, .19, .21–23 | Source comparison and research precede argument; students analyze a speaker as well as deliver their own claims | Argument, listening, and independent reading each require evidence. |

ELA.15, .16, .21, and .23 use evidence across all four projects. Grammar and vocabulary receive repeated instruction and application. Titles and exact editions of reading texts, complexity/access supports, permissions, and comparison performances remain to be selected before lesson design. No invented reading list is being credited as coverage.

### Science

The progression is **energy in a device → energy and interactions in ecosystems → coupled Earth systems → resource and biodiversity decisions**. Thermal transfer supports convection and climate. Population interactions and ecosystem health support later conservation design.

| Cluster | Primary-home standards | Recurring standards from earlier projects | Prerequisite sequence and major skills | Context and demand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | SCI.01–02, .20 | Grade 5 forces, measurement, controlled testing | Track energy forms and conservation; model conduction, convection, and radiation; combine device features and test | Thermal-container prototype. A physical build is needed for “construct and test”; a digital sketch alone is insufficient. |
| S2 | SCI.03–08 | SCI.01 | Resource variables and interaction patterns → food-web/energy models → terrestrial and aquatic health → Tennessee invasive-species response and biodiversity services | Required ecological evidence cases; a generic animal-identification game would not meet the scope. |
| S3 | SCI.09–15 | SCI.01–02 | Uneven heating → convection → solar/salinity circulation → regional climate; solar energy/gravity → water cycle and impacts; then greenhouse model and air-mass prediction | Seven system standards need distinct demonstrations. Weather prediction alone does not cover the whole cluster. |
| S4 | SCI.16–19 | SCI.01–08, .11–14, .20 | Ecosystem and Earth-system evidence support sustainability comparisons, energy technology choices, biosphere impacts, and iterative biodiversity design | Engineering must include evaluation and improvement, not only selecting a conservation slogan. |

SCI.02’s supplied description says “sound or thermal” while naming thermal-transfer mechanisms. S1 uses the thermal route. Verify the official wording before claiming a sound requirement or treating conduction, convection, and radiation as sound mechanisms. Crosswalked middle-school NGSS codes do not automatically become a separate complete Grade 6 NGSS curriculum.

### Social Studies

The progression is **reconstruct from evidence → compare geography and society → trace institutions and diffusion → explain complex historical change**. All required societies remain in scope. Learner choice can change an extension or artifact, not remove a required civilization.

| Cluster | Primary-home standards | Recurring standards from earlier projects | Prerequisite sequence and major skills | Context and demand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | SS.01–10 | Grade 5 inquiry and geography | Inquiry/source critique supports chronology, Paleolithic/Neolithic comparison, civilization characteristics, and Mesopotamian geography/institutions | Four content standards plus six recurring inquiry owners. Hammurabi is one part of SS.10. |
| H2 | SS.11–15 | SS.01–10 | Mesopotamian river systems and institutions support Egypt/Nubia; contextualize Israelite movement and Judaism; compare Indian geography, cities, migration, caste, beliefs, and achievements | Five bundled standards across multiple societies. Protect teaching time for each required case. |
| H3 | SS.16–19 | SS.01–06, .08, .10, .12–15 | Prior geography/governance work supports Chinese dynasties/philosophies and Greek city-states; trace Han/Silk Road and Hellenistic diffusion in their own chronologies | Mandatory China and Greece cases. A route or collection of discoveries alone is insufficient. |
| H4 | SS.20–22 | SS.01–06, .08, .10, .13, .16–19 | Greek and earlier institutional comparison → Roman republic → empire/religious change → division, western fall, Byzantine continuation | Three very broad standards. Low row count is not low workload. |

SS.01–06 require increasingly independent evidence in every project. Religious traditions are studied historically and respectfully; distinguish historical accounts, beliefs, and interpretations. A speculative council decision must be labeled as a counterfactual, with actual outcomes checked against sources.
