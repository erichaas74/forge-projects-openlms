## Review method and result

The review compares each supplied Forge description and its individual-evidence direction against the selected concept, including bundled components. It reviews all **94 home assignments and 115 recurrence links**. Cross-subject opportunities remain supporting practice and do not substitute for primary-subject coverage. The labels are curriculum judgments about the proposed concepts, not a runtime audit or proof of mastery.

| Label | Meaning | Step 2 obligation |
|---|---|---|
| NATURAL | The assigned learning fits the core work. | Still teach it explicitly and collect individual evidence; a theme alone proves nothing. |
| SHOEHORN | The assigned learning needs separate teaching. | Schedule instruction, practice, independent evidence, fresh transfer, and reassessment. No project application required. |
| NATURAL + SHOEHORN | Some components fit; other required components need separate teaching. | Preserve both parts and identify which evidence demonstrates each. |

Fit is independent of source priority and whether a link is a home or recurrence. A recurring link can cover selected components without repeating the complete standard. The home remains responsible for the full supplied description. Labels do not change the source text or reduce required content.

## How Step 2 must use this handoff

1. Retain the project owner for every standard. Copy the home and recurrence rows, fit labels, and precise SHOEHORN components into that project’s backward-design plan.
2. Design the final around its natural core. Give separate instruction and individual evidence equal coverage standing. A SHOEHORN row may explicitly say “no project application required.”
3. Put each named teaching block into the actual lesson calendar with minutes. Placement windows below use Step 2’s L1–L8 language provisionally; they are not completed lesson plans or evidence that all teaching fits the confirmed four-week schedule.
4. For every block, plan teacher introduction/modeling, guided practice, a later practice encounter, independent evidence, a fresh transfer task, and a reserved reassessment slot after targeted reteaching. Whole-class required content is not delegated only to one expert role or saved solely for remediation.
5. Use fresh individual evidence. A team product, a calculator/interface result, source copying, or a heavily scaffolded response does not demonstrate independent mastery. Keep component-level unmet needs visible.
6. Protect ongoing ELA reading/language/writing and Social Studies inquiry. Recurrence rows specify scope; do not treat a brief retrieval task as full re-certification of a bundled standard.
7. Respect the confirmed four-week, eight-block calendar and budget out-of-class work explicitly. M2, S3, H2, and H3 require particular load review. Record unresolved time/support needs rather than silently adding class blocks or dropping standards. Do not hide all additions in L7/L8 or double-count simultaneous project and teaching minutes.
8. Develop one detailed Step 2 project map at a time, review it with the user, then move to the next. M1, E3 and S3 were drafted previously; H2 is now the current review. All four selected architecture drafts exist; review H2 before starting Step 3 or another project.

Main-panel activity rules remain in force. Separate teaching may occur in a teacher-led subject lesson; writing/question answering in the LMS belongs in the tutor-side area. The current tutor remains a disconnected planning list. No software assessment, gated testing access, new video, or implemented activity is implied.
