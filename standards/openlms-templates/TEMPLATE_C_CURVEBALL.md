# Template C — Curveball

**Use for:** the group lesson where conditions change and teams must revise. In math: **W3-G** of every project (M1 stock change, M2 route closure, M3 larger capacity, M4 changed data value).

**What students experience:** a story event breaks their plan, they spot the mistake in a tempting quick fix, the team revises the product, and each student privately justifies the revision.

Shared settings are in the [README](README.md#part-3--settings-used-by-every-template).

### Branching Scenario tips
- Write the event in the project's story voice ("Supplier A calls: only 2 gold packs left!").
- Make one choice the tempting shortcut from *Teach and model* (for example "just buy the cheapest clips"), and show why it fails.
- Keep it under 3 minutes. Grading stays in items 5 and 6.
- **Alternative:** the Moodle **Lesson** activity does the same branching with reliable gradebook recording, but looks plainer. Use it if Branching Scenario scoring causes problems.

## Worked example: M1-W3-G — Respond to a stock change

| # | Item | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ① The curveball | "Supplier A has only 2 gold packs left." Choices: *Buy 3 A packs anyway* → "Not possible: only 2 in stock." *Switch everything to B* → "Works: 4 B packs = 60 clips for $11.40." *Mix suppliers* → "Works: 2 A + 2 B = 66 clips for $12.90." End: "Which feasible order is better for your kits?" |
| 2 | ② Spot the mistake | Image of a plan using 3 A gold packs at $10.80 — hotspots on the unavailable packs, the cheapest-per-clip claim and a missing leftover check. |
| 3 | ③ Revise the plan | 1. Mark the failed stock constraint. 2. Calculate at least two feasible replacements and update totals and leftovers. 3. Rotate explanation roles; challenge an unsupported claim. 4. Each student annotates a changed calculation, a factored packing sum and a power expression. |
| 4 | ③ Before and after | Original and revised ledgers. Checklist: within stock; enough clips; totals correct. |
| 5 | ④ My justification | "Explain which revised order your team chose and prove it meets the stock limit." Marking guide: feasible stock quantities; accurate written algorithms; factored sum or power explained with multiplication. |
| 6 | ④ Quick audit | 2.85 × 4 = 11.40; 11.40 ÷ 15 = 0.76; 7.20 + 11.40 + 3.00 = 21.60; 24.00 − 21.60 = 2.40; GCF(36, 54) = 18; LCM(6, 9) = 18; 2 + 2³ = 10. Calculated variants for each. Link 6.NS.B.3, 6.NS.B.4, 6.EE.A.1. |
| 7 | If you're stuck | "Cover the price and check quantity and stock first." Exponent: expand only the base. Recheck: an offer with stock 2 but need 3; 4 + 3² = 13. |
| 8 | ⑤ What changed? | "What did the stock change teach you about choosing the cheapest option?" |
