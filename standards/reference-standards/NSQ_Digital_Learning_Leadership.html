Introduction

Purpose of the Standards

The National Standards for Quality Digital Learning Leadership provide a comprehensive, research-informed framework for the knowledge, skills, and dispositions required to lead effective, learner-centered digital learning programs. The standards are designed to guide leaders in creating and sustaining digital learning environments that are innovative and responsive, ensuring that all learners have access to high-quality educational opportunities. The standards are intended for use by school and district leaders, policy-makers, professional organizations, school leader preparation programs, and others seeking to improve leadership capacity in digital education. They serve both as a tool for policy and strategic guidance and as a resource for practical implementation and evaluation.

Development Process

The Digital Learning Leadership Standards are a new addition to the National Standards for Quality collection, designed to strengthen the overall framework by defining the knowledge, skills, and dispositions leaders need to ensure the quality of online teaching, courses, and programs. The development process began with a comprehensive literature review of research on digital leadership, followed by interviews and focus groups with educators, researchers, and leaders across multiple states and program types to identify priorities and essential practices. A volunteer committee of subject-matter experts contributed through working groups and structured feedback processes ensuring the standards reflect both research-based practices and the realities of leading in different digital contexts.

Guiding Principles

The following guiding principles reflect the core beliefs and values that underpin the Digital Leadership Standards, shaping both their intent and practical application across varied digital learning contexts.
  • Learner-Centered Leadership – Every decision, policy, and strategy should support the success and well-being of all learners.
  • Innovation with Purpose – Digital tools and strategies should be intentionally selected and applied to advance learning goals and improve outcomes, ensuring that technology serves as a means to meaningful educational impact.
  • Sustainability and Adaptability – Leadership practices should be flexible and responsive to changing learner needs, technologies, and contexts.
  • Collaboration and Shared Responsibility – Effective digital leadership engages stakeholders across the learning community, including educators, learners, families, and community partners.
  • Evidence Informed Practice – Decisions should be based on reliable data, research, and evaluation of outcomes.

How to Use the Standards

The standards can serve multiple purposes and can be used in a variety of ways, including:
  • An evaluation framework – to inform leadership and performance reviews, self-assessments, and accreditation processes for leaders of digital learning programs.
  • A professional development resource – to promote leadership competencies through professional learning, coaching, and mentorship.
  • A guide for school leadership preparation programs -to shape coursework, field experiences, and assessment of leadership candidates in relation to digital learning.
  • A policy reference – to guide policymakers in establishing leader preparation requirements and policies that support high-quality digital learning programs.

Organization of the Standards

The Digital Learning Leadership Standards are organized into four dimensions, each representing a critical area of responsibility for leaders in K-12 digital learning environments. While the dimensions are distinct, they are interrelated and work together to support high-quality, learner-centered digital programs.

Dimension 1: Visionary Leadership and Ethical Stewardship

Digital learning leaders develop and implement strategic vision while upholding ethical standards in virtual and hybrid environments. They build trust through transparent decision-making, foster innovation in digital learning practices, and guide their organizations through the unique challenges of technology-mediated education. These leaders ensure that mission-driven approaches create sustainable, learner-centered digital learning environments.

STANDARD A: STRATEGIC VISION AND SYSTEM ALIGNMENT

Digital learning leaders collaboratively develop and steward a shared mission, vision, and core values that reflect the unique priorities of digital learning environments. These guiding principles anchor strategic planning, align resources, and shape decision-making. Digital learning leaders ensure that goals are learner-responsive, future-focused, and grounded in community input, enabling continuous improvement and system-wide coherence.

A1: Digital learning leaders collaboratively develop, review, and revise mission, vision, and core values using community-building digital engagement strategies.

EXPLANATION

Visionary and ethical digital learning leaders ensure the mission, vision, and core values are living statements that guide fair and equal opportunity in digital learning environments. Through welcoming and transparent processes, they engage multiple stakeholder voices using digital collaboration tools that promote participation across geographic, cultural, and program boundaries. Regular review and revision ensure these foundational statements remain responsive to emerging needs, technologies, and community priorities. This ongoing, participatory approach strengthens trust, shared ownership, and alignment between organizational purpose and practice.

EXAMPLES

A consortium of online schools uses virtual town halls, multilingual surveys, and collaborative whiteboards to gather input from teachers, students, and families when revising its mission and core values.

A state virtual program director forms an advisory council representing educators, families, and business partners who meet through video conferences to review the vision statement annually and recommend updates.

A hybrid school leader conducts an asynchronous discussion forum for staff and learners to reflect on how the mission aligns with new accessibility and technology goals, integrating feedback into a revised vision document.

EVIDENCE

Stakeholder engagement records, such as survey data, meeting minutes, focus group summaries, or online discussion transcripts, demonstrate broad participation across the organization.

The organization has updated its mission, vision, and values statements, with documented timelines showing when revisions occurred.

Digital engagement artifacts—including collaboration boards, multilingual communications, and virtual event materials—show active participation by a diverse group of leaders.

Reports and communications demonstrate how the revised mission and vision statements now guide program priorities, practices, and performance metrics.

A2: Digital learning leaders translate the shared mission and vision into strategic plans with measurable goals and aligned resources.

EXPLANATION

Effective digital learning leadership requires translating the organization’s mission and vision into coherent strategic plans that guide decision-making and resource allocation. Digital learning leaders establish measurable goals aligned to core values and ensure fiscal, human, and technological resources are intentionally directed to support fair and sustainable digital learning. These plans connect long-term vision to operational priorities while promoting transparency, accountability, and responsible stewardship focused on learner success.

EXAMPLES

A district digital learning director develops a multi-year strategic plan that aligns funding priorities, instructional technology investments, and staffing models with the district’s mission to expand access to digital learning for all learners.

A charter network’s digital learning leader creates a strategic framework linking the school’s vision for personalized learning to measurable goals for student engagement, platform adoption, and instructional quality.

A state virtual program leader aligns budget priorities, staffing decisions, and technology investments to strategic goals focused on learner access, instructional consistency, and program sustainability.

EVIDENCE

Strategic and improvement plan documents demonstrate clear alignment between the organization’s mission, vision, measurable goals, and core values.

Documentation shows that fiscal, human, and technological resources are aligned with the organization’s strategic priorities, as reflected in budgets, staffing plans, and technology plans.

Records of stakeholder engagement—including meeting agendas, planning committee rosters, and feedback summaries—illustrate active participation in the strategic planning process.

Goal-setting frameworks and performance indicators are linked to strategic priorities but do not include implementation-level detail.

Communication materials shared with internal and external stakeholders clearly outline the organization’s strategic goals and priorities.

A3: Digital learning leaders establish clear implementation structures, timelines, and indicators to operationalize strategic plans.

EXPLANATION

Strategic plans in digital learning environments require clear implementation structures to move from intent to action. Digital learning leaders define timelines, milestones, roles, and implementation indicators that support the execution of strategic priorities across virtual and hybrid contexts. By establishing responsibility, sequencing actions, and monitoring readiness for implementation, leaders ensure strategic initiatives are actionable, scalable, and positioned for effective oversight and communication.

EXAMPLES

A virtual academy director develops an implementation roadmap for a three-year strategic plan, outlining timelines, responsible teams, and key indicators for expanding career and technical education offerings.

A hybrid program principal assigns ownership and milestones for family engagement initiatives, using implementation indicators such as participation benchmarks and platform adoption metrics to guide execution.

A supplemental online program manager establishes phased implementation plans for bilingual course expansion, identifying launch timelines, staffing responsibilities, and readiness indicators tied to enrollment and instructional capacity.

EVIDENCE

Strategic and improvement plan documents demonstrate clear alignment between the organization’s mission, vision, measurable goals, and core values.

Documentation shows that fiscal, human, and technological resources are aligned with the organization’s strategic priorities, as reflected in budgets, staffing plans, and technology plans.

Records of stakeholder engagement—including meeting agendas, planning committee rosters, and feedback summaries—illustrate active participation in the strategic planning process.

Goal-setting frameworks and performance indicators are linked to strategic priorities but do not include implementation-level detail.

Communication materials shared with internal and external stakeholders clearly outline the organization’s strategic goals and priorities.

A4: Digital learning leaders communicate and monitor progress toward goals using transparent, data-informed processes.

EXPLANATION

Transparent communication of progress toward strategic goals builds trust and facilitates collaborative oversight of program direction and impact. Digital learning environments require leaders to leverage technology tools for communication while ensuring accessibility for all stakeholders. Effective communication promotes transparency, stakeholder engagement, and shared accountability by making progress visible and understandable to different audiences. Digital learning leaders should implement communication methods that reach geographically dispersed stakeholders and accommodate different technology access levels and preferences.

EXAMPLES

The principal of a full-time virtual school uses a public dashboard accessible through the school website and sends monthly email updates with visual progress reports to highlight key metrics for families, staff, and board members.

The program director of a hybrid program shares quarterly infographic updates on progress against strategic goals through multiple channels including the district’s learning management system, parent portal, and community social media accounts.

The coordinator of a virtual program creates video progress reports featuring student and teacher testimonials alongside data visualizations, distributed through virtual town halls and posted on the virtual program’s website.

EVIDENCE

Aggregated student data—including course enrollments, course completions, and credits earned—illustrate academic progress across the organization.

Communication artifacts, such as newsletters, dashboards, video messages, and social media posts, demonstrate efforts to share progress updates with stakeholders.

Website analytics and platform engagement data show stakeholder access and interaction with strategic progress information.

Stakeholder feedback surveys confirm awareness and understanding of the organization’s strategic progress.

Documentation of communication schedules and multi-platform distribution strategies shows a consistent approach to sharing updates and progress information.

A5: Digital learning leaders align budget, staffing, and professional development resources to strategic goals.

EXPLANATION

Strategic resource allocation ensures that digital learning programs are adequately supported and investments directly advance the mission and strategic priorities. Digital learning environments have unique resource needs including technology infrastructure and support, digital tools and resources to support students with academic needs, specialized staff roles, and ongoing professional development for virtual instruction. Leaders should make intentional decisions about how to allocate limited resources to maximize impact on strategic goals while addressing the specific needs of digital learning environments such as technical support, online curriculum development, and virtual community engagement.

EXAMPLES

The principal of a hybrid middle school uses strategic funds to build internal professional development capacity for personalized learning technologies, hiring an instructional technology specialist and providing teacher stipends for peer coaching in digital tools.

The director of a district virtual school adjusts the staffing plan to add multilingual family engagement coordinators and online learning success coaches in response to enrollment trends and commitments to educational access.

The technology director of a supplemental online program allocates budget resources for adaptive learning platforms and data analytics tools that support the strategic goal of individualized student progression and mastery-based learning.

EVIDENCE

Budget documents show clear connections between investments, strategic initiatives, and intended outcomes.

Staffing plans and hiring decisions include written rationales demonstrating alignment with the organization’s strategic goals.

Professional development schedules and resource allocations are aligned with strategic priorities to support capacity building and goal achievement.

Leadership team meeting notes document resource allocation decisions and explain how those decisions align with strategic priorities.

STANDARD B: ETHICS AND DIGITAL PROFESSIONAL NORMS

A digital leader models and establishes professional norms and ethical conduct in digital environments.

B1: Digital learning leaders establish and model professionalism in digital communication, presence, and relationships.

EXPLANATION

Ethical digital leadership depends on modeling professionalism in every aspect of online presence, communication, and collaboration. Digital learning leaders demonstrate integrity, respect, and accountability in both synchronous and asynchronous interactions. They promote norms of respectful discourse, data privacy, and responsible technology use across their organizations. By setting clear expectations for digital conduct, both within internal teams and public-facing platforms, leaders cultivate trust, foster psychologically safe learning communities, and reinforce the credibility and ethical standing of digital education programs.

EXAMPLES

A district digital learning leader establishes communication guidelines for staff and students that emphasize respectful digital discourse, accessible design, and data privacy.

A virtual program director models professional social media use, maintaining transparency and credibility while avoiding potential conflicts of interest or breaches of confidentiality.

A hybrid school leader facilitates professional learning sessions on digital wellness, online presence, and ethical online communication for educators and support staff.

EVIDENCE

Digital communication policies and professional norms documents address ethics, privacy, and respectful collaboration in all digital interactions.

Professional development agendas, attendance logs, and training materials demonstrate the organization’s commitment to digital professionalism through ongoing education and awareness-building.

Communication artifacts—including emails, newsletters, social media posts, and discussion forum transcripts—reflect professional tone, conduct, and adherence to established communication standards.

B2: Digital learning leaders promote ethical and responsible use of technology for teaching, learning, and decision-making.

EXPLANATION

Digital learning leaders set the tone for responsible technology use by establishing clear expectations, policies, and professional norms for educators and learners. They foster ethical decision-making, protect privacy, and encourage transparency in how digital tools, platforms, and data are leveraged for instruction and operations. By prioritizing fair and equal opportunity, safety, and integrity, leaders ensure that technology serves the best interests of all stakeholders, mitigates risks of misuse, and enables innovation within ethical boundaries.

EXAMPLES

An online program administrator implements and regularly reviews policies on digital privacy, responsible data sharing, and appropriate use of artificial intelligence for grading or feedback.

A digital learning coordinator organizes workshops on digital citizenship and responsible technology use, including case studies about intellectual property, cyberbullying, and bias in algorithms.

A school leader develops a code of conduct for virtual classrooms that addresses privacy, respectful communication, and ethical use of digital content and resources.

EVIDENCE

Formal technology ethics policies and digital citizenship guidelines are distributed to staff, students, and families to promote responsible and ethical technology use.

Documentation of professional learning events, workshop materials, and training completion reports demonstrates organizational efforts to educate stakeholders on ethical technology use.

Records of incident reviews and decision-making processes show adherence to ethical standards in technology use and data management practices.

STANDARD C: ADVOCACY FOR DIGITAL LEARNING

A digital learning leader advocates for fair and equal opportunity and effective, sustainable digital learning through policy, partnerships, and storytelling.

C1: Digital learning leaders build coalitions and partnerships to advance digital learning.

EXPLANATION

Effective advocacy for digital learning relies on leaders’ abilities to build and sustain coalitions that unite different stakeholders around shared goals. Digital learning leaders strategically cultivate partnerships across educational institutions, community organizations, businesses, and policy makers. These relationships amplify resources, expertise, and influence, enabling fair, innovative, and sustainable digital learning opportunities. By leveraging digital communication platforms and collaborative networks, leaders foster collective action and shared accountability to advance systemic change and continuous improvement.

EXAMPLES

A district digital learning leader collaborates with community organizations and technology companies to create internship programs that support digital skills development and career readiness for learners.

State virtual program directors partner with educational service centers and local governments to align digital access initiatives and share resources for broadband access and device distribution.

An online learning consortium develops cross-district professional learning communities using asynchronous and synchronous digital platforms to exchange best practices and tackle common challenges.

EVIDENCE

Memoranda of understanding, partnership agreements, and coalition membership lists reflect broad and inclusive stakeholder involvement.

Records of joint initiatives, co-sponsored events, and collaborative grant applications demonstrate active and coordinated advocacy efforts among partners.

Communication artifacts, such as newsletters, websites, and social media channels, highlight partnership activities and their collective impact.

C2: Digital learning leaders advocate for fair and sustainable digital learning models using data and stories.

EXPLANATION

Digital leaders harness the power of both data and narrative to promote fair and sustainable digital learning models. Data-driven insights provide evidence of learner outcomes, technology access, and resource allocation, enabling leaders to identify gaps and advocate for needed investments and policies. Complementing data with stories humanizes the experience of learners, educators, and communities, building empathy, inspiring action, and fostering broad-based support. By combining quantitative and qualitative advocacy strategies, leaders advance digital opportunities that are responsive, impactful, and enduring.

EXAMPLES

A digital learning director presents data on device distribution disparities alongside student testimonials in meetings with policymakers to secure funding for expanded broadband access in underserved communities.

An online program leader shares dashboards showing improvements in learner engagement and outcomes alongside educator stories in public forums and social media campaigns designed to build community support and partnerships.

A state digital learning coalition uses longitudinal data and case studies to advocate for sustainable funding models that prioritize fair access to technology and professional learning over multiple years.

EVIDENCE

Data reports and dashboards submitted to stakeholders demonstrate organizational impact and identify opportunity gaps.

Presentation materials that combine quantitative data with qualitative stories or testimonials are used in advocacy events to illustrate outcomes and needs.

Communications and campaign records document the use of storytelling and data to influence policy decisions, attract funding, and strengthen community engagement.

Dimension 2: Instructional Leadership for Digital Learning

Digital learning leaders ensure that curriculum, instruction, assessment, and professional learning are coherent, rigorous, and aligned to content standards and digital pedagogy principles. They evaluate and implement digital tools and strategies that enhance engagement, personalize learning, and expand opportunities for all learners. These leaders guide the development and selection of courses that reflect research-based practices, foster learner agency, and ensure accessibility. Digital learning leaders cultivate high-quality instructional teams through clear expectations, ongoing observation, constructive feedback, and targeted professional development.

STANDARD D: DIGITAL CURRICULUM AND COURSE DESIGN

Digital learning leaders ensure the quality, coherence, and accessibility of digital curriculum across all delivery models. Whether courses are developed in-house, licensed, or curated from multiple sources, leaders are responsible for overseeing the alignment of content to state and national standards, ensuring relevance to all learners, and upholding legal and ethical guidelines. Course design should foster engagement, offer interaction and choice, and provide clear expectations with appropriate scaffolding. Effective digital curriculum reflects strong alignment across standards, instruction, and assessment; supports personalization; incorporates Universal Design for Learning (UDL); and includes current, high-quality instructional resources.

D1: Digital learning leaders require systematic review processes to ensure curriculum and course design are high-quality, standards-aligned, and accessible.

EXPLANATION

Digital learning leaders advocate for and implement systematic curriculum review processes to ensure digital courses are rigorous, relevant, and accessible to learners. They ensure alignment with state and national content standards and principles of digital pedagogy, including Universal Design for Learning (UDL) to address learner variability. Leaders verify content accuracy and representation of varied perspectives while fostering instructional models that promote accessibility, digital citizenship, and learner self-regulation. Continuous improvement is supported through professional development, coaching, and use of data to refine instruction and curriculum to meet varied learner needs in virtual and hybrid environments.

EXAMPLES

A district leader guides curriculum teams to collaboratively review digital course materials for alignment to state standards, representation of varied perspectives, and accessibility compliance, using rubrics and structured review protocols.

An online program director establishes a review cycle where educators and instructional designers analyze course rigor, learner engagement, and accessibility using tools such as Universal Design for Learning (UDL) frameworks and accessibility checkers.

A hybrid school principal implements professional learning communities focused on embedding digital citizenship and self-regulation practices in course design and instructional strategies, supported by ongoing observational feedback and coaching.

EVIDENCE

Curriculum review documents and evaluation protocols demonstrate a systematic process for ensuring course alignment, academic rigor, factual accuracy, and accessibility.

Records of professional development sessions highlight training focused on digital curriculum design, accessibility, and instruction tailored to diverse learner contexts.

Reports and dashboards track learner engagement, monitor accessibility compliance, and measure outcomes across digital courses.

D2: Digital learning leaders ensure instructional design is engaging, accessible, and aligned with learner-centered goals.

EXPLANATION

Digital learning leaders foster instructional practices that prioritize inclusivity, engagement, and alignment with learner-centered goals. They champion instructional models that support accessibility, digital citizenship, and learner self-regulation while maintaining academic rigor and meaningful engagement. Leaders provide tailored professional development, coaching, and feedback to educators, especially in virtual and hybrid settings, ensuring that instruction is responsive to different learner needs and aligned with curriculum standards and program goals. Through continuous improvement and collaborative reflection, leaders build educator capacity and sustain high-quality teaching in digital environments.

EXAMPLES

A digital learning coordinator implements a coaching program where educators receive personalized feedback and training on Universal Design for Learning (UDL)-aligned teaching strategies and engagement techniques tailored for online classrooms.

A hybrid school leader facilitates professional learning communities focused on integrating digital citizenship and promoting learner autonomy through self-regulation practices.

An online program director uses data and educator input to adjust instructional strategies, ensuring that courses meet different learner needs and support high engagement with rigorous content.

EVIDENCE

Records of professional development sessions, coaching logs, and educator feedback reports demonstrate ongoing learning and application of community-building digital instructional strategies.

Observation and evaluation documents show the consistent application of best practices that foster welcoming and engaging digital instruction.

Data reports illustrate learner engagement, participation, and achievement that are aligned with established instructional goals.

D3: Digital learning leaders ensure all digital curriculum complies with legal and ethical standards.

EXPLANATION

Digital curriculum compliance requires adherence to copyright law, accessibility guidelines, and learner data privacy requirements that may be more complex in online environments than in traditional settings. Digital learning leaders should ensure that all content, whether internally created or externally sourced, meets established accessibility guidelines, such as WCAG, and all third-party materials are appropriately licensed, with attribution and citations, and safeguard learner information, according to FERPA and state privacy laws. Compliance oversight is essential for protecting both learners and institutions while maintaining the integrity of educational programs.

EXAMPLES

The technology director of a virtual school district ensures that all newly acquired digital content undergoes comprehensive copyright and accessibility audits before being uploaded to learning management systems, maintaining detailed compliance documentation for all educational materials.

The curriculum coordinator of a hybrid program creates systematic checklists to evaluate vendor compliance with data privacy requirements, accessibility standards, and content licensing agreements, requiring legal review of all third-party educational technology contracts.

The academic director of an online charter school implements compliance protocols that include regular WCAG accessibility testing, copyright verification procedures, and FERPA-aligned data handling practices for all digital curriculum and assessment materials.

EVIDENCE

WCAG accessibility audit reports and compliance documentation verify that all digital content meets established accessibility standards.

Copyright verification logs and licensing agreements confirm that all educational materials are lawfully sourced and properly licensed.

FERPA-aligned content approval processes and vendor compliance reviews ensure the protection of student data and privacy throughout curriculum development and implementation.

Legal compliance training records demonstrate that staff involved in curriculum selection and development are trained to uphold ethical and regulatory standards.

STANDARD E: DIGITAL INSTRUCTION

Digital learning leaders ensure that instruction in technology-enabled environments is engaging, learner-centered, and aligned with digital learning quality standards. They implement evidence-based strategies and systems designed for virtual contexts that promote belonging, active participation, and learner success. Effective leaders foster instructional models that leverage digital tools to build meaningful interaction, self-regulation, digital citizenship, and academic achievement. Instructional practices are responsive to different learner needs and strengths, and are supported through professional development, coaching, and continuous improvement tailored to digital modalities.

E1: Digital learning leaders ensure that learners are introduced, guided, and supported throughout their digital learning journey through structured orientation, clear expectations, and accessible academic and technical resources.

EXPLANATION

In digital and hybrid learning environments, learners require intentional, structured support to successfully navigate platforms, expectations, and available services. Digital learning leaders establish comprehensive orientation systems that introduce learners to digital tools, learning expectations, and support resources while promoting confidence and independence. These supports remain accessible throughout the learning experience and include clear communication of program requirements, pathways for academic and technical assistance, and guidance for navigating digital systems, ensuring learners can focus on learning rather than logistics.

EXAMPLES

A virtual program implements a learner onboarding sequence that includes guided platform walkthroughs, interactive orientation modules, and clear explanations of academic and technical support services available throughout the year.

A hybrid school maintains a centralized digital resource hub that provides learners with ongoing access to expectations, schedules, support contacts, and troubleshooting guidance.

An online program ensures learners receive clear guidance on how to access help, interpret course expectations, and navigate learning tools through consistent orientation materials embedded across courses.

EVIDENCE

Orientation materials, including screencasts, tutorials, checklists, and interactive modules, introduce learners to digital platforms, tools, and expectations for online participation.

Documentation of learner access to academic and technical support resources—such as help desks, support hubs, or service directories—demonstrates the availability of assistance throughout the learning process.

Records of learner participation and usage data show engagement with orientation activities and onboarding resources.

Learner feedback and survey data reflect clarity of expectations and ease of navigation within digital learning systems.

E2: Digital learning leaders define, implement, and support instructional practices that are high-quality, learner-centered, and adaptive to different needs through purposeful integration of technology, flexible design, and instructional practices that support all learners.

EXPLANATION

Effective digital instruction combines clear expectations for teaching quality with flexible, accessible learning experiences that engage all students. Leaders establish and communicate frameworks for digital pedagogy that emphasize collaboration, active learning, and connection across virtual and blended settings. Instructional models should accommodate varied pacing, multiple modes of engagement and expression, and differentiated pathways supported by technology. Leaders establish expectations for instructional quality and sustain a culture of innovation through continuous observation, feedback, and targeted professional development, ensuring teaching practices are responsive to varied learner strengths, needs, and learning contexts.

EXAMPLES

A digital learning leader establishes professional learning focused on technology tools that support personalized learning, such as adaptive software and interactive collaboration platforms, enabling educators to tailor instruction to individual learner profiles.

An online program director designs flexible course structures allowing students varied pacing and multiple project options, supported by digital resources that accommodate different learning styles.

A hybrid school principal promotes blended learning practices where educators integrate gamified activities and multimedia to increase engagement and use data analytics to inform instructional adjustments.

EVIDENCE

Documentation of professional development programs illustrates training on high-quality digital pedagogy, adaptive technology use, and flexible instructional design aligned with learner variability and accessibility needs.

Lesson plans, course materials, and instructional observations demonstrate the use of differentiated pathways and varied engagement modes supported by technology-enhanced learning tools.

Data reports track learner participation, engagement, and academic outcomes, showing correlations with adaptive, flexible, and Universal Design for Learning (UDL)-aligned instructional strategies.

E3: Digital learning leaders facilitate the design and support instructional environments that foster learner engagement, agency, and positive digital learning experiences.

EXPLANATION

Effective digital learning environments are intentionally designed to promote active learner engagement, autonomy, and meaningful participation. Leaders prioritize learner agency by creating flexible, interactive spaces where students can make choices aligned with their interests, strengths, and learning goals. Instructional environments leverage Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles to ensure accessibility and learner access, while incorporating collaborative tools and real-time feedback to build a sense of community and belonging. By reducing cognitive load through clear navigation and coherent visual design, leaders ensure positive, motivating experiences that sustain learner investment and success in digital settings.

EXAMPLES

A digital learning leader implements learning platforms that offer personalized pathways, choice boards, and multimedia content supporting different learner preferences.

An online program uses synchronous breakout rooms, discussion forums, and peer feedback systems to foster collaboration and social presence, boosting engagement and agency.

A hybrid school integrates gamified elements, real-time formative feedback, and interactive multimedia to create immersive, student-centered learning experiences that encourage self-regulation and persistence.

EVIDENCE

Documentation of professional development programs shows training focused on high-quality digital pedagogy, adaptive technology use, and flexible instructional design that support learner variability and equitable access.

Lesson plans, course materials, and instructional observations demonstrate differentiated learning pathways and multiple modes of engagement made possible through technology integration.

Data reports track learner participation, engagement, and academic outcomes, highlighting positive correlations with adaptive, flexible, and Universal Design for Learning (UDL)-aligned instructional strategies.

STANDARD F: DIGITAL ASSESSMENT AND LEARNER PERFORMANCE

Digital learning leaders ensure that assessment systems in digital environments align with academic goals, support continuous improvement, and promote meaningful learner growth. They ensure that digital assessments are valid, timely, and accessible, and that they inform instruction and learner support. Digital learning leaders guide and model the effective use of real-time data by teachers to monitor learner progress, personalize instruction, and identify areas of success and need. They establish systems and professional learning that enable educators to interpret and act upon live performance data to adjust instruction and interventions. Throughout all assessment and data practices, leaders uphold ethical and legal standards for data collection, use, and privacy.

F1: Digital learning leaders establish and maintain assessment systems that are valid, reliable, aligned to standards, and designed to be fair, accessible, and adaptable for all learners in digital environments.

EXPLANATION

High-quality digital assessment systems ensure accurate, fair measurement of learner outcomes across different kinds of digital learning contexts. Leaders oversee the design of assessments that uphold academic integrity, align closely with content standards, and incorporate accessibility and fairness principles. These systems provide varied, authentic opportunities for learners to demonstrate knowledge and skills with consistent rigor across delivery modes. Regular reviews of validity, fairness, and accessibility compliance foster trustworthy and encompassing evaluation, ensuring all students, regardless of ability, language, or access, can fully participate.

EXAMPLES

A digital learning leader ensures multiple assessment formats (quizzes, projects, portfolios) that align with standards and include accessibility features such as screen reader compatibility and adjustable font sizes.

An online program regularly conducts validity and reliability audits of assessments to ensure fairness and rigor across different student groups and digital platforms.

A hybrid school adapts assessments with alternative response options and language supports to accommodate learners with different abilities and backgrounds, ensuring equal opportunity for participation.

EVIDENCE

Assessment design documents demonstrate alignment with standards and the inclusion of accessibility features to support all learners.

Reports from periodic validity and accessibility audits or reviews confirm the quality and inclusiveness of assessment practices.

Records of alternative and adapted assessment administrations and accommodations show that learners are provided equitable opportunities to participate and demonstrate their knowledge.

F2: Digital learning leaders ensure that timely, specific, and actionable feedback is embedded in digital instructional systems.

EXPLANATION

Timely feedback in digital learning environments requires systematic approaches that provide students
with ongoing, clear, and constructive guidance using digital tools and platforms. Digital learning leaders should establish expectations and systems that enable educators to deliver meaningful feedback efficiently while maintaining personal connection with students in virtual settings. Effective feedback systems combine automated responses for immediate guidance with personalized teacher feedback for deeper learning support. Feedback should be ongoing and collaborative, incorporating opportunities for learners to self-assess and reflect, fostering a growth mindset and continuous improvement.

EXAMPLES

The instructional leader of a virtual school sets specific response time expectations for teacher feedback and integrates these requirements into observation rubrics, ensuring students receive timely guidance on their learning performance and progress.

The principal of an online charter school implements assessment systems that include auto-graded formative assessments with immediate explanatory feedback combined with requirements for individualized teacher comments on major assignments and projects.

The curriculum coordinator of a hybrid program establishes feedback protocols that include both digital annotation tools for quick responses and video feedback options for more detailed explanations, with training for teachers on effective digital feedback strategies.

EVIDENCE

Feedback policy documents outline clear expectations for timelines and quality standards to ensure consistent and effective communication with learners.

Learning management system feedback logs and response time analytics provide evidence of timely and structured instructor feedback.

Teacher observation and coaching tools focus on the effectiveness of digital feedback practices and support ongoing improvement.

Student satisfaction surveys capture learner perceptions of feedback quality, specificity, and timeliness, informing instructional refinement efforts.

STANDARD G: PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT, INDUCTION, AND EVALUATION

Digital learning leaders ensure that educators in digital learning environments are effectively onboarded, supported, and evaluated through systems that reflect the unique demands of digital instruction. They design and implement professional learning that develops digital teaching workforce capacity in course design, digital pedagogy, accessibility, and learner engagement. Digital learning leaders ensure observation and evaluation practices align with expectations for digital instruction, are grounded in performance data, and focus on continuous improvement.

G1: Digital learning leaders ensure that all educators are effectively prepared and continuously supported to teach in digital environments through comprehensive induction, mentoring, and ongoing professional learning aligned with digital instruction competencies.

EXPLANATION

Educators in online and hybrid settings require specialized preparation to teach effectively in digital learning environments. Digital learning leaders establish structured induction and onboarding systems that develop foundational competencies in digital pedagogy, communication, accessibility, learner engagement, and online instructional practices. Q2: Digital learning leaders ensure retention systems are in place that incorporate data analysis and targeted interventions.

EXPLANATION

Student retention in digital learning environments requires proactive systems that use predictive analytics, maintain human connections, and provide real-time interventions to support student persistence and engagement. Digital learning leaders should create tiered support approaches that identify students at risk of disengagement early while providing meaningful relationships and targeted assistance that address the unique challenges students face in virtual learning settings.

EXAMPLES

The student success coordinator of a virtual charter school implements comprehensive retention tracking that uses engagement analytics, assignment completion data, and communication patterns to identify students needing additional support, automatically triggering outreach from academic coaches and family liaisons.

The principal of a hybrid program tracks disengagement trends and adjusts schedules or access supports based on patterns.

The program manager of a supplemental online school integrates course progress data with student support notes to flag and intervene with students nearing withdrawal.

EVIDENCE

Maintain retention tracking systems and data analysis protocols.

Document early intervention efforts and measure their effectiveness.

Analyze student persistence rates and completion trends.

Track support system utilization and monitor associated outcomes.
