# Grade 6 history scope and sequence

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning build: four projects and 32 detailed teacher lesson plans. Step 6 student packets, source-dependent checks and classroom pacing remain. Tennessee 6.39 chronology is Unverified; SSP.01 oral modality is Conditional.

## Course overview

Students investigate how societies formed, compare their institutions and beliefs, connect inventions with historical context, and explain Rome’s transformation using evidence.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule | 16 weeks; four projects; eight 90-minute blocks per project; 32 blocks / 48 hours; individual then group each week; no required homework. |
| National basis | NCSS National Curriculum Standards (2010), public ten-theme framework; C3 Framework (2013), selected Grades 6–8 indicators. Theme labels NCSS-T1–T10 are local navigation labels, not official performance codes. |
| Content allocation | Early humans and Mesopotamia → Egypt/Nubia, Israel and India → China and Greece → Rome through western collapse and eastern continuation. Societies overlap in time; the project order is not a single worldwide succession. |
| Tennessee check | 2024 adoption / 2027–28 implementation. 62 content standards and six recurring practices routed; 6.39 chronology is Unverified and SSP.01 oral modality Conditional. |

## Progression and assessment

| Strand | Sequence |
| --- | --- |
| Inquiry | Guided sourcing/inference → contextualized comparison → distinguish historical evidence and modern measurement → increasingly independent corroboration and multi-cause argument. |
| Geography and chronology | Local settlement map → overlapping society maps/timelines → separate China/Greece exchange networks → changing Roman boundaries and eastern continuation. |
| Institutions and economy | Surplus, specialization and city authority → social roles/belief institutions → philosophical/state support and citizenship → republic/empire, infrastructure and political/economic pressures. |
| Source reasoning | Maker/date/place/type → purpose/perspective and missing voices → evidence limits and transmission claims → competing interpretations and relative causes. |
| Assessment | Practice and drafts get specific feedback. Final individual responses and fresh-source checks establish each learner’s reasoning separately from group polish. No invented weights, scores or cutoffs. |

## H1 — First Cities Evidence Dig

**Question:** How can evidence explain the move from mobile communities to early cities?

**Lasting understandings:** Historical explanations depend on evidence and its limits. Agriculture changed work, settlement and exchange unevenly. Geography, institutions and technology interact in city development.

| Course week | Priority learning | Individual / group blocks | Evidence and feedback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Paleolithic tools, fire, hunting, shelter and mobility; Neolithic agriculture, domestication, surplus, barter, clothing/shelter and specialized work. | [H1-W1-I](history-projects/H1_FIRST_CITIES_EVIDENCE_DIG.md#h1-w1-i): Read time and question the evidence; [H1-W1-G](history-projects/H1_FIRST_CITIES_EVIDENCE_DIG.md#h1-w1-g): Compare mobile and settled lives | Private continuity/change comparison and one evidence-supported cause/effect chain. If surplus is automatic, add a failed-harvest case and revise; use pictured process cards with a fresh explanation. |
| 2 | Tigris, Euphrates, Zagros, Mediterranean and Persian Gulf; rainfall/floods, water access, irrigation and agricultural risk. Food supply, technology, culture, writing, religion, government and social structure; irrigation/metallurgy/animals/wheel/sail/plow; surplus, trade, transport and city-states. | [H1-W2-I](history-projects/H1_FIRST_CITIES_EVIDENCE_DIG.md#h1-w2-i): Locate the Fertile Crescent; [H1-W2-G](history-projects/H1_FIRST_CITIES_EVIDENCE_DIG.md#h1-w2-g): Explain how an early city worked | Each student explains an institution and two links among agriculture, labor and exchange. ## H2 — River Kingdoms Atlas

**Question:** How did geography, institutions and beliefs shape different societies?

**Lasting understandings:** Similar geographic challenges can lead to different institutions. Beliefs and social position shape perspectives and daily life. Comparison requires distinct dates, places and evidence for each society.

## H3 — Ancient Science: Inventions, Sky and Navigation

**Question:** How did China and Greece use observations and inventions, and how can we compare their methods?

**Lasting understandings:** Scientific methods develop within institutions, needs and traditions. A modern model tests a principle without reproducing every ancient instrument. Chronology and corroboration distinguish exchange from coincidental similarity.

| Course week | Priority learning | Individual / group blocks | Evidence and feedback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | Gobi, Himalayas, Pacific, Tibet plateau, Yangtze/Yellow rivers; Xia tradition and Shang evidence; geographic governing constraints; Zhou/Mandate of Heaven and Legalism. Qin Shi Huangdi/unification, walls/roads/canals/writing; Confucius, Analects, kinship/order/hierarchy; Han government and technological achievement chronology. | [H3-W1-I](history-projects/H3_ANCIENT_SCIENCE.md#h3-w1-i): Locate China and explain early authority; [H3-W1-G](history-projects/H3_ANCIENT_SCIENCE.md#h3-w1-g): Connect Qin, Confucius and Han institutions | Each student explains institutional influence and corrects one unsupported invention/date attribution. If latest evidence becomes earliest invention, model the distinction and recheck; provide a date/evidence grid. |
| 10 | Aegean, Asia Minor, Athens, Macedonia, Mediterranean, Peloponnese, Sparta; mountains/sea, city-states/trade/colonies; polis, law, civic participation; Athens/Sparta government, education, women and enslaved people. Persian/Peloponnesian wars causes/effects; polytheism/Olympics; Socrates/Plato/Aristotle and education; Parthenon/Acropolis/columns; Greek/Hellenistic sky observation. | [H3-W2-I](history-projects/H3_ANCIENT_SCIENCE.md#h3-w2-i): Compare Greek places and citizenship; [H3-W2-G](history-projects/H3_ANCIENT_SCIENCE.md#h3-w2-g): Explain Greek conflict, ideas and achievements | Individual war comparison and an achievement/significance explanation citing a source limit. If science lens displaces government/conflict, use private retrieval before adding instrument detail; glossary and comparison cards support reading. |
| 11 | Han water clocks/sundials, Athenian court timer, astronomical patterns; elapsed-time versus time-of-day; historical evidence versus modern measurement. Repeated elapsed-time observations; water-level/flow variability; comparison with supplied source-labeled shadow data. | [H3-W3-I](history-projects/H3_ANCIENT_SCIENCE.md#h3-w3-i): Plan a fair timekeeping comparison; [H3-W3-G](history-projects/H3_ANCIENT_SCIENCE.md#h3-w3-g): Test a modern timer and interpret its limits | Each student records/reads a trial, explains a data pattern and states one limit on historical inference. If invented perfect repeats replace measurements, keep anomalies and recheck inference; accessible direct timing/recording participation or labeled supplied data. |
| 12 | Silk Road goods/Buddhist diffusion; Macedonia/Alexander/Hellenistic diffusion; dated networks and distinct chronology. If polished display masks history gaps, use separate map/institution/conflict mini-checks and source clinic; record unresolved component. |

## H4 — Rome: Republic to Division

**Question:** How did Rome’s institutions change, and what explains division and western collapse?

**Lasting understandings:** Institutions change through interacting political, social and economic forces. Different groups experience expansion and policy differently. Western collapse and eastern continuation require a multi-cause explanation.

| Course week | Priority learning | Individual / group blocks | Evidence and feedback |
