# Grade 6 M1 Supply Exchange scope and sequence

**Historical planning version — superseded.** Use the [current math course map](GRADE_6_MATH_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) and [current M1 teaching plan](math-projects/M1_SUPPLY_EXCHANGE.md). The earlier timing and outside-work assumptions below are retained for reference, not as current planning requirements.

Prepared October 1, 2026. Teacher planning draft based on the restored sixth-grade Step 1 packet.

Students plan, compare, test, and revise purchases for activity kits. The mathematics includes all eight standards assigned to M1, even when a skill does not help with purchasing. **Full standards coverage takes priority over a smooth project story.** Required separate instruction is labeled **SHOEHORN** and carries the same evidence expectations as project-related learning.

“Individual” and “group” describe the main work mode. Every block can include direct teacher instruction, and group blocks include brief individual checks. Group work never substitutes for a learner's own evidence.

This is a newly proposed instructional sequence using the confirmed four-week schedule. It preserves the restored Step 1 scope and the existing M1 project concept. It does not adopt the later software mini-module clocks or certify that an older Step 2 pacing plan has been validated.

## Project destination

Teams of three or four fill identical activity kits. Each kit requires **2 blue clips, 3 gold clips, and 1/4 meter of ribbon**. The teacher provides supplier cards with pack sizes, spool lengths, prices, and available stock. Students buy whole packs or spools, account for leftovers, stay within the published budget, and compare at least two feasible plans. Suppliers may be combined. There are no hidden fees or cutting losses in the classroom model.

Begin with a 12-kit order and expand to 18 kits in Week 2. Week 3 introduces a common change to stock or price. Week 4 uses a fresh, teacher-checked offer set and an announced change. Teams retain their original decision, test evidence, revision, and explanation. Each student separately defends a fresh purchase using different values.

The intended result is a valid and well-supported purchase. A low price, attractive display, or successful team performance does not establish individual mastery. GCF, LCM, distribution, and exponents may be taught through entirely separate mathematics.

## Prerequisites and learning order

Start Block 1 with a short check of fraction meaning and multiplication, place value and division strategies, decimal addition/subtraction, and familiar measurement units. Check factors/multiples and powers of ten before Week 3. These checks diagnose readiness; they do not count as Grade 6 mastery.

Teach ratio meaning before unit-rate comparisons. Use simple whole-number rates before decimal division is taught. Connect fraction division to models and multiplication before using it to justify ribbon capacity. Connect decimal methods to place value before accepting a cost calculation. Establish equivalent ratios before the full representation set, percents, and conversions. Teach factors/multiples before GCF/LCM choice and distributive rewriting; distinguish repeated factors from repeated addition before exponent evaluation.

If a prerequisite is missing, provide teacher modeling, manipulatives, or a smaller-number case during guided practice. Keep the original Grade 6 target and collect a fresh independent attempt later. Ready learners can compare additional valid plans or explain why an apparent rule fails without adding new mandatory standards.

## Four week sequence

### Week 1 Understand quantities and make an initial plan

**Block 1 — Individual: quantities and rates.** Introduce the kit brief and diagnose prerequisite needs. Teach ratios and unit rates using labeled quantities. Teach whole-number standard division with place-value reasoning (**M1-B; SHOEHORN**). Each student creates a quantity proposal for the actual 12-kit order. Collect a brief independent ratio/rate interpretation and division sample; record which methods still need support.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Launch and readiness check |
| 10–35 | Ratio meaning, part-to-part versus part-to-whole, guided representations — M.01 |
| 35–50 | Simple unit rates, units, nonzero second quantity — M.02 |
| 50–70 | Standard whole-number division, estimate and interpret result — M.05, SHOEHORN |
| 70–80 | Individual quantity proposal for 12 kits — I1 |
| 80–90 | Independent foundation check — C1 |

**Block 2 — Group: capacity and first purchase.** Address C1 misconceptions before comparison work. Model fraction division as capacity; connect a diagram, equation, and multiplication check. Teach decimal addition/subtraction with place-value models (**M1-A/B; added instruction**). Teams compare proposals and assemble a first feasible purchase and alternative. A short independent check samples fraction meaning and decimal methods; unfinished components remain on the evidence record.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Feedback, retrieval, and needed whole-division consolidation |
| 10–30 | Fraction-division model, computation, and inverse relationship — M.04 |
| 30–45 | Decimal addition/subtraction and model connections — M.06 |
| 45–75 | Group initial plan and feasible alternative — G1 |
| 75–85 | Independent fraction and decimal add/subtract sample — C2 |
| 85–90 | Record needs and retain the team's plan |

**Week 1 milestone:** each student has a quantity proposal; each team has an initial purchase, an alternative, and capacity/cost reasoning. No formal decimal-division comparison is required before Block 3 instruction.

### Week 2 Audit comparisons and scale the order

**Block 3 — Individual: methods and representations.** Teach decimal multiplication/division with conceptual models, including equivalent scaling for a decimal divisor. Construct equivalent-ratio tables, diagrams, graphs, and equations, connecting each representation to the same quantities (**M1-A/B; mixed fit**). Students audit one actual comparison from the team plan. The independent check captures a compact connected representation task plus selected operation work; it does not declare every representation or operation mastered.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–25 | Decimal multiplication/division and model connections — M.06 |
| 25–45 | Equivalent-ratio table, diagram, graph, and equation — M.03 |
| 45–55 | Guided practice with feedback and correct units |
| 55–70 | Individual comparison and cost audit of G1 — I2 |
| 70–85 | Independent representation/operation check — C3 |
| 85–90 | Identify missing evidence for later checks |

**Block 4 — Group: percents, conversions, and a larger order.** Explicitly teach both percent directions and unit conversion (**M1-A; SHOEHORN components**). Include examples beyond buying supplies. Publish the 18-kit order and mixed-unit offers; teams test and revise the original plan using the individual audits. Collect individual percent and conversion explanations.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Percent of a quantity and finding the whole from a part/percent — M.03 |
| 20–35 | Unit relationships, conversion, and comparison — M.03 |
| 35–45 | Guided percent/conversion practice and fraction-capacity retrieval |
| 45–70 | Group scaled 18-kit plan and retest — G2 |
| 70–85 | Independent percent and conversion check — C4 |
| 85–90 | Record which representations or methods still need evidence |

**Week 2 milestone:** an audited and tested 18-kit plan; all four ratio representations have been taught, and both percent directions and conversions have explicit evidence destinations. A 50% order increase alone does not cover the percent standard.

### Week 3 Teach number structure and revise under constraints

**Block 5 — Individual: factors, multiples, distribution, and powers.** Teach GCF, LCM, distributive rewriting, and exponents directly (**M1-C; SHOEHORN**). Use numerical and non-shopping situations. Keep this learning distinct from the student's project contribution: a backup purchase and vulnerability analysis applying previously taught ratio, fraction, and decimal skills.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Retrieve factor/multiple and powers-of-ten readiness |
| 5–25 | GCF, LCM, and deciding which is appropriate — M.07 |
| 25–35 | Common factors and distributive rewriting — M.07 |
| 35–50 | Exponent notation, meaning, and evaluation — M.12 |
| 50–60 | Guided number-structure practice and feedback |
| 60–75 | Individual backup purchase and vulnerability note — I3 |
| 75–90 | Independent number-structure check — C5 |

**Block 6 — Group: explain, revise, and retest.** Revisit every learner's number-structure work before team activity. Team members prepare and share a checked explanation of quantity, unit rate, fractional capacity, or cost. Each learner practices the mathematics beyond their own contribution. Publish the same stock/price update for all teams; they revise G2 and retest it. Collect fresh individual arithmetic and number-structure evidence; roles do not divide up required standards.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Common GCF/LCM/distribution/exponent feedback and retrieval |
| 10–25 | Prepare and verify quantitative explanations with the teacher |
| 25–35 | Peer explanation and application across the team's reasoning |
| 35–65 | Group response to the common update and retest — G3 |
| 65–85 | Individual check: 10 minutes arithmetic, 10 minutes number structure — C6 |
| 85–90 | Select specific Week 4 support and evidence needs |

**Week 3 milestone:** a revised purchase with a documented reason and retest. Every student has received explicit number-structure teaching; the team revision does not provide evidence for M.07 or M.12.

### Week 4 Complete individual evidence and defend the final purchase

**Block 7 — Individual: targeted support and independent transfer.** Use accumulated C1–C6 work to identify missing components before this block. Provide targeted reteaching and have students revise their own final recommendation. Follow supported work with fresh independent tasks. The 40-minute check uses connected tasks and previously collected evidence; it is not a complete new test of every component for every learner.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Targeted teaching/practice based on C1–C6 |
| 20–40 | Individual final recommendation, models, and cost justification — I4 |
| 40–80 | Fresh independent transfer: 20 minutes ratio/percent/conversion/fraction; 10 minutes algorithms; 10 minutes number structure — C7 |
| 80–90 | Review component status; assign exact Block 8 reassessment needs |

**Block 8 — Group: fulfillment and defense.** Provide a fresh offer set, verified to allow a valid solution within budget. Teams make a purchase, receive a common change, revise, and retest. Keep the showcase brief and use simultaneous presentations if needed. Each learner then completes a different individual purchase/defense and targeted reassessment. Do not introduce new required content here.

| Minutes | Learning or work |
|---|---|
| 0–30 | Group final: 12 minutes initial plan, 3 minutes common update, 15 minutes revision/retest — G4 |
| 30–40 | Classroom showcase of a meaningful mathematical revision |
| 40–60 | Fresh individual purchase and explanation — C8a |
| 60–85 | Targeted fresh reassessment of named open components — C8b |
| 85–90 | Record results, remaining needs, and next steps |

**Week 4 milestone:** four individual contributions, four group versions, and a component-level evidence record for every learner. Completion of the project and mastery of its eight standards are reported separately.

## Outside work

Each assignment has a proposed **20-minute cap**. Stop at the cap, record help and unfinished work, and bring uncertainties to class. Provide paper/offline equivalents; students need not buy supplies. Outside work reinforces taught material and never becomes the only evidence of independent mastery.

| Assignment | Placement | Work and classroom follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | After B1, before B2 | Retrieve ratio/rate meanings and practice the taught whole-division method. B2 addresses errors. |
| O2 | After B2, before B3 | Practice fraction models and decimal addition/subtraction; annotate one issue in G1. B3 reviews the issue. |
| O3 | After B3, before B4 | Practice taught decimal methods and translate a ratio between two taught representations. Bring gaps to B4/B7. |
| O4 | After B4, before B5 | Practice both percent directions and a conversion; explain one G2 revision. Teacher reviews C4/O4 before B7. |
| O5 | After B5, before B6 | Retrieve GCF, LCM, distribution, and exponent meaning with a small targeted set. B6 begins with feedback. |
| O6 | After B6, before B7 | Practice the learner's named arithmetic or number-structure gaps; prepare a question for B7 reteaching. |
| O7 | After B7, before B8 | Practice a diagnosed component and refine the final recommendation. B8 uses a different fresh reassessment. |
| O8 | After B8, by the end of Week 4 | Reflect on one consequential revision and record any remaining learning need; targeted review or extension. This does not retroactively certify mastery. |

If outside access is limited, use available in-class guided/project time for an equivalent supported task and record any resulting time shortfall. Do not silently add homework, a ninth block, or remove a standard.

## Assessment and teaching decisions

Keep a simple learner record: **component / evidence date and task / help provided / current status / next action**. Use the statuses **not yet evidenced**, **supported practice**, **independent demonstration**, and **independent transfer confirmed**. These are proposed reporting labels, not a new school grading policy.

A standard is ready for a full mastery claim only after all required components have independent evidence and later fresh transfer. Fluency needs accurate, reasonably efficient work on separated occasions, not a race. A corrected example is practice until followed by fresh independent work. Retain open components instead of averaging them into a passing team score.

Team feedback addresses four separate qualities: valid fulfillment within constraints, accurate mathematical comparisons, a justified revision after evidence/change, and a defensible recommendation. This feedback does not replace the component record. Students may use calculators to check team calculations after recording reasoning; individual algorithm checks require the student's own method without supplied computed answers.

Likely misconceptions to address explicitly:

- A ratio of 2 blue to 3 gold is not a blue fraction of 2/3 of all clips; compare 2:3 with 2/5.
- A cheaper unit price may require more whole packs and a higher total purchase cost.
- Fraction division can produce a quotient larger than the dividend; interpret the units and check by multiplication.
- Decimal algorithms require place-value and scaling explanations, not a memorized decimal-point rule alone.
- Percent depends on a stated whole; a conversion changes the numerical measure and unit together.
- GCF uses common factors, LCM uses common multiples; distribution preserves a sum's value.
- An exponent represents repeated factors, not multiplication of the base by the exponent.

Use counters, fraction strips, labeled diagrams, accessible graphing tools, vocabulary supports, and oral explanations where appropriate. Distinguish access supports from mathematical hints. Require student-generated numerical methods and representations when those are the target. Do not reduce mathematical demand because a learner needs reading, language, or motor support.

## Preparation and feasibility

Before instruction, prepare the kit specifications; two supplier sets and feasible budgets for practice; a separate final set and common update; fraction/decimal models; ratio grids and graphs; short standalone SHOEHORN tasks; compact independent checks C1–C8 with parallel reassessment forms; and a component tracker. Verify that each offer set has at least two feasible alternatives and that a valid post-update purchase exists. The old worked purchasing example is a teacher reference, not a fresh assessment.

This schedule reserves **720 class minutes**, including **170 minutes of independent checks**. That allocation is a planning proposal, not evidence that every learner will reach mastery in four weeks. Week 1 prerequisite repair, Week 2's representation/operation breadth, and Week 3's new number structure are the main pressure points. Review C2 before adding harder cases, C4 before Week 3, and C6 before selecting Week 4 checks.

Protect first instruction and individual evidence before optional comparison rounds or presentation polish. Reduce redundant project work when needed while retaining four individual contributions and four group work periods. If the learner's required teaching or evidence still exceeds the available time, name the component and needed support in the next instructional planning record; do not declare it covered or silently change the calendar.

M.07 and M.12 prepare students for later expression work. M.03/M.04/M.06 support later equations and geometry. Preserve unresolved components when handing learning records to the next mathematics project.
