# E1 Story Evidence Bureau

Grade 6 ELA project teaching document. **Step 5: eight detailed lesson drafts and assessment specifications, October 2, 2026.** Tennessee baseline: 2027–28. Proposed source texts are identified below. Text/access review, classroom pacing trials, and Step 6 material production remain open; these plans are not yet classroom-ready. ## Supplied project

- **Role:** Literary investigator.
- **Goal:** Test competing interpretations against passages and character changes.
- **Final experience:** An interpretation reveal supported by independent reading evidence.
- **Time:** Four weeks; eight 90-minute lessons, four individual and four group. The lesson-specific budget reserves 720 minutes, including at least 30 minutes of actual project work in every block; practical fit remains conditional on resource and readiness checks.
- **Primary assignments:** FF.G6.ELA.01, FF.G6.ELA.02, FF.G6.ELA.04, FF.G6.ELA.07, FF.G6.ELA.08, FF.G6.ELA.09, FF.G6.ELA.10, FF.G6.ELA.15, FF.G6.ELA.16, FF.G6.ELA.21, FF.G6.ELA.23.
- **Recurring assignments:** None; check prior-grade readiness.

## How to use this map

`ELA.01a` identifies a stable planning target under `FF.G6.ELA.01`; it is not an official standard code or a lesson ID. Source descriptions are preserved verbatim from the supplied Forge inventory below; all target, support, teaching, and evidence wording is planning interpretation. Exact official wording remains in the linked PDFs.

Primary targets retain their supplied project owner. The preparation/support and teaching paragraphs under each competency apply to every target in that section. Within the project, model first, provide guided practice and feedback, then collect an individual application and a later fresh transfer example. After reteaching, use another fresh check and retain unresolved needs. A team product or corrected shared answer is not individual mastery.

Support suggestions respond to likely difficulties; actual learner profiles are still needed. Document aids used and distinguish access supports from help that supplies the assessed reasoning. For writing-process and upper-band reading targets, preserve the guidance/scaffolding allowed by the source rather than inventing an unaided-performance rule.

**Recurrence:** Revisit the named components in the recurrence table. Use diagnostic retrieval to choose which need reteaching; retain component-level evidence rather than assuming every component was mastered again. All teaching, including content beyond the theme, stays within this project's lessons.

Diagnose Grade 5 literal/inferential comprehension, paragraph writing, conventions, vocabulary strategies, discussion, and technology access. Teach vocabulary and evidence use before demanding close interpretation; event/character analysis supports theme and craft. Begin reading/writing routines immediately.

### FF.G6.ELA.01 — Use standard grammar and usage

**Supplied Forge wording:** Explain and use pronoun case, intensive pronouns, pronoun-antecedent agreement, phrases, clauses, and varied sentence types.

**Tennessee reference:** 6.L.CSE.1 (PDF pp. 21). **Common Core reference:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.L.6.1 (PDF pp. 52). **Handoff teaching:** E1-A. **Later assigned recurrence:** E2, E3, E4.

**Prerequisites for these targets:** Grade 5 sentence boundaries, subjects/verbs, and reference to a named noun.

**Likely difficulty and support:** Pronoun case, agreement, and intensive use can be confused; a phrase may be mistaken for a clause. Use sentence strips, noun/pronoun links, and short spoken examples before removing cues.

**Instruction and practice:** Model explanations while reading/listening and deliberate choices while writing/speaking. Contrast examples, revise guided sentences, and later retrieve the forms in an unfamiliar passage and individual writing. E1-A remains explicit instruction.

## Step 3 — Success criteria and weekly milestones

**Essential question:** How can evidence change our interpretation of a story?

**Lasting understandings:** An interpretation must account for the whole text. Readers distinguish what is stated from what is inferred. Author choices shape meaning, and a stronger explanation may emerge after challenge and revision.

### Success criteria for the interpretation reveal

The audience is classmates and the teacher acting as fellow investigators. Each learner explains a defensible interpretation, considers a competing reading, and uses passages and character responses to justify a conclusion. Different evidence-supported conclusions are welcome. The shared case file includes competing readings, passage references, an episode/character map, and a revised explanation. Each learner's contribution is identifiable; display quality does not substitute for reasoning.

The individual evidence set combines an objective summary, whole-text theme/character analysis, a craft note, the revised interpretation, language/word applications, and a reading/writing/discussion record. These can share a case file; they are not separate long essays. [Assessment specifications](#assessment-specifications) define prompts, evidence conditions, and scoring. The target map above retains the precise component criteria.

### Four weekly milestones

| Local week | Principal learning | Milestone and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Evidence/inference; episode and character connections | Open case: individual notes and a provisional team board with competing readings. |
| 2 | Theme/objective summary; craft/viewpoint | Tested interpretation: individual draft, craft note, and revised board. Pronoun instruction supports clarity. |
| 3 | Reading across forms; substantive revision and language | Supported rehearsal: revised explanation, memoir/poem evidence, word/sentence work, and each learner's oral reasoning. |
| 4 | Independent application; explanation for an audience | Interpretation reveal, final individual work, fresh applications, and a specific E2 handoff. |

### Descriptive scoring guidance

Use **Demonstrated**, **Developing**, **Not yet demonstrated**, and **Not observed** for each applicable criterion. These labels support feedback; they are not a school grading scale or a pass threshold. “Demonstrated” requires the stated evidence under recorded, appropriate conditions. “Developing” means a specific part is accurate but required reasoning/use is incomplete. “Not yet” means attempted evidence does not show the criterion. “Not observed” means the task, feature, mode, or individual response is missing. Record permitted guidance separately; do not automatically lower achievement for access supports.

| Dimension / targets | Demonstrated evidence | Developing / not-yet distinction and next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence and inference · .07a–b | Accurate explicit conclusion and logical inference, each tied to relevant passages with explanation. | Relevant detail without a reasoning link is developing; contradiction or unsupported guess is not yet. Model a different passage, then use a fresh one. |
| Whole-text meaning · .08a–b, .09a–b | Theme development across separated moments; objective summary; episode connections and character response/change toward resolution. | Listing events or naming a topic alone is incomplete. Rebuild one link and check a different moment; keep the four components distinct. |
| Craft · .10a–c | Explains meaning/tone including the contextualized allusion, a part's structural contribution, and the author's viewpoint clues. | Device labels or narrator identification without effects are developing. Model an effect, then test another part or word. |
| Coherent writing and process · .21a–d | Purposeful, developed organization; visible substantive revision and separate editing; individual digital production/collaboration/publication; complete typed product with conditions recorded. | Editing-only changes do not demonstrate substantive revision. Missing digital or typing evidence stays unobserved even if the prose is strong. |
| Language and word inquiry · .01, .02, .04 | Accurate explanations and uses of every required form/strategy in the required modes, including pronunciation/reference and etymological reasoning. | Score each component from its cases and authentic writing. A correct form in one mode cannot establish another; reteach the specific gap and collect another case. |
| Reading range and writing routines · .15, .23 | Dated evidence across the required categories/purposes, comprehension justified with details, and short/extended writing with development over time. | A single polished response or team summary is insufficient; request the missing individual category or process evidence. Continue the longitudinal record in E2–E4. |
| Discussion/media · .16a–c | Prepared evidence, responsive contribution/questioning, perspective reflection, and explanation of what media information contributes. | Mere participation or recall is incomplete. Give a prepared new exchange or media example after feedback. |

For the **group case file/reveal**, separately review clarity of competing readings, accurate attributed evidence, coherent organization, and usefulness of the display. Use group feedback to improve the experience. It cannot replace the individual criteria above. No grade weights or mastery cutoff are assumed.
