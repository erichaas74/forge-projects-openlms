# E3 Public Knowledge Desk

Grade 6 ELA project teaching document. **Step 5: eight detailed lesson drafts and assessment specifications, October 2, 2026.** Tennessee baseline: 2027–28. Proposed sources and literary texts are identified below. Text/access review, classroom pacing trials, and Step 6 material production remain open. [Open the lesson plans](#step5).

## Supplied project

- **Role:** Explainer editor.
- **Goal:** Reconcile sources and publish a clear, sourced explanation.
- **Final experience:** A source-checked information release and editor conference.
- **Time:** Four weeks; eight 90-minute lessons, four individual and four group. The lesson-specific budget reserves 720 minutes, including at least 30 minutes of actual project work in every block; practical fit remains conditional on resource and readiness checks.
- **Primary assignments:** FF.G6.ELA.06, FF.G6.ELA.12, FF.G6.ELA.13, FF.G6.ELA.19, FF.G6.ELA.22.
- **Recurring assignments:** FF.G6.ELA.01, FF.G6.ELA.02, FF.G6.ELA.03, FF.G6.ELA.04, FF.G6.ELA.05, FF.G6.ELA.15, FF.G6.ELA.16, FF.G6.ELA.21, FF.G6.ELA.23.

## Step 3 — Success criteria and weekly milestones

**Essential question:** How can we turn reliable sources into a clear explanation?

**Lasting understandings:** Source quality shapes an explanation's reliability. Writers organize and elaborate for readers. New information can refine a question or explanation.

### Success criteria for the information release

The audience is classmates who need a clear explanation and peers/teacher serving as editors. The proposed inquiry is **why the Moon appears to change shape, and how that differs from a lunar eclipse**. Students choose a manageable question within that boundary. This context supplies material for ELA reading, research, and writing; no science-course prerequisite or NGSS coverage is claimed.

Every learner produces a **complete individual sourced explanation**, a research trail, and a **separate literary reflection**. The release is a class collection of those explanations with a simple shared index/introduction. It does not require a second group article. Each writer introduces the question, organizes evidence, explains rather than lists facts, uses precise language and helpful formatting/media, and concludes coherently. Source differences and limits remain visible rather than being forced into a disagreement.

At the editor conference each student justifies a source choice, connects evidence to an explanation, describes a substantive revision, and answers a reader's question. Keep individual authorship and decision-making visible. Group roles, attractive layouts, and a peer's correct answer cannot establish another student's learning.

## Step 5 — Detailed lesson drafts

### Student project guide

You are an editor helping readers understand a question. Investigate sources, write your own explanation and explain your choices in an editor conference. Keep a separate literary reflection alongside your research. Each lesson below tells you what to do and what to save. The expandable teacher planning reference keeps the original scheduling and standards details separate.

**How the project is graded:** Your teacher will review the project work named in each lesson and tell you which parts are clear and supported, which need revision and which have not yet been shown. Drafts and practice lead to feedback and improvement. Team work does not replace your own writing, reading or speaking/listening evidence. This plan does not set point values, grade weights or letter-grade cutoffs.

### Product size and reading support

Write one explanation of about 300–450 words that answers your Moon question using at least two relevant sources, a useful heading or diagram, and a short source list. Save research notes rather than rewriting them as essays. Develop a separate 100–150-word poem reflection over several lessons. Explain a source choice and a revision in a two-minute editor conference.

These are local workload estimates, not standards, grading cutoffs or a typing-speed requirement. Short practice notes receive feedback; the completed main product and named individual reading/speaking evidence receive final review. Reuse notes and drafts. No required homework is added.

Use NASA Space Place as the supported entry reading, then read the bounded NASA Science and Greenwich sections with diagram labels and a glossary for phase, orbit and rotation. Revisit the connected source set in Weeks 1–3; do not substitute a picture for reading. Read Dickinson with the teacher in Week 1 group. Read the Jessica Meir first-person interview in Week 2 group and a held-back answer in Week 3 group. Record which help you used.

**Examples to prepare in Step 6:** A short worked example using a different passage, a developing response with one specific improvement, and a complete model matching the product size above. Keep the student’s assessed interpretation or source choice open.

### Shared teaching and evidence routines

Start a source note with the writer's question and the source's provenance. Separate what the source explicitly says, an inference with evidence, and the student's explanation. Mark quotations while taking notes; paraphrase from understanding and then reopen the source to check accuracy. A bibliography does not fix copied sentences. Attribute diagrams/captions separately when their credit differs.

Use a word-inquiry record: self-identified comprehension/expression need → contextual/morphological possibility → print/digital verification, including pronunciation → checked meaning → later accurate use. Supply access vocabulary where necessary, but use another learner-identified word to demonstrate .06b if the definition was supplied. The glossary itself is not independent inquiry.

One portfolio holds the explanation, research/word trail, separate literary reflection, source/literary comprehension, short editor notes and digital contributions. Review actual target components and required modes, including written/heard interpretation and written/spoken use. Record learner, target, date/task, evidence, support and next action. Preserve permitted guidance for reading/writing-process targets; distinguish it from an adult supplying the assessed reasoning.

### Descriptive scoring guidance

Use **Demonstrated**, **Developing**, **Not yet demonstrated**, or **Not observed** for each applicable component. Developing means partly accurate evidence with a named missing part; not yet means an attempted response does not demonstrate the criterion; not observed means the necessary task, feature, mode or individual evidence is absent. These are descriptive feedback labels, not approved school grades or cut scores. Record permitted guidance/access separately without automatically penalizing it.

| Dimension / targets | Demonstrated evidence | Partial evidence and next teaching action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Informational meaning · .12a–d | Accurate explicit/inferential reading, central idea/detail links, objective summary and evidence-backed account of how the idea develops. | Topic facts or a sequence list alone are incomplete. Model the missing relationship and use a different source passage. |
| Informational craft · .13a–c | Contextual technical/figurative/connotative meanings, part-to-whole reasoning and source-supported purpose/viewpoint. | Device labels, guessed purpose or a heading copied without its contribution are incomplete. Model a choice/effect link, then check a new case. |
| Inquiry/credibility · .22a–b | Answerable question, justified print/digital selections and limits, integrated relevant evidence, and reasoned retain/refocus decision. | Teacher curation, logo trust or unexplained source counts do not establish judgment. Compare relevance/evidence for a new question. |
| Ethical evidence use · .22c–d | Accurate quotation/paraphrase, traceable source details and evidence-based writing in both informational and literary domains. | Bibliography without attribution or an unsupported literary reflection leaves distinct gaps. Model the missing operation and recheck in that domain. |
| Explanation development · .19a–b | Introduction and organization guide readers; accurate relevant evidence is elaborated into an explanation. | A fact list, copied sequence or evidence without explanation is incomplete. Revise a weak connection and transfer to a fresh question. |
| Explanation craft · .19c–e | Formatting/media helps understanding, transitions connect ideas, precise varied formal language fits the audience, and conclusion follows from development. | Decoration, arbitrary sentence variety or an unrelated ending is incomplete.
