# E4 Claims on Trial

Grade 6 ELA project teaching document. **Step 5: eight detailed lesson drafts and assessment specifications, October 2, 2026.** Tennessee baseline: 2027–28. Proposed sources, literary texts and hearing resources are identified below. Text/access review, classroom pacing trials and Step 6 material production remain open. [Open the lesson plans](#step5).

## Supplied project

- **Role:** Argument advocate and listener.
- **Goal:** Build a defensible claim and evaluate the reasoning of another speaker.
- **Final experience:** An evidence hearing with an individual argument and listening analysis.
- **Time:** Four weeks; eight 90-minute lessons, four individual and four group. The lesson-specific budget reserves 720 minutes, including at least 30 minutes of actual project work in every block; practical fit remains conditional on resource and readiness checks.
- **Primary assignments:** FF.G6.ELA.14, FF.G6.ELA.17, FF.G6.ELA.18.
- **Recurring assignments:** FF.G6.ELA.01, FF.G6.ELA.02, FF.G6.ELA.03, FF.G6.ELA.04, FF.G6.ELA.05, FF.G6.ELA.06, FF.G6.ELA.07, FF.G6.ELA.08, FF.G6.ELA.10, FF.G6.ELA.11, FF.G6.ELA.12, FF.G6.ELA.13, FF.G6.ELA.15, FF.G6.ELA.16, FF.G6.ELA.19, FF.G6.ELA.21, FF.G6.ELA.22, FF.G6.ELA.23.

## Step 3 — Success criteria and weekly milestones

**Essential question:** What makes a claim defensible when others challenge it?

**Lasting understandings:** Evidence must support a claim, not merely relate to its topic. Fair attention to opposing claims strengthens reasoning. Effective advocates also listen and adapt.

### Success criteria for the evidence hearing

Classmates and the teacher act as an informed hearing panel. Each learner presents a clear claim, accurately acknowledges a relevant alternative, and supports reasoning with relevant, sufficient evidence from credible sources. Logical order, useful media and intelligible delivery help the panel follow the reasoning. Each student also analyzes another actual speaker and adapts speech for a contrasting audience/context. Winning a vote is not a learning criterion.

The **proposed issue** is whether a fictional school should trial a small flowering lawn area, retain the current lawn, or choose a different planting approach. Students may support a conditional proposal or defer a decision for specified missing evidence. The case is a reading/reasoning simulation with clearly labeled invented site details, not an actual school landscaping recommendation. Regional source advice must not be silently treated as Tennessee-specific evidence. Teach necessary background inside ELA; no science prerequisite or NGSS coverage is claimed.

Keep four individual evidence sets identifiable: **written argument and research trail; separate neutral informative brief; literary reading and two distinct comparison sections; presentation and another-speaker listening analysis**. Teams share a source docket, hearing agenda and simple display resources. Roles never replace an individual's reading, writing, speaking or listening. The informative brief explains the issue for an unfamiliar reader; an argument introduction cannot replace it.

### Shared teaching and evidence routines

They may live in one portfolio, but their purposes and scores remain distinct. A student's speech does not show that the student evaluated another speaker. During listening, capture the speaker's actual claim/support before agreeing or disagreeing; do not fill missing evidence from one's own research.

Record learner, target/component, task/date, evidence, access/support, required mode and next action. Preserve permitted reading/writing guidance while identifying who supplied the reasoning. ### Descriptive scoring guidance

Use **Demonstrated**, **Developing**, **Not yet demonstrated**, or **Not observed** for each applicable component. Developing means partially accurate evidence with a specific missing part; not yet means the attempt does not demonstrate the criterion; not observed means the required task, feature, mode or individual evidence is missing. These are descriptive feedback labels, not approved school grades or passing thresholds. Record permitted support/access separately.
