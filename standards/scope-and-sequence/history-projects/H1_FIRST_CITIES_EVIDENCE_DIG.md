# H1 — First Cities Evidence Dig

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning build: four projects and 32 detailed teacher lesson plans. Step 6 student packets, source-dependent checks and classroom pacing remain. Tennessee 6.39 chronology is Unverified; SSP.01 oral modality is Conditional.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How can evidence explain the move from mobile communities to early cities?

**Student work:** Reconstruct early settlement and a Mesopotamian city using artifacts, maps and competing explanations.

**Final experience:** One evidence reconstruction board with a map/timeline, labeled artifacts, causal links and a revised explanation; each student supplies a private source-based argument.

**Lasting understandings:** Historical explanations depend on evidence and its limits. Agriculture changed work, settlement and exchange unevenly. Geography, institutions and technology interact in city development.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Plan status:** Detailed Step 5 teacher plans; source adaptations and student packets remain Step 6. Each clock is a planning budget, not observed pacing. Collect private evidence separately from team product quality.

### H1-W1-I — Read time and question the evidence

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Outcomes:** Individual dated sequence, source classification and justified source choice. Explain relevant evidence and context while identifying a limitation.

**Learning and project contribution:** BCE/CE, BC/AD, circa, decades/centuries; primary/secondary sources and archaeological inference.

**Starting work:** Grade 5 maps, sequence and evidence language.

**Selected resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). In class: 150–250 words of attributed teacher adaptation from 3.1 Early Civilizations: evidence/timeline introduction, with at most two map/image/source cards. Read once for context and reread the cited clue; preview 3–5 topic terms. Adaptations are proposed materials, not finished packets.

**Direct section links:** [OpenStax 3.1](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/3-1-early-civilizations)

**Source record:** OpenStax, World History Volume 1 (2023), modern secondary synthesis from Rice University: teaching audience, context/explanation purpose. For every reproduced primary item retain original creator or unknown, approximate date, find/origin place, intended audience/purpose when evidenced, collection/translation and stable source. Unknown metadata stays unknown; do not invent it.

Primary-object records and adaptation fit must be checked during material production.

#### Teacher procedure

1. Retrieve starting work: Grade 5 maps, sequence and evidence language. Ask learners to show one prior claim and its evidence; use gaps to select the short support clinic.
2. Read the bounded selection and inspect metadata. Learners mark one observation, one interpretation and an unfamiliar term. Teacher supplies topic context from: BCE/CE, BC/AD, circa, decades/centuries; primary/secondary sources and archaeological inference.
3. Demonstrate: Contrast an artifact with a modern caption; place three dated examples on a timeline and distinguish observation from inference. Think aloud using the different-content worked example below; learners explain why the inference is bounded before trying their own clue.
4. Build from saved work: Annotate a timeline; classify sources; pose the settlement inquiry and choose two sources that could answer a supporting question. Assign recorder, evidence checker, questioner and organizer in group blocks, rotating next block; everyone keeps a private reasoning note. In individual blocks work independently after the guided turn.
5. Collect the private response to the exact assessment prompt below. Allow source log and vocabulary aids, but no team-composed answers; record oral/text access separately. Ask a follow-up when a named component is missing.
6. Feedback and revision: If oldest BCE is reversed, model an ordered number line and recheck new dates; offer a labeled chronology strip. Use the developing response to contrast assertion with supported reasoning. Save the original plus correction, label unresolved learning, and carry it to the next block clinic.

**Explicit instruction:** Contrast an artifact with a modern caption; place three dated examples on a timeline and distinguish observation from inference.

**Worked example / expected reasoning:** On different dates, 3000 BCE precedes 1000 BCE; circa signals approximation. An object is primary evidence for its manufacture, while a present caption is secondary interpretation; neither alone establishes every user's beliefs.

**Developing response and feedback:** Developing response: “Read time and question the evidence proves everyone lived the same way.” Feedback: identify the time/place and one concrete clue, explain the mechanism, then name a person/group or claim the source cannot establish. Recheck with the different case specified below.

**Student task directions:** Start with your saved notes. Read the assigned source cards and mark the clue you use. Annotate a timeline; classify sources; pose the settlement inquiry and choose two sources that could answer a supporting question. Explain your own answer to the teacher prompt. Show one change you made after feedback and why.

**Project activity:** Annotate a timeline; classify sources; pose the settlement inquiry and choose two sources that could answer a supporting question.

#### Assessment and response to difficulty

**Private prompt:** Order 2400 BCE, 1800 BCE and 500 CE. Explain circa. Classify an excavated tablet and a modern museum caption. Which two source types could answer how a settlement obtained food, and how would they offer different perspectives?

**Collect:** Individual dated sequence, source classification and justified source choice.

**Evidence conditions:** Practice is coached. The private check is completed without a teammate supplying reasoning, with the assigned source log available. Equivalent oral, dictated or typed responses preserve the same history/evidence demands. Teacher records support and unanswered components separately; unobserved learning is not mastery.

**Quality criteria:** Correct chronology and labels; source choice matches question; inference exceeds neither object nor caption.

**Descriptive assessment:** Secure for the specified task: Correct chronology and labels; source choice matches question; inference exceeds neither object nor caption. Developing: relevant facts/clues present but a required explanation, date, comparison or limitation is missing; give component feedback. Needs further evidence: unsupported/inaccurate account or no independent response; reteach and collect a fresh check. Group accuracy/source attribution and organization receive separate descriptive feedback, not an individual achievement substitute.

**Difficulty and teacher response:** If oldest BCE is reversed, model an ordered number line and recheck new dates; offer a labeled chronology strip.

**Different-example recheck:** Use a different dated source card with one unfamiliar claim and full attribution. Ask which part of the previous conclusion still holds, what the new clue supports and what remains unknown. Collect a fresh explanation after the clinic, without copying the worked example.

**Extension:** Compare a plausible alternative interpretation using one existing source. Identify what additional evidence would discriminate between it and the current claim; do not add another polished product.

**Preparation:** Prepare the bounded source adaptation, map/image cards with captions/text equivalents, vocabulary strip, private response prompt, model and developing-response display, and held-back recheck case. Reuse the project board/atlas/display/dossier and source log; provide large-print/digital equivalents. Finished copies and keys remain Step 6.

**Next lesson handoff:** Prepare timeline cards and provenance labels; save the inquiry/source log for all projects. Carry saved private evidence and unresolved component notes to H1-W1-G; use its opening retrieval/clinic budget for a fresh check. Final unresolved needs go in the receiving teacher record. No homework required.
