# H1 — First Cities Evidence Dig

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–4 scope and sequence drafted and reviewed. Detailed lesson plans (Step 5), student materials (Step 6) and classroom pacing remain to develop. One Tennessee chronology item remains unverified.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How can evidence explain the move from mobile communities to early cities?

**Student work:** Reconstruct early settlement and a Mesopotamian city using artifacts, maps and competing explanations.

**Final experience:** One evidence reconstruction board with a map/timeline, labeled artifacts, causal links and a revised explanation; each student supplies a private source-based argument.

**Lasting understandings:** Historical explanations depend on evidence and its limits. Agriculture changed work, settlement and exchange unevenly. Geography, institutions and technology interact in city development.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Outline status:** These are Step 4 design specifications. Step 5 must select exact texts, verify interpretation-dependent standards roles and expand procedures/prompts, examples and support. The planning clock is 10 minutes retrieval/source reading, 20 explicit context/modeling, 35 project analysis, 15 private evidence and 10 feedback/handoff. This is a budget to test, not observed pacing. H3-W3-G uses the separately stated investigation budget.

### H1-W1-I — Read time and question the evidence

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** BCE/CE, BC/AD, circa, decades/centuries; primary/secondary sources and archaeological inference.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Contrast an artifact with a modern caption; place three dated examples on a timeline and distinguish observation from inference.

**Planned activity:** Annotate a timeline; classify sources; pose the settlement inquiry and choose two sources that could answer a supporting question.

**Individual evidence:** Individual dated sequence, source classification and justified source choice.

**Quality criteria:** Correct chronology and labels; source choice matches question; inference exceeds neither object nor caption.

**Feedback/support:** If oldest BCE is reversed, model an ordered number line and recheck new dates; offer a labeled chronology strip.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare timeline cards and provenance labels; save the inquiry/source log for all projects.

**Builds on:** Grade 5 maps, sequence and evidence language.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.1.6-8, D2.His.9.6-8, D1.5.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T2. ### H1-W1-G — Compare mobile and settled lives

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Paleolithic tools, fire, hunting, shelter and mobility; Neolithic agriculture, domestication, surplus, barter, clothing/shelter and specialized work.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model a multi-cause chain on a different settlement; explain that mobile lifeways did not simply disappear everywhere.

**Planned activity:** Compare two evidence sets; construct a settlement-change chain; teams propose and challenge two explanations.

**Individual evidence:** Private continuity/change comparison and one evidence-supported cause/effect chain.

**Quality criteria:** Distinguishes material evidence from assumptions; includes both continuity and change without ranking people.

**Feedback/support:** If surplus is automatic, add a failed-harvest case and revise; use pictured process cards with a fresh explanation.

**Preparation/handoff:** Create bounded site cases and preserve individual chains on the reconstruction board.

**Builds on:** H1-W1-I source and chronology routines.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.2.6-8, D2.His.14.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T2, NCSS-T7, NCSS-T8. ### H1-W2-I — Locate the Fertile Crescent

**Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Tigris, Euphrates, Zagros, Mediterranean and Persian Gulf; rainfall/floods, water access, irrigation and agricultural risk.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model map selection by purpose/date; explain how irrigation modifies the environment rather than saying rivers guarantee prosperity.

**Planned activity:** Label a bounded regional map; compare two possible settlement locations; annotate benefits, risks and human adaptations.

**Individual evidence:** Individual map and location explanation using two features and an adaptation.

**Quality criteria:** Features are positioned meaningfully; cultural/economic decisions and environmental effects are connected.

**Feedback/support:** If modern borders become ancient boundaries, compare dated maps then recheck; provide readable relief and text equivalents.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare relief/water maps and carry location decisions into the city model.

**Builds on:** Timeline and cause/effect reasoning.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.Geo.1.6-8, D2.Geo.4.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T3. ### H1-W2-G — Explain how an early city worked

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Food supply, technology, culture, writing, religion, government and social structure; irrigation/metallurgy/animals/wheel/sail/plow; surplus, trade, transport and city-states.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model surplus-to-specialization with alternatives; show institutions allocating water/work without a modern market assumption.

**Planned activity:** Build a city systems diagram; match each civilization characteristic to evidence; analyze agricultural tools and a trade route.

**Individual evidence:** Each student explains an institution and two links among agriculture, labor and exchange.

**Quality criteria:** All seven characteristics appear with evidence; mechanisms explain tool and trade effects; no single-cause story.

**Feedback/support:** If diagram is only pictures, add labeled causal verbs and recheck a new link; offer a systems organizer.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare verified agriculture/technology cards; keep the same city board for law and belief sources.

**Builds on:** Map, adaptation and settlement chains.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.14.6-8, D2.Civ.6.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T5, NCSS-T7, NCSS-T8. ### H1-W3-I — Read authority, hierarchy and beliefs

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** City-state versus monarchy/empire; Mesopotamian social hierarchy; polytheism and nature; writing, tablets, ziggurats and Gilgamesh.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Compare a ruler inscription with an archaeological account; teach how elite sources preserve some voices and omit others.

**Planned activity:** Annotate a social-role/source chart; distinguish belief from historical claim; connect cuneiform and architecture to institutions.

**Individual evidence:** Individual explanation of power/social position and one source-perspective limitation.

**Quality criteria:** Defines government forms; explains daily-life effects and belief context; treats an epic as literature/evidence rather than a literal event log.

**Feedback/support:** If a ruler speaks for everyone, identify absent groups and recheck a different source; use role/source labels.

**Preparation/handoff:** Select translations and exact object records; distinguish first-known empire claims and Gilgamesh traditions from universal absolutes.

**Builds on:** Institutions and artifact inference.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.Civ.6.6-8, D2.His.4.6-8, D2.His.6.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T1, NCSS-T4, NCSS-T5, NCSS-T6. ### H1-W3-G — Compare law and lived experience

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Hammurabi, written law, status-dependent justice; corroboration and source purpose.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model inferring audience/purpose by comparing a law excerpt with a second account; written law does not prove equal enforcement.

**Planned activity:** Compare two adapted laws and contextual evidence; explain affected groups; revise an unsupported equality claim.

**Individual evidence:** Private corroborated source-purpose inference and a claim with one limitation.

**Quality criteria:** Cites both sources; explains relevance and status differences; avoids reading modern equality into the text.

**Feedback/support:** If paraphrase becomes endorsement, separate description from evaluation and recheck; offer side-by-side source chunks.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare verified translator/date/section labels and carry law evidence into reconstruction.

**Builds on:** Hierarchy and source perspective.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.11.6-8, D3.2.6-8, D3.3.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T6, NCSS-T10. ### H1-W4-I — Defend the city reconstruction

**Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Synthesize chronology, geography, technology, institutions and competing settlement explanations.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model a supported claim with an alternative explanation and an evidence limit on a different site.

**Planned activity:** Finish existing board annotations; independently argue which explanation fits; use peer credibility feedback to revise.

**Individual evidence:** Individual argument from at least two source types plus one substantive revision.

**Quality criteria:** Evidence supports mechanisms and chronology; alternative is fairly considered; uncertainty remains visible.

**Feedback/support:** If claim repeats a caption, ask how evidence supports it; recheck an unfamiliar artifact; allow oral reasoning with citations.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare short fresh artifact check and archive each student’s source choices.

**Builds on:** All H1 source and city-system work.

### H1-W4-G — Review competing reconstructions

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Evidence conference, new artifact interpretation and unresolved inquiry.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Art of the Ancient Near East: educator resource](https://www.metmuseum.org/-/media/files/learn/for-educators/publications-for-educators/art-of-the-ancient-near-east.pdf). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Demonstrate critiquing sequence and causal links without judging decorative design.

**Planned activity:** Teams give concise reconstruction walkthroughs; each learner responds to a new artifact/caption; revise one causal link.

**Individual evidence:** Private new-source inference, limitation and teacher-recorded explanation; team board reviewed separately.

**Quality criteria:** New evidence is contextualized; explanation order is coherent; revision changes reasoning.

**Feedback/support:** If new object is dated incorrectly, use timeline/source clinic and a second card; carry missing evidence forward.

**Preparation/handoff:** Save map/source log and individual unresolved needs for H2; prepare source packets before detailed lessons.

**Builds on:** Source credibility, causal argument and timeline.
