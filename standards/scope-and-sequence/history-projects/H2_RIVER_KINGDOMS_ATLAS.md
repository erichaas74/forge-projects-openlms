# H2 — River Kingdoms Atlas

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–4 scope and sequence drafted and reviewed. Detailed lesson plans (Step 5), student materials (Step 6) and classroom pacing remain to develop. One Tennessee chronology item remains unverified.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How did geography, institutions and beliefs shape different societies?

**Student work:** Build a small linked exhibit for Egypt/Nubia, ancient Israel and India, using common comparison questions and distinct contexts.

**Final experience:** One shared atlas exhibit with three concise society panels and a common timeline/map. Every student completes evidence for all three; no specialist-only coverage.

**Lasting understandings:** Similar geographic challenges can lead to different institutions. Beliefs and social position shape perspectives and daily life. Comparison requires distinct dates, places and evidence for each society.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Outline status:** These are Step 4 design specifications. Step 5 must select exact texts, verify interpretation-dependent standards roles and expand procedures/prompts, examples and support. The planning clock is 10 minutes retrieval/source reading, 20 explicit context/modeling, 35 project analysis, 15 private evidence and 10 feedback/handoff. This is a budget to test, not observed pacing. H3-W3-G uses the separately stated investigation budget.

### H2-W1-I — Explain Egypt along the Nile

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Egypt map: Nile/delta, Upper/Lower Egypt, Nubia, Sahara, Red/Mediterranean seas; irrigation/calendar; occupations, enslaved people, pharaoh; polytheism, afterlife/mummification.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Egyptian Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/departments/egyptian-art). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model Nile direction versus Upper/Lower labels; connect calendar/irrigation decisions and social work to environmental conditions.

**Planned activity:** Annotate map and seasonal sequence; compare occupation roles; add a respectful beliefs/context caption.

**Individual evidence:** Individual map plus one agriculture/institution explanation and belief-context annotation.

**Quality criteria:** Correct relative locations; causal links use evidence; afterlife belief is attributed to historical practitioners.

**Feedback/support:** If Upper means north, follow upstream and recheck a blank map; chunk text and read captions together.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare two brief context cards and exact map/monument records; carry Egypt annotations to W1-G.

**Builds on:** H1 rivers, institutions and source labels.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.Geo.1.6-8, D2.Geo.4.6-8, D2.Civ.6.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T1, NCSS-T3, NCSS-T5. ### H2-W1-G — Connect Egypt and Nubia

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Hatshepsut policies/trade, Ramses military growth, Tutankhamun tomb discovery and revised knowledge; hieroglyphics/papyrus/Giza; Nubian trade/conflict and agency.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Egyptian Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/departments/egyptian-art). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model how a later discovery changes an interpretation; give Nubian actors their own context rather than defining them only through Egypt.

**Planned activity:** Build a compact evidence panel; place rulers/discovery on separate timeline lanes; compare trade/conflict accounts and artifact limits. Compare a brief, sourced modern excavator oral recollection with a dated discovery record; distinguish recollection from ancient eyewitness evidence.

**Individual evidence:** Each student explains one figure’s significance, an achievement and how a discovery or perspective changes knowledge. Include one comparison of the oral recollection and discovery record.

**Quality criteria:** Distinguishes ancient events from modern excavation; includes Nubian agency and sourced achievement effects.

**Feedback/support:** If tomb wealth proves all lives were alike, compare nonelite evidence and recheck; use a discovery/event timeline key.

**Preparation/handoff:** Keep panel to existing notes and two captions; select a Nubian-perspective source before Step 5. Select and caption a 60–90-second recorded excavator recollection, or attributed oral-history transcript, with creator/date/context; keep total new reading/listening within the block budget.

**Builds on:** Egypt map/social structure and H1 evidence limits.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.3.6-8, D2.His.6.6-8, D2.His.10.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T2, NCSS-T8, NCSS-T9. ### H2-W2-I — Trace ancient Israel and its traditions

**Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Dead/Red/Mediterranean seas, Jordan, Jerusalem, Sinai; Ur/Canaan/Egypt movements; Judaism, Abraham/Moses, Tanakh/Torah, monotheism, Ten Commandments, responsibility.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Distinguish traditions recorded in sacred texts, archaeological evidence and modern historical interpretation; teach religions academically with a common template.

**Planned activity:** Map attributed movement accounts; annotate people/texts/beliefs/context; compare what different source types can establish.

**Individual evidence:** Individual route/source explanation and a respectful Judaism profile.

**Quality criteria:** Uses attribution where historical evidence is contested; accurate text/belief terms; no devotional activity required.

**Feedback/support:** If every account is called verified eyewitness history, classify source type and recheck; provide pronunciation and neutral profiles.

**Preparation/handoff:** Select an accessible religious-studies/history source and exact attributed excerpts.

**Builds on:** Chronology, geography and source-purpose reasoning.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.Geo.1.6-8, D2.His.4.6-8, D3.2.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T1, NCSS-T2, NCSS-T3. ### H2-W2-G — Explain kingdoms, exile and return

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Saul, David/Jerusalem, Solomon/temple; kingdom division, Assyrian/Babylonian conquest/exiles, Persian return; cross-society comparison.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Egyptian Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/departments/egyptian-art). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model a dated movement explanation separating political cause from a tradition’s meaning; avoid modern-border substitution.

**Planned activity:** Construct kingdom/conquest sequence; compare one Egypt/Nubia and Israel movement; add two evidence-based exhibit captions.

**Individual evidence:** Private cause/effect sequence and comparison with dated evidence from both contexts.

**Quality criteria:** Actors/sequence are coherent; multiple political/geographic factors appear; similarity does not erase difference.

**Feedback/support:** If all exile periods merge, separate timeline bands and recheck events; use a movement legend.

**Preparation/handoff:** Limit captions to 60–80 words each; retain Israel panel and shared chronology.

**Builds on:** Israel routes/traditions and Egypt/Nubia evidence.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.1.6-8, D2.His.14.6-8, D4.2.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T2, NCSS-T5, NCSS-T9. ### H2-W3-I — Investigate Indus cities

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** India subcontinent, Himalayas, Indus/Ganges, Indian Ocean, monsoons; Harappa/Mohenjo-Daro, bricks, grid roads and sanitation.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model a city-plan inference and its limits; compare economic/environmental decisions without claiming undeciphered writing supplies a known government.

**Planned activity:** Annotate map/city plan; identify water/sanitation evidence; compare a useful Mesopotamian pattern with a distinct Indus case.

**Individual evidence:** Individual plan-based inference and explanation of two documented achievements.

**Quality criteria:** Separates observation from unknown institutions; explains infrastructure rather than praising advanced design alone.

**Feedback/support:** If a regular grid proves democracy, show inference limits and recheck a different plan; use magnified plans.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare dated archaeological plan/source card and retain inference notes for India panel.

**Builds on:** H1 city systems and H2 maps.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.Geo.1.6-8, D2.Geo.4.6-8, D2.His.9.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T3, NCSS-T7, NCSS-T8. ### H2-W3-G — Explain traditions and social change in India

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Indo-Aryan migrations/language/religious change; caste/social hierarchy; Hinduism: Vedas, dharma/karma/reincarnation/moksha; Buddhism: Siddhartha, Tripitaka, Four Noble Truths/Eightfold Path/Nirvana; medicine, yoga and numerals with correct dates.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Use matched neutral religion profiles; distinguish beliefs, social practices and later developments. Explain changing scholarship on migration rather than a single unqualified invasion story.

**Planned activity:** Compare the two profiles and dated evidence cards; explain hierarchy’s daily-life effects; attach a short achievement timeline.

**Individual evidence:** Private comparison including people/texts/beliefs, a social-position explanation and correctly dated achievement.

**Quality criteria:** Keeps traditions distinct and attributed; does not portray caste or either religion as uniform across all people/time.

**Feedback/support:** If unfamiliar terms replace explanation, model one in context and recheck another; provide a glossary and reduced-copy organizer.

**Preparation/handoff:** Busiest H2 block: two profiles totaling about 250 words plus a diagram. Verify comprehension/time; move caption polish to W4-I, never required reading to homework.

**Builds on:** Institution/perspective reasoning and Indus chronology.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.4.6-8, D2.Civ.6.6-8, D3.3.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T1, NCSS-T4, NCSS-T5, NCSS-T8.
