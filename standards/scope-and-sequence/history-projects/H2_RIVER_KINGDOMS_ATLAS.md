# H2 — River Kingdoms Atlas

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning build: four projects and 32 detailed teacher lesson plans. Step 6 student packets, source-dependent checks and classroom pacing remain. Tennessee 6.39 chronology is Unverified; SSP.01 oral modality is Conditional.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How did geography, institutions and beliefs shape different societies?

**Student work:** Build a small linked exhibit for Egypt/Nubia, ancient Israel and India, using common comparison questions and distinct contexts.

**Final experience:** One shared atlas exhibit with three concise society panels and a common timeline/map. Every student completes evidence for all three; no specialist-only coverage.

**Lasting understandings:** Similar geographic challenges can lead to different institutions. Beliefs and social position shape perspectives and daily life. Comparison requires distinct dates, places and evidence for each society.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Plan status:** Detailed Step 5 teacher plans; source adaptations and student packets remain Step 6. Each clock is a planning budget, not observed pacing. Collect private evidence separately from team product quality.

### H2-W1-I — Explain Egypt along the Nile

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Outcomes:** Individual map plus one agriculture/institution explanation and belief-context annotation. Explain relevant evidence and context while identifying a limitation.

**Learning and project contribution:** Egypt map: Nile/delta, Upper/Lower Egypt, Nubia, Sahara, Red/Mediterranean seas; irrigation/calendar; occupations, enslaved people, pharaoh; polytheism, afterlife/mummification.

**Starting work:** H1 rivers, institutions and source labels.

**Selected resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [The Met — Egyptian Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/departments/egyptian-art). In class: 150–250 words of attributed teacher adaptation from 3.3 Ancient Egypt: geography, agriculture, religion and society, with at most two map/image/source cards. Read once for context and reread the cited clue; preview 3–5 topic terms. Adaptations are proposed materials, not finished packets.

**Direct section links:** [OpenStax 3.3](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/3-3-ancient-egypt)

**Source record:** OpenStax, World History Volume 1 (2023), modern secondary synthesis from Rice University: teaching audience, context/explanation purpose. For every reproduced primary item retain original creator or unknown, approximate date, find/origin place, intended audience/purpose when evidenced, collection/translation and stable source. Unknown metadata stays unknown; do not invent it.

Primary-object records and adaptation fit must be checked during material production.

#### Teacher procedure

1. Retrieve starting work: H1 rivers, institutions and source labels. Ask learners to show one prior claim and its evidence; use gaps to select the short support clinic.
2. Read the bounded selection and inspect metadata. Learners mark one observation, one interpretation and an unfamiliar term. Teacher supplies topic context from: Egypt map: Nile/delta, Upper/Lower Egypt, Nubia, Sahara, Red/Mediterranean seas; irrigation/calendar; occupations, enslaved people, pharaoh; polytheism, afterlife/mummification.
3. Demonstrate: Model Nile direction versus Upper/Lower labels; connect calendar/irrigation decisions and social work to environmental conditions. Think aloud using the different-content worked example below; learners explain why the inference is bounded before trying their own clue.
4. Build from saved work: Annotate map and seasonal sequence; compare occupation roles; add a respectful beliefs/context caption. Assign recorder, evidence checker, questioner and organizer in group blocks, rotating next block; everyone keeps a private reasoning note. In individual blocks work independently after the guided turn.
5. Collect the private response to the exact assessment prompt below. Allow source log and vocabulary aids, but no team-composed answers; record oral/text access separately. Ask a follow-up when a named component is missing.
6. Feedback and revision: If Upper means north, follow upstream and recheck a blank map; chunk text and read captions together. Use the developing response to contrast assertion with supported reasoning. Save the original plus correction, label unresolved learning, and carry it to the next block clinic.

**Explicit instruction:** Model Nile direction versus Upper/Lower labels; connect calendar/irrigation decisions and social work to environmental conditions.

**Worked example / expected reasoning:** Upper Egypt lies upstream toward the south; river direction and north are different concepts. Calendar knowledge helps plan work but cannot guarantee harvests; occupations depend on institutions as well as the river.

**Developing response and feedback:** Developing response: “Explain Egypt along the Nile proves everyone lived the same way.” Feedback: identify the time/place and one concrete clue, explain the mechanism, then name a person/group or claim the source cannot establish. Recheck with the different case specified below.

**Student task directions:** Start with your saved notes. Read the assigned source cards and mark the clue you use. Annotate map and seasonal sequence; compare occupation roles; add a respectful beliefs/context caption. Explain your own answer to the teacher prompt. Show one change you made after feedback and why.

**Project activity:** Annotate map and seasonal sequence; compare occupation roles; add a respectful beliefs/context caption.

#### Assessment and response to difficulty

**Private prompt:** Label all seven Egyptian map features from the teacher key. Explain flood/calendar/irrigation decisions and one occupational relationship; attribute an afterlife belief without claiming the belief is independently proven.

**Collect:** Individual map plus one agriculture/institution explanation and belief-context annotation.

**Evidence conditions:** Practice is coached. The private check is completed without a teammate supplying reasoning, with the assigned source log available. Equivalent oral, dictated or typed responses preserve the same history/evidence demands. Teacher records support and unanswered components separately; unobserved learning is not mastery.

**Quality criteria:** Correct relative locations; causal links use evidence; afterlife belief is attributed to historical practitioners.

**Descriptive assessment:** Secure for the specified task: Correct relative locations; causal links use evidence; afterlife belief is attributed to historical practitioners. Developing: relevant facts/clues present but a required explanation, date, comparison or limitation is missing; give component feedback. Needs further evidence: unsupported/inaccurate account or no independent response; reteach and collect a fresh check. Group accuracy/source attribution and organization receive separate descriptive feedback, not an individual achievement substitute.

**Difficulty and teacher response:** If Upper means north, follow upstream and recheck a blank map; chunk text and read captions together.

**Different-example recheck:** Use a different dated map with a changed boundary/feature. Ask which part of the previous conclusion still holds, what the new clue supports and what remains unknown. Collect a fresh explanation after the clinic, without copying the worked example.

**Extension:** Compare a plausible alternative interpretation using one existing source. Identify what additional evidence would discriminate between it and the current claim; do not add another polished product.

**Preparation:** Prepare the bounded source adaptation, map/image cards with captions/text equivalents, vocabulary strip, private response prompt, model and developing-response display, and held-back recheck case. Reuse the project board/atlas/display/dossier and source log; provide large-print/digital equivalents. Finished copies and keys remain Step 6.

**Next lesson handoff:** Prepare two brief context cards and exact map/monument records; carry Egypt annotations to W1-G. Carry saved private evidence and unresolved component notes to H2-W1-G; use its opening retrieval/clinic budget for a fresh check. Final unresolved needs go in the receiving teacher record. No homework required.
