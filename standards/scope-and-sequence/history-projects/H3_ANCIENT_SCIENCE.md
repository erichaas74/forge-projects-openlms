# H3 — Ancient Science: Inventions, Sky and Navigation

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning build: four projects and 32 detailed teacher lesson plans. Step 6 student packets, source-dependent checks and classroom pacing remain. Tennessee 6.39 chronology is Unverified; SSP.01 oral modality is Conditional.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How did China and Greece use observations and inventions, and how can we compare their methods?

**Student work:** Study China and Greece in context, compare historical timekeeping evidence, and conduct one small investigation of a measurement principle.

**Final experience:** One paired China–Greece display with map/timeline, historical source panels, comparison table and investigation data; a short demonstration and individual explanation. Shadow data are supplied; the one practical investigation tests a water timer.

**Lasting understandings:** Scientific methods develop within institutions, needs and traditions. A modern model tests a principle without reproducing every ancient instrument. Chronology and corroboration distinguish exchange from coincidental similarity.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Plan status:** Detailed Step 5 teacher plans; source adaptations and student packets remain Step 6. Each clock is a planning budget, not observed pacing. Collect private evidence separately from team product quality.

### H3-W1-I — Locate China and explain early authority

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Outcomes:** Individual map/context explanation and one claim distinguishing tradition/evidence. Explain relevant evidence and context while identifying a limitation.

**Learning and project contribution:** Gobi, Himalayas, Pacific, Tibet plateau, Yangtze/Yellow rivers; Xia tradition and Shang evidence; geographic governing constraints; Zhou/Mandate of Heaven and Legalism.

**Starting work:** H1/H2 geography, evidence and hierarchy.

**Selected resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction). In class: 150–250 words of attributed teacher adaptation from 5.1 Ancient China: early dynasties, geography and Zhou authority, with at most two map/image/source cards. Read once for context and reread the cited clue; preview 3–5 topic terms. Adaptations are proposed materials, not finished packets.

**Direct section links:** [OpenStax 5.1](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/5-1-ancient-china)

**Source record:** OpenStax, World History Volume 1 (2023), modern secondary synthesis from Rice University: teaching audience, context/explanation purpose. For every reproduced primary item retain original creator or unknown, approximate date, find/origin place, intended audience/purpose when evidenced, collection/translation and stable source. Unknown metadata stays unknown; do not invent it.

Primary-object records and adaptation fit must be checked during material production.

#### Teacher procedure

1. Retrieve starting work: H1/H2 geography, evidence and hierarchy. Ask learners to show one prior claim and its evidence; use gaps to select the short support clinic.
2. Read the bounded selection and inspect metadata. Learners mark one observation, one interpretation and an unfamiliar term. Teacher supplies topic context from: Gobi, Himalayas, Pacific, Tibet plateau, Yangtze/Yellow rivers; Xia tradition and Shang evidence; geographic governing constraints; Zhou/Mandate of Heaven and Legalism.
3. Demonstrate: Model a geographic governance explanation; distinguish traditional accounts of Xia from excavated Shang evidence. Think aloud using the different-content worked example below; learners explain why the inference is bounded before trying their own clue.
4. Build from saved work: Annotate map and dynasty sequence; explain authority claims and Legalist responses to governing challenges; start China panel. Assign recorder, evidence checker, questioner and organizer in group blocks, rotating next block; everyone keeps a private reasoning note. In individual blocks work independently after the guided turn.
5. Collect the private response to the exact assessment prompt below. Allow source log and vocabulary aids, but no team-composed answers; record oral/text access separately. Ask a follow-up when a named component is missing.
6. Feedback and revision: If dynasty names become a list, connect a challenge and response then recheck; use a labeled timeline strip. Use the developing response to contrast assertion with supported reasoning. Save the original plus correction, label unresolved learning, and carry it to the next block clinic.

**Explicit instruction:** Model a geographic governance explanation; distinguish traditional accounts of Xia from excavated Shang evidence.

**Worked example / expected reasoning:** Shang material records and Xia traditions offer different evidence conditions. Mountains can constrain routes while exchange still occurs; geography influences possibilities without dictating one government.

**Developing response and feedback:** Developing response: “Locate China and explain early authority proves everyone lived the same way.” Feedback: identify the time/place and one concrete clue, explain the mechanism, then name a person/group or claim the source cannot establish. Recheck with the different case specified below.

**Student task directions:** Start with your saved notes. Read the assigned source cards and mark the clue you use. Annotate map and dynasty sequence; explain authority claims and Legalist responses to governing challenges; start China panel. Explain your own answer to the teacher prompt. Show one change you made after feedback and why.

**Project activity:** Annotate map and dynasty sequence; explain authority claims and Legalist responses to governing challenges; start China panel.

#### Assessment and response to difficulty

**Private prompt:** Label China's six features and sequence Xia tradition, Shang evidence and Zhou authority. Explain one geographic influence without claiming complete isolation. Contrast Mandate of Heaven and Legalism as governing ideas.

**Collect:** Individual map/context explanation and one claim distinguishing tradition/evidence.

**Evidence conditions:** Practice is coached. The private check is completed without a teammate supplying reasoning, with the assigned source log available. Equivalent oral, dictated or typed responses preserve the same history/evidence demands. Teacher records support and unanswered components separately; unobserved learning is not mastery.

**Quality criteria:** Maps all required features; connects geography to institutions without assuming total isolation; dates/claim types are explicit.

**Descriptive assessment:** Secure for the specified task: Maps all required features; connects geography to institutions without assuming total isolation; dates/claim types are explicit. Developing: relevant facts/clues present but a required explanation, date, comparison or limitation is missing; give component feedback. Needs further evidence: unsupported/inaccurate account or no independent response; reteach and collect a fresh check. Group accuracy/source attribution and organization receive separate descriptive feedback, not an individual achievement substitute.

**Difficulty and teacher response:** If dynasty names become a list, connect a challenge and response then recheck; use a labeled timeline strip.

**Different-example recheck:** Use a different dated map with a changed boundary/feature. Ask which part of the previous conclusion still holds, what the new clue supports and what remains unknown. Collect a fresh explanation after the clinic, without copying the worked example.

**Extension:** Compare a plausible alternative interpretation using one existing source. Identify what additional evidence would discriminate between it and the current claim; do not add another polished product.

**Preparation:** Prepare the bounded source adaptation, map/image cards with captions/text equivalents, vocabulary strip, private response prompt, model and developing-response display, and held-back recheck case. Reuse the project board/atlas/display/dossier and source log; provide large-print/digital equivalents. Finished copies and keys remain Step 6.

**Next lesson handoff:** Prepare two context cards and retain authority questions for Qin/Han. Carry saved private evidence and unresolved component notes to H3-W1-G; use its opening retrieval/clinic budget for a fresh check. Final unresolved needs go in the receiving teacher record. No homework required.
