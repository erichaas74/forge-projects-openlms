# H4 — Rome: Republic to Division

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–4 scope and sequence drafted and reviewed. Detailed lesson plans (Step 5), student materials (Step 6) and classroom pacing remain to develop. One Tennessee chronology item remains unverified.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How did Rome’s institutions change, and what explains division and western collapse?

**Student work:** Build a Roman change-and-cause dossier, compare accounts and hold a historical evidence council using actual outcomes.

**Final experience:** One team council dossier with map/timeline, institutional comparison and causal evidence chart. Each student submits a short independent multi-cause account and fresh-source revision.

**Lasting understandings:** Institutions change through interacting political, social and economic forces. Different groups experience expansion and policy differently. Western collapse and eastern continuation require a multi-cause explanation.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Outline status:** These are Step 4 design specifications. Step 5 must select exact texts, verify interpretation-dependent standards roles and expand procedures/prompts, examples and support. The planning clock is 10 minutes retrieval/source reading, 20 explicit context/modeling, 35 project analysis, 15 private evidence and 10 feedback/handoff. This is a budget to test, not observed pacing. H3-W3-G uses the separately stated investigation budget.

### H4-W1-I — Locate Rome and explain the republic

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Tiber, Rome, Italy/Alps, Mediterranean, Constantinople later timeline; geography/growth; patricians, plebeians and enslaved people; republican offices, Senate/assemblies, checks, participation and Twelve Tables.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model offices/participation without calling Rome a modern inclusive democracy; place later Constantinople on a dated map layer.

**Planned activity:** Annotate geographic growth map; diagram institutions and eligible participation; compare a rule to affected social groups.

**Individual evidence:** Individual map plus institution/participation explanation including excluded groups.

**Quality criteria:** Dates/layers explicit; government functions and social limits clear; geography connected to growth.

**Feedback/support:** If checks imply equality for everyone, use participation matrix and recheck; accessible map/source chunks.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare verified Twelve Tables translation and dated maps; reuse H3 citizenship comparison.

**Builds on:** Greek polis and source-based comparison.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.Geo.1.6-8, D2.Civ.6.6-8, D2.His.4.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T3, NCSS-T4, NCSS-T5, NCSS-T6, NCSS-T10. ### H4-W1-G — Explain republic-to-empire change

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Julius Caesar military leadership, popular support, dictatorship/assassination; Augustus and Pax Romana expansion and institutions.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Compare a ruler image/claim to another account; trace structural tensions alongside leaders and trigger events.

**Planned activity:** Build transition timeline; compare institutional change and source perspectives; add cause/effect evidence to council dossier.

**Individual evidence:** Private multi-factor transition explanation and ruler-source purpose inference.

**Quality criteria:** Distinguishes Caesar from Augustus and continuity from change; propaganda not treated as complete social evidence.

**Feedback/support:** If one leader explains everything, add a structural evidence card and recheck; use dated actors/events.

**Preparation/handoff:** Select contrasting accounts and dated coin/inscription; preserve institutional chart for W4 synthesis.

### H4-W2-G — Contextualize Christianity and Jewish diaspora

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Christianity: Jesus/Paul, Bible, monotheism, sin/forgiveness, eternal life, Messiah; historical spread/persecution and imperial change; Roman/Jewish conflicts and dispersal.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Use the common religion template from H2; distinguish faith claims from historical analysis and explain multiple diaspora phases.

**Planned activity:** Annotate religion/context cards; map dated displacement/spread; compare two attributed accounts without devotional performance.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare sensitive, scholarly source selection and timeline; no present-day group blame.

**Builds on:** H2 Judaism and respectful religious-source inquiry.

### H4-W3-I — Map division and eastern continuation

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Constantine/Constantinople; administrative division versus relocation; East/West and Byzantine continuation; territory/governing pressures.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model changing dated boundaries and multiple stages of division; 476 in the West does not end every Roman institution.

**Planned activity:** Layer empire maps; identify capital-location factors; sort continuity/change evidence for eastern/western regions.

**Individual evidence:** Individual division map and explanation of one continuity and two capital/governance factors.

**Quality criteria:** Distinguishes division from western collapse; East continues; time/place shapes cause.

**Feedback/support:** If a single map becomes timeless, add dates and recheck layers; use paired maps with consistent scale.

**Preparation/handoff:** Prepare map dates and source captions; save pressures for cause-ranking session.

**Builds on:** Rome institutions, geography and regional comparison.

### H4-W3-G — Weigh explanations for western collapse

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Large territory, political instability/corruption, economic pressures, Germanic groups and military conflict; relative cause influence and evidence limits.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model comparing the explanatory reach of causes; avoid a single invasion-only or moral-decline explanation.

**Planned activity:** Compare three bounded interpretations with evidence; make a causal web; weigh two causes and challenge unsupported claims.

**Individual evidence:** Private cause-ranking argument with evidence, a competing view and limitation.

**Quality criteria:** Multiple interacting causes; relative influence justified; distinguishes evidence from a rhetorical moral claim.

**Feedback/support:** If ranking is personal preference, require a sourced mechanism then recheck a new cause; provide an evidence/cause chart.

**Preparation/handoff:** Select contrasting historian arguments and provenance; reserve revision for W4-I.

**Builds on:** Division/continuation and source credibility.

**National roles:** Primary: D2.His.15.6-8, D3.3.6-8, D4.4.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T2, NCSS-T6, NCSS-T7, NCSS-T9. ### H4-W4-I — Prepare the evidence council

**Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Synthesize republic, empire, infrastructure, religion, diaspora, division and fall; evaluate arguments and coherent explanations.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model an evidence council discussing actual historical causes; conjectured policy outcomes, if mentioned, are labeled counterfactual.

**Planned activity:** Finish existing dossier; individually write a bounded multi-cause account; critique source credibility and sequence; revise one argument.

**Individual evidence:** Independent 200–300-word or equivalent causal account with multiple sources, limitation and revision.

**Quality criteria:** Historical outcomes accurate; East/West distinction preserved; evidence addresses causes and institutional change.

**Feedback/support:** If team consensus replaces private learning, collect independent account first; scaffold organization without supplying the claim.

### H4-W4-G — Hold the council and apply new evidence

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Learning and project contribution:** Evidence council, fresh historical source interpretation and course-wide inquiry reflection.

**Resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). Planning limit: 150–250 words of new adapted prose plus up to two images/maps/source cards. Exact excerpts, provenance and access pending; essential reading stays in class.

**Instruction/model to develop:** Model civil critique: identify claim, relevant source, limitation and evidence that would change the conclusion.

**Planned activity:** Teams present actual-history explanations; every student responds to a question; independently analyze a fresh source and revise the causal account.

**Individual evidence:** Teacher-recorded response, fresh source inference and independent revision; team council quality assessed separately.

**Quality criteria:** Civil discussion and supported reasoning; new evidence changes or sustains a conclusion for a stated reason.

**Feedback/support:** If performance masks missing history, use targeted map/chronology/source recheck; record remaining learning for the next course.

**Preparation/handoff:** Archive inquiry growth and unresolved content; use a class-approved exhibit correction as a bounded civic practice, without a full informed-action indicator claim.

**Builds on:** All-course source selection, comparison and causation.

**National roles:** Primary: D4.1.6-8, D4.4.6-8, D2.His.10.6-8; Supporting: D3.1.6-8. NCSS themes: NCSS-T2, NCSS-T9, NCSS-T10.
