# H4 — Rome: Republic to Division

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning build: four projects and 32 detailed teacher lesson plans. Step 6 student packets, source-dependent checks and classroom pacing remain. Tennessee 6.39 chronology is Unverified; SSP.01 oral modality is Conditional.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_HISTORY_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_History_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

**Inquiry:** How did Rome’s institutions change, and what explains division and western collapse?

**Student work:** Build a Roman change-and-cause dossier, compare accounts and hold a historical evidence council using actual outcomes.

**Final experience:** One team council dossier with map/timeline, institutional comparison and causal evidence chart. Each student submits a short independent multi-cause account and fresh-source revision.

**Lasting understandings:** Institutions change through interacting political, social and economic forces. Different groups experience expansion and policy differently. Western collapse and eastern continuation require a multi-cause explanation.

**Schedule and access:** Four weeks, eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group weekly. No required homework. Plan captions/diagram descriptions, readable maps, vocabulary previews and equivalent oral/text response routes preserving historical reasoning. Physical investigation access must be arranged for H3.

**Plan status:** Detailed Step 5 teacher plans; source adaptations and student packets remain Step 6. Each clock is a planning budget, not observed pacing. Collect private evidence separately from team product quality.

### H4-W1-I — Locate Rome and explain the republic

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Outcomes:** Individual map plus institution/participation explanation including excluded groups. Explain relevant evidence and context while identifying a limitation.

**Learning and project contribution:** Tiber, Rome, Italy/Alps, Mediterranean, Constantinople later timeline; geography/growth; patricians, plebeians and enslaved people; republican offices, Senate/assemblies, checks, participation and Twelve Tables.

**Starting work:** Greek polis and source-based comparison.

**Selected resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). In class: 150–250 words of attributed teacher adaptation from 6.4 The Roman Republic: institutions, law, social groups and geography, with at most two map/image/source cards. Read once for context and reread the cited clue; preview 3–5 topic terms. Adaptations are proposed materials, not finished packets.

**Direct section links:** [OpenStax 6.4](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/6-4-the-roman-republic)

**Source record:** OpenStax, World History Volume 1 (2023), modern secondary synthesis from Rice University: teaching audience, context/explanation purpose. For every reproduced primary item retain original creator or unknown, approximate date, find/origin place, intended audience/purpose when evidenced, collection/translation and stable source. Unknown metadata stays unknown; do not invent it.

Primary-object records and adaptation fit must be checked during material production.

#### Teacher procedure

1. Retrieve starting work: Greek polis and source-based comparison. Ask learners to show one prior claim and its evidence; use gaps to select the short support clinic.
2. Read the bounded selection and inspect metadata. Learners mark one observation, one interpretation and an unfamiliar term. Teacher supplies topic context from: Tiber, Rome, Italy/Alps, Mediterranean, Constantinople later timeline; geography/growth; patricians, plebeians and enslaved people; republican offices, Senate/assemblies, checks, participation and Twelve Tables.
3. Demonstrate: Model offices/participation without calling Rome a modern inclusive democracy; place later Constantinople on a dated map layer. Think aloud using the different-content worked example below; learners explain why the inference is bounded before trying their own clue.
4. Build from saved work: Annotate geographic growth map; diagram institutions and eligible participation; compare a rule to affected social groups. Assign recorder, evidence checker, questioner and organizer in group blocks, rotating next block; everyone keeps a private reasoning note. In individual blocks work independently after the guided turn.
5. Collect the private response to the exact assessment prompt below. Allow source log and vocabulary aids, but no team-composed answers; record oral/text access separately. Ask a follow-up when a named component is missing.
6. Feedback and revision: If checks imply equality for everyone, use participation matrix and recheck; accessible map/source chunks. Use the developing response to contrast assertion with supported reasoning. Save the original plus correction, label unresolved learning, and carry it to the next block clinic.

**Explicit instruction:** Model offices/participation without calling Rome a modern inclusive democracy; place later Constantinople on a dated map layer.

**Worked example / expected reasoning:** An institutional diagram shows a check on officials but also eligibility limits. A road/marine route may help movement; access alone does not explain every conquest.

**Developing response and feedback:** Developing response: “Locate Rome and explain the republic proves everyone lived the same way.” Feedback: identify the time/place and one concrete clue, explain the mechanism, then name a person/group or claim the source cannot establish. Recheck with the different case specified below.

**Student task directions:** Start with your saved notes. Read the assigned source cards and mark the clue you use. Annotate geographic growth map; diagram institutions and eligible participation; compare a rule to affected social groups. Explain your own answer to the teacher prompt. Show one change you made after feedback and why.

**Project activity:** Annotate geographic growth map; diagram institutions and eligible participation; compare a rule to affected social groups.

#### Assessment and response to difficulty

**Private prompt:** Label Rome's six features, dating Constantinople as a later capital. Diagram republic branches/checks and Twelve Tables; explain patrician/plebeian/enslaved participation and a geographic mechanism of expansion.

**Collect:** Individual map plus institution/participation explanation including excluded groups.

**Evidence conditions:** Practice is coached. The private check is completed without a teammate supplying reasoning, with the assigned source log available. Equivalent oral, dictated or typed responses preserve the same history/evidence demands. Teacher records support and unanswered components separately; unobserved learning is not mastery.

**Quality criteria:** Dates/layers explicit; government functions and social limits clear; geography connected to growth.

**Descriptive assessment:** Secure for the specified task: Dates/layers explicit; government functions and social limits clear; geography connected to growth. Developing: relevant facts/clues present but a required explanation, date, comparison or limitation is missing; give component feedback. Needs further evidence: unsupported/inaccurate account or no independent response; reteach and collect a fresh check. Group accuracy/source attribution and organization receive separate descriptive feedback, not an individual achievement substitute.

**Difficulty and teacher response:** If checks imply equality for everyone, use participation matrix and recheck; accessible map/source chunks.

**Different-example recheck:** Use a different dated map with a changed boundary/feature. Ask which part of the previous conclusion still holds, what the new clue supports and what remains unknown. Collect a fresh explanation after the clinic, without copying the worked example.

**Extension:** Compare a plausible alternative interpretation using one existing source. Identify what additional evidence would discriminate between it and the current claim; do not add another polished product.

**Preparation:** Prepare the bounded source adaptation, map/image cards with captions/text equivalents, vocabulary strip, private response prompt, model and developing-response display, and held-back recheck case. Reuse the project board/atlas/display/dossier and source log; provide large-print/digital equivalents. Finished copies and keys remain Step 6.

**Next lesson handoff:** Prepare verified Twelve Tables translation and dated maps; reuse H3 citizenship comparison. Carry saved private evidence and unresolved component notes to H4-W1-G; use its opening retrieval/clinic budget for a fresh check. Final unresolved needs go in the receiving teacher record. No homework required.

### H4-W4-G — Hold the council and apply new evidence

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Outcomes:** Teacher-recorded response, fresh source inference and independent revision; team council quality assessed separately. Explain relevant evidence and context while identifying a limitation.

**Learning and project contribution:** Evidence council, fresh historical source interpretation and course-wide inquiry reflection.

**Starting work:** All-course source selection, comparison and causation.

**Selected resources:** [OpenStax — World History Volume 1, to 1500](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/1-introduction); [British Museum — Greece and Rome](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/departments/greece-and-rome). In class: 150–250 words of attributed teacher adaptation from Held-back account/map; reuse H4 dossier, with at most two map/image/source cards. Read once for context and reread the cited clue; preview 3–5 topic terms. Adaptations are proposed materials, not finished packets.

**Direct section links:** Reuse the previously attributed packet; fresh cases remain to produce.

**Source record:** OpenStax, World History Volume 1 (2023), modern secondary synthesis from Rice University: teaching audience, context/explanation purpose. For every reproduced primary item retain original creator or unknown, approximate date, find/origin place, intended audience/purpose when evidenced, collection/translation and stable source. Unknown metadata stays unknown; do not invent it.

Primary-object records and adaptation fit must be checked during material production.

#### Teacher procedure

1. Retrieve starting work: All-course source selection, comparison and causation. Ask learners to show one prior claim and its evidence; use gaps to select the short support clinic.
2. Read the bounded selection and inspect metadata. Learners mark one observation, one interpretation and an unfamiliar term. Teacher supplies topic context from: Evidence council, fresh historical source interpretation and course-wide inquiry reflection.
3. Demonstrate: Model civil critique: identify claim, relevant source, limitation and evidence that would change the conclusion. Think aloud using the different-content worked example below; learners explain why the inference is bounded before trying their own clue.
4. Build from saved work: Teams present actual-history explanations; every student responds to a question; independently analyze a fresh source and revise the causal account. Assign recorder, evidence checker, questioner and organizer in group blocks, rotating next block; everyone keeps a private reasoning note. In individual blocks work independently after the guided turn.
5. Collect the private response to the exact assessment prompt below. Allow source log and vocabulary aids, but no team-composed answers; record oral/text access separately. Ask a follow-up when a named component is missing.
6. Feedback and revision: If performance masks missing history, use targeted map/chronology/source recheck; record remaining learning for the next course. Use the developing response to contrast assertion with supported reasoning. Save the original plus correction, label unresolved learning, and carry it to the next block clinic.

**Explicit instruction:** Model civil critique: identify claim, relevant source, limitation and evidence that would change the conclusion.

**Worked example / expected reasoning:** A fresh account can strengthen one causal mechanism while leaving another uncertain. Civil critique asks what evidence would change a claim and avoids attacking its author personally.

**Developing response and feedback:** Developing response: “Hold the council and apply new evidence proves everyone lived the same way.” Feedback: identify the time/place and one concrete clue, explain the mechanism, then name a person/group or claim the source cannot establish. Recheck with the different case specified below.

**Student task directions:** Start with your saved notes. Read the assigned source cards and mark the clue you use. Teams present actual-history explanations; every student responds to a question; independently analyze a fresh source and revise the causal account. Explain your own answer to the teacher prompt. Show one change you made after feedback and why.

#### Assessment and response to difficulty

**Private prompt:** Respond privately to a held-back historical account/map: identify claim, context, credibility and a limit. Explain whether it changes your causal account. Support one council response with evidence and record your own revision.

**Collect:** Teacher-recorded response, fresh source inference and independent revision; team council quality assessed separately.

**Evidence conditions:** Practice is coached. The private check is completed without a teammate supplying reasoning, with the assigned source log available. Equivalent oral, dictated or typed responses preserve the same history/evidence demands. Teacher records support and unanswered components separately; unobserved learning is not mastery.

**Quality criteria:** Civil discussion and supported reasoning; new evidence changes or sustains a conclusion for a stated reason.

**Descriptive assessment:** Secure for the specified task: Civil discussion and supported reasoning; new evidence changes or sustains a conclusion for a stated reason. Developing: relevant facts/clues present but a required explanation, date, comparison or limitation is missing; give component feedback. Needs further evidence: unsupported/inaccurate account or no independent response; reteach and collect a fresh check. Group accuracy/source attribution and organization receive separate descriptive feedback, not an individual achievement substitute.

**Difficulty and teacher response:** If performance masks missing history, use targeted map/chronology/source recheck; record remaining learning for the next course.

**Different-example recheck:** Use a different dated source card with one unfamiliar claim and full attribution. Ask which part of the previous conclusion still holds, what the new clue supports and what remains unknown. Collect a fresh explanation after the clinic, without copying the worked example.

**Extension:** Compare a plausible alternative interpretation using one existing source. Identify what additional evidence would discriminate between it and the current claim; do not add another polished product.

**Preparation:** Prepare the bounded source adaptation, map/image cards with captions/text equivalents, vocabulary strip, private response prompt, model and developing-response display, and held-back recheck case. Reuse the project board/atlas/display/dossier and source log; provide large-print/digital equivalents. Finished copies and keys remain Step 6.

**Next lesson handoff:** Archive inquiry growth and unresolved content; use a class-approved exhibit correction as a bounded civic practice, without a full informed-action indicator claim. Carry saved private evidence and unresolved component notes to the next history course; use its opening retrieval/clinic budget for a fresh check. Final unresolved needs go in the receiving teacher record. No homework required.
