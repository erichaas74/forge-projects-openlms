# Grade 6 Math - M3 Fold-to-Fit Cargo

Updated October 2, 2026. Teacher planning document: eight 90-minute lessons, four weekly individual/group pairs. Stable lesson IDs connect the course map and workbook.

**Planning status:** Steps 1-5 planning workflow completed with a pacing condition: the 48-hour sequence assumes grade-appropriate entry skills and needs a practical trial, especially M2-W2-G. Materials, calibrated examples and final accessibility checks remain Step 6. This is a 16-week project sequence, not a verified full-year mathematics course or a claim of student mastery.

**Project at a glance:** Design a small cargo box, draw its flat pattern, and show how much it holds and how much material it uses. Test a model and change the design when the capacity requirement grows.

**Final product:** One design file with a coordinate footprint, area/volume calculations, a labeled rectangular-prism net and a small paper model. Add a short comparison with a triangular-prism net and a revised design after the new capacity requirement.

**Essential question:** How can we prove that a container fits its cargo without wasting material?

**What good work shows:** Correct lengths and units; area justified by decomposition; fractional volume connected to cube count; all faces included in surface area; cargo fit verified in each dimension; revision meets the stated constraints.

### Project design brief

Proposed container: 3 dm by 2 dm by 1.5 dm; cargo block 2.5 dm by 1.5 dm by 1 dm. Initial capacity at least 9 cubic dm; material limit 32 square dm for faces only. Paper models use teacher-supplied templates, 1 cm representing 1 dm; ignore material thickness and do not grade scale-drawing construction. Glue tabs are extra and excluded from mathematical surface area. Final update requires at least 12 cubic dm with no edge longer than 3 dm and face material at most 32 square dm. A 3x2x2 dm container is feasible. A separate triangular-prism example has a 3-4-5 right-triangle base and length 6 dm; it is a surface-area comparison, not a requirement to find triangular-prism volume.

### Materials to build in Step 6

Produce right/nonright triangle, parallelogram, trapezoid and L-shape area cards; coordinate footprints; half-unit cube diagram; rectangular and triangular nets with every length supplied; precut paper model templates, ruler, tape and blunt scissors. Provide accessible preassembled/tactile models. Prepare a complete design file and a developing net missing a face for Step 6 calibration.

### M3-W1-I - Decompose the footprint

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Explain area through rearranging and splitting figures, including nonright triangles and special quadrilaterals.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Teach: area composition/decomposition across figure types. Practice: footprint choice. Check: annotated shapes and new triangle. Builds on: rectangle area and M1 conversions.

**Resources and reading:** Cargo brief plus five labeled shape cards: right triangle 6x4; nonright triangle base 6/perpendicular height 4; parallelogram base 8/height 3; trapezoid bases 8 and 4/height 3; L-shape 6x4 rectangle missing 2x2 corner. Read dimensions and unit labels; shape diagrams carry the main information.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Pair congruent triangles to make a parallelogram; show why half the base-height product is valid. Rearrange a parallelogram into a rectangle. Split the trapezoid and L-shape without overlap/gaps. Model area units as squares, not length units.

**Activity design:** 1. Learners annotate base/height on right and nonright triangles. 2. Decompose the quadrilateral and L-shape cards with two colors and calculate areas. 3. Choose a simple rectangular box footprint for the cargo and explain how area differs from perimeter. 4. Record conversions for both length and labeled dimensions before area calculation.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Collect areas 12,12,24,18,20 square units with decomposition lines; each learner independently explains a new triangle base 5/height 2.4 (area 6). Retrieve 2.5 dm=25 cm and 2.4x5=12 with a standard decimal algorithm.

**How this work is judged:** A sloping side is not automatically the height. Require a perpendicular height and a decomposition accounting for the whole figure. Assess reasoning as well as area values.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If learners multiply a sloping side as height, mark a right angle to the base and compare two triangles sharing base/height. Recheck base 7,height 4 -> area 14. If L-shape pieces overlap, color each unit region once.

**Preparation and handoff:** Prepare clearly distinguishable sloped sides and perpendicular heights. M3-W1-G opens with one area recheck before plotting the footprint.

**Mathematical practice:** MP7: see familiar shapes inside a complex boundary.

**Sequential clock:** 0-10: retrieval and resource reading; 10-35: two short modeling/guided-practice segments; 35-70: individual project application and teacher support; 70-85: independent evidence and different-example recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.1. Supporting: 6.RP.A.3, 6.NS.B.3. Confirmed in plan for the specified components; lesson materials are not yet produced and student learning is unobserved. Primary means a central teaching/review and individual evidence target; it is separate from the project's home-standard assignment.

### M3-W1-G - Plot the cargo footprint

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Draw a coordinate polygon and find horizontal/vertical side lengths; revisit rational coordinates, reflection and signed order.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Teach: coordinate polygons and side lengths. Revisit: rational positions, reflections and order. Practice: footprint fit. Check: new polygon. Builds on: M2 maps and M3 area.

**Resources and reading:** Four-quadrant grid with half-units; footprint vertices (-1.5,-1), (1.5,-1), (1.5,1), (-1.5,1). Read ordered pairs and cargo width/length; define vertex.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Plot in order, close the polygon, and find width 3 and height 2 by axis distances. Compare (-1.5,1) with its reflections. Explain -1.5<1.5 but equal absolute values; use horizontal/vertical differences without diagonal distance.

**Activity design:** 1. Each learner plots the footprint before team comparison. 2. Teams verify that a 2.5x1.5 cargo footprint fits inside a 3x2 rectangle. 3. Label all sides, area and a reflected footprint. 4. Correct a deliberately swapped coordinate and retain the checked drawing in the design file.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Individually draw rectangle (-2,-0.5),(1,-0.5),(1,1.5),(-2,1.5); find sides 3 and 2 and area 6. Explain one reflection and compare -0.5 with -2 by order and distance from zero.

**How this work is judged:** Require correct vertex order, aligned axes, side distances and dimension-wise fit. Equal area alone does not prove cargo fits. Signed comparison and reflection are distinct annotations.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If a student says any equal-area cargo fits, compare 6x1 and 3x2 rectangles. Recheck whether 4x1 fits a 3x2 footprint without bending (it does not in the required aligned orientation). If a side crosses zero, count intervals.

**Preparation and handoff:** Prepare overlays with cargo aligned to box edges; no rotation optimization. M3-W2-I opens with coordinate/unit rechecks and brings the footprint into the volume model.

**Mathematical practice:** MP4: use a coordinate drawing to test a physical claim.

**Sequential clock:** 0-10: retrieval and reading the brief; 10-30: model and guided example; 30-70: team project work; collect private annotations during work; 70-85: individual evidence and targeted recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.3. Supporting: 6.NS.C.6, 6.NS.C.7, 6.NS.C.8, 6.G.A.1. Confirmed in plan for the specified components; lesson materials are not yet produced and student learning is unobserved. ### M3-W2-I - Build fractional volume

**Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Connect fractional unit cubes, edge multiplication and base-area-times-height.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Teach: fractional-cube volume and two formulas. Practice: capacity proof. Check: new prism and fraction count. Builds on: fraction division, decimal multiplication and formula substitution.

**Resources and reading:** 3x2x1.5 dm box diagram and half-dm cube diagram. Read three dimensions and one cargo card. Teacher-provided cube drawing avoids constructing 72 physical cubes.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Count 6x4x3 half-dm cubes =72, each volume 1/8 cubic dm; total 9. Connect V=3x2x1.5 and B=6, V=Bh=6x1.5. Model 1.5/(0.5)=3 as a fraction-division count, not a volume.

**Activity design:** 1. Individually annotate the small-cube count and both volume formulas. 2. Compare box capacity 9 with cargo volume 3.75, then check all three cargo dimensions; volume alone cannot prove fit. 3. Add volume proof to the existing footprint. 4. Use a calculator only after a written decimal method for the sampled calculation.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Fresh prism 2x1.5x0.5 dm with half-dm cubes: 4x3x1=12 cubes, total 1.5 cubic dm. Show both formulas, (3/4)/(1/4)=3 with model/inverse check, and 2.5x1.5=3.75 with a decimal algorithm.

**How this work is judged:** Require cubed units and the small-cube volume factor. Count alone is not the answer in cubic dm. Formula evaluation must use defined dimensions; assess fraction meaning separately from decimal accuracy.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If 72 cubes is called 72 cubic dm, compare a half-dm cube with a one-dm cube (8 fit). Recheck 8 small cubes =1 cubic dm. If V=Bh uses perimeter for B, shade the base and recheck a 2x3 base.

**Preparation and handoff:** Prepare labeled cutaway diagrams with hidden layers visible. M3-W2-G opens with a volume-unit recheck, then distinguishes face material from capacity.

**Mathematical practice:** MP2: distinguish counts, edge length, area and volume.

**Sequential clock:** 0-10: retrieval and resource reading; 10-35: two short modeling/guided-practice segments; 35-70: individual project application and teacher support; 70-85: independent evidence and different-example recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.2. Supporting: 6.NS.A.1, 6.NS.B.3, 6.EE.A.2. Confirmed in plan for the specified components; lesson materials are not yet produced and student learning is unobserved. ### M3-W2-G - Unfold and account for every face

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Connect a solid to rectangular/triangular nets and sum face areas without confusing area with volume.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Teach: rectangular/triangular nets and surface area. Practice: build and annotate one model. Check: individual face accounting. Builds on: area, powers and volume.

**Resources and reading:** Rectangular net for 3x2x1.5 box and triangular-prism net with right triangle sides 3,4,5 and prism length 6. Read dimensions on the diagrams; explain face, net and surface area.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Color opposite faces of the rectangular prism: 2(6+4.5+3)=27 square dm. Unfold the triangular prism: 2(0.5x3x4)+6(3+4+5)=84 square dm. Show each of the five faces; tabs are not faces. Retrieve 2 cubed=8 versus 2 squared=4.

**Activity design:** 1. Every student labels both nets and computes each face. 2. Teams assemble a precut miniature rectangular box and compare it with the drawing. 3. Photograph or sketch the model with dimensions. 4. Attach the triangular-net calculation as a short required comparison; do not add a second elaborate model.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Collect independent net annotations and a fresh 2x1x1.5 rectangular prism: face sum 13 square units. Students identify and calculate both triangular faces and three rectangles in the given comparison, then explain area versus volume. Check 3 squared+2 cubed=17.

**How this work is judged:** All faces must appear exactly once; nets must fold without overlap. Require correct squared units and a separate capacity label. Decorative construction quality does not substitute for mathematics.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If one face is missing, trace all outer surfaces on the solid and match them to the net. Recheck a 1x2x3 prism: six faces, area 22. If students use V for material, compare a face covering with interior cubes.

**Preparation and handoff:** Prepare cut/fold models before class and a verified net with tabs separately marked. M3-W3-I opening retrieval rechecks a missing face or unit error before design comparison.

**Mathematical practice:** MP5: use a physical model to verify a drawing.

**Sequential clock:** 0-10: retrieval and reading the brief; 10-30: model and guided example; 30-70: team project work; collect private annotations during work; 70-85: individual evidence and targeted recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.4. Supporting: 6.EE.A.1, 6.G.A.1, 6.G.A.2. Confirmed in plan for the specified components; lesson materials are not yet produced and student learning is unobserved. Primary means a central teaching/review and individual evidence target; it is separate from the project's home-standard assignment.

### M3-W3-I - Compare designs with formulas

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Revisit geometry while using equivalent formulas and one-step equations to compare two bounded designs.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Revisit: area, volume and nets; algebraic formula structure. Practice: bounded design comparison. Check: fresh missing dimension and explanation. Builds on: M2 properties/equations and M3 geometry.

**Resources and reading:** Existing net and volume proof; design A=3x2x1.5, design B=3x2x2. Read six dimensions and the material limit. Formula tiles; no new model construction.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Show V=lwh=Bh using B=lw, and 2lw+2lh+2wh=2(lw+lh+wh) by distribution. Define h as a varying height or as the unknown in 6h=12; solve h=2 and substitute. Explain why a numerical match for one box is not proof of equivalent formulas.

**Activity design:** 1. Individually compare A: V9/SA27 and B: V12/SA32. 2. Explain a formula equivalence with a property, labeling variables and units. 3. Solve one missing dimension using a one-step equation. 4. Write a 3-4 sentence design choice in the existing file; keep the supporting calculations, not a second report.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Collect an independent formula comparison and fresh 4h=10 -> h=2.5, with V=Bh meaning. Revisit triangle area base 4,height 3 ->6 and a triangular-net face area from W2-G. Explain why 2(a+b)=2a+2b for all allowed a,b.

**How this work is judged:** Require dimension-consistent formulas, correct capacity/material comparison and structural equivalence. Score mathematical choice rather than always favoring the larger box.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If area and volume are ranked as interchangeable, label square/cubic units beside each value and ask which constraint each addresses. Recheck a box with V6 and SA22 against separate limits. For 4h=10, use a balance then verify 4x2.5.

**Preparation and handoff:** Prepare two fixed designs to avoid an unbounded search. M3-W3-G uses the chosen base and carries unresolved equation/area evidence into its opening clinic.

**Mathematical practice:** MP3: support a design choice with separate capacity/material evidence.

**Sequential clock:** 0-10: readiness and retrieval; 10-30: explicit modeling and guided practice; 30-70: individual project work and brief feedback conferences; 70-85: independent evidence and different-example recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.1, 6.G.A.2, 6.G.A.4. Supporting: 6.EE.A.2, 6.EE.A.3, 6.EE.A.4, 6.EE.B.6, 6.EE.B.7. Confirmed in plan for the specified components; lesson materials are not yet produced and student learning is unobserved. ### M3-W3-G - Vary height and test the model

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Connect changing height with volume through V=6h and revisit ratio/decimal reasoning.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Revisit: volume and linked quantities. Practice: height comparison and model check. Check: new rule/table/graph. Builds on: M2 dependent variables and M3 capacity.

**Resources and reading:** Fixed 3x2 base; table heights 0.5,1,1.5,2 dm; existing model. Read a height table and final-design constraints. Use a sketch rather than build four models.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Model B=6 square dm and V=6h; label h independent and V dependent. Compute volumes 3,6,9,12 and plot ordered pairs. Distinguish the mathematical nonnegative rule from feasible positive box heights.

**Activity design:** 1. Teams build a height-volume table and graph for the fixed base. 2. Each learner explains a new row. 3. Test cargo dimensions against the paper model and annotate fit/material limitations. 4. Revise the design file using measurements; write the required conversions before comparing units.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Individually represent V=4h for h=0.5,1,1.5, with outputs 2,4,6 and labeled axes. Explain the multiplier and compute 1.25x4=5 by a decimal algorithm; convert 1.5 dm to 15 cm with ratio reasoning.

**How this work is judged:** Require agreement among equation/table/graph and a dimensional fit check; a model observation alone does not establish the fractional-cube standard. Retrieve W2-I cube evidence.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If volume grows by adding the base area to the height, compare two stacked layers with V=Bh. Recheck B=3,h=2.5 ->7.5. If model centimeters are confused with design decimeters, label both and use the teacher-provided scale key.

**Preparation and handoff:** Prepare fixed-base axes and scale labels. M3-W4-I targets remaining cube-count, net or coordinate evidence, with the current design as the only main product.

**Mathematical practice:** MP8: explain repeated equal layers as a multiplicative rule.

**Sequential clock:** 0-10: retrieval and correction feedback; 10-25: review/model changed condition; 25-70: team revision with short individual conferences; 70-85: independent evidence/recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.2. Supporting: 6.RP.A.3, 6.NS.B.3, 6.EE.C.9, 6.G.A.4. ### M3-W4-I - Complete the geometry justification

**Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Integrate four geometry families with concise individual annotations rather than a new design report.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Revisit: area, volume, coordinate polygons and nets. Practice: final design justification. Check: fresh four-family card. Builds on: W1-W3 geometry evidence.

**Resources and reading:** Existing design file, model and short fresh geometry card. Read one polygon, one prism and one net; reuse earlier work for already-observed components.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Run a 15-minute need workshop on one of: missing perpendicular height, fractional cube volume, coordinate side length, or net face accounting. Model on a different figure before independent evidence.

**Activity design:** 1. Learners audit the file against fit, capacity and face-material requirements. 2. Complete one missing explanation and label each unit. 3. Check the model against the drawing. 4. Complete the fresh geometry card with representations; bring corrections into the final design rather than make a separate poster.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Card: area of a nonright triangle base 8,height 3 =12; prism 1.5x1x0.5 with half-unit cubes (6 cubes, 0.75 cubic units); coordinate rectangle (-1,0),(2,0),(2,2),(-1,2), sides3/2; rectangular net 2x2x1, SA16. Use prior triangular-net evidence, with a targeted recheck if missing.

**How this work is judged:** Require a reason for each method, appropriate units, labeled representations and all faces/cubes accounted for. One correct formula answer does not replace the intended model.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If responses are only numbers, ask the learner to mark the part of the picture each factor represents, then recheck a changed dimension privately. Record unsupported or unobserved components rather than infer them from the model.

### M3-W4-G - Meet the changed cargo requirement

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Revise capacity and surface area together while keeping the cargo fit and size constraints.

**Skills and prerequisite:** Revisit: capacity and face material. Practice: changed design. Check: private revision and targeted transfer. Builds on: completed geometry file.

**Resources and reading:** Update card: V at least 12 cubic dm, no edge over3 dm, face material at most 32 square dm. Existing net/model and two allowable candidate sketches 3x2x2 and 3x3x1.5.
[Full project brief and material specifications](#project-design-brief) · [Tennessee source](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/stateboardofeducation/documents/standards/math-standards-recommendation-committee-2020/2021%20Math%20Standards%20Edited%20October%202021.pdf#page=68)

**Teacher instruction and modeling:** Review constraint checking with unrelated dimensions; distinguish ideal mathematical faces from extra glue tabs. Demonstrate how to label a revised dimension without recopying the entire file.

**Activity design:** 1. Teams evaluate the two candidate designs; 3x2x2 gives V12/SA32, while 3x3x1.5 gives V13.5/SA36 and exceeds material. 2. Revise the net and annotate the existing model or a sketch. 3. Demonstrate dimension-wise cargo fit and explain the rejected alternative. 4. Each learner records a private changed-volume/face-area explanation.

**Individual work to collect / assessment prompt:** Collect the individual comparison and an explanation of the added faces/height effect; use a fresh prism/net for each learner's outstanding component in the 15-minute recheck window.

**How this work is judged:** The revision must meet all three constraints and fit the cargo; count every face and use cubic versus square units. Do not grade a rushed rebuild or decoration.

**Difficulty: observable signal, response and fresh recheck:** If learners choose the largest capacity without checking material, mark each constraint beside the candidate calculations. Recheck 2x2x3: V12, SA32 and all edges at most 3. Compare cargo dimensions in the aligned orientation before acceptance.

**Preparation and handoff:** Produce final candidate cards and answer guidance. Archive geometry evidence; M4 begins statistical work and only revisits its assigned number standards, so schedule any remaining geometry support explicitly within this project's final window.

**Mathematical practice:** MP1: revise a design when one constraint fails.

**Sequential clock:** 0-5: read performance brief; 5-40: team performance, change and revision; 40-50: paired defenses or table visits; 50-70: private fresh application; 70-85: targeted reteaching and different-example recheck; 85-90: archive and handoff. Total 90 minutes.

**Standards match:** Primary: 6.G.A.2, 6.G.A.4. Supporting: 6.G.A.1. Confirmed in plan for the specified components; lesson materials are not yet produced and student learning is unobserved.
