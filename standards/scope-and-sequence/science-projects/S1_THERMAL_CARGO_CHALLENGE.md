# S1 — Thermal Cargo Challenge

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning drafted and reviewed; materials, access arrangements and classroom timing remain to verify in Step 6. This is a 16-week sequence, not a verified full-year course or student mastery claim.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_SCIENCE_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Science workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_Science_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

## Project overview

**Question:** How can we keep a small cargo sample cool using a tested design?

**What students do:** Investigate energy transfer, build a small insulated carrier, compare results and improve it.

**Final product:** One reusable design record with labeled energy model, investigation plan, prototype sketches, test table/graph and justified revision; a physical carrier and a short individual explanation.

**Lasting understandings:**
- Energy moves between a system and surroundings; insulation slows transfer rather than making cold.
- Fair comparisons and repeated measurements help distinguish promising designs.
- Testing and combining useful features can improve a design within constraints.

**Schedule:** Four weeks; eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group each week. No required homework. Minimum 30 minutes of actual project work per block. Class-size assumption: 24 students/six groups of four; confirm before teaching.

**Evidence and access:** Keep individual reasoning and support records separate from team product quality. Use the lesson-specific criteria below; no invented weights or cutoffs. Provide readable diagrams, vocabulary previews, chunked text, captions and equivalent oral/text responses while preserving the scientific task. Physical construction/test expectations still require direct accessible participation. Extend with an alternate explanation or constraint, not an extra polished product.

### S1-W1-I — Trace energy into and out of the cargo

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Define the system and explain why a cold sample warms.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). One cargo diagram and about 150 words; read the diagram aloud together. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a warm drink cooling: draw sample, container and room; arrows show transfer from warmer to cooler objects. Contrast temperature with total thermal energy. Use a rolling ball, stretched band and battery-driven fan as separate energy-accounting examples; energy is transferred, not used up.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Record a prediction for identical cold samples in bare and wrapped cups.
2. Observe teacher-prepared temperature readings at start and finish; label system boundaries.
3. Build a before/after energy model and annotate conduction, moving-fluid convection and radiation paths.
4. Use short energy-source cards to trace gravitational, elastic and chemical stores into motion/thermal effects.
5. Revise the cargo explanation after a partner challenges the direction of an arrow.

**Individual work and assessment:** Collect each student’s labeled cargo model and one energy-source transfer chain; a fresh warm-lunchbox prompt checks transfer direction.

**Quality criteria:** Energy arrows run from warmer surroundings toward the cold cargo; name the system, source and destination. Preserve energy accounting; temperature is not energy amount.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If cold is drawn flowing outward, compare two objects at different temperatures, redraw energy arrows, then recheck an ice pack beside a warm bottle. Extend by changing the system boundary.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare safe sealed samples and actual readings; carry unresolved arrow/temperature errors into W1-G opening. Save the same model for revision.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Define the system and explain why a cold sample warms.  
Practice: Annotate a system and an energy chain.  
Check: Collect each student’s labeled cargo model and one energy-source transfer chain; a fresh warm-lunchbox prompt checks transfer direction.  
Builds on: Read a thermometer and compare numbers.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: PS3.A/B; ETS1.A/B. Practice: Develop a model. Crosscutting reasoning: Energy and matter.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-PS3-3. Supporting: MS-PS3-4. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S1-W1-G — Set criteria and compare carrier ideas

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Turn the cargo need into measurable requirements before building.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). Cargo brief and three small material cards; at most 200 new words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model criteria versus constraints using a rainproof book bag, then return to cargo. Propose a local design target: least warming over 10 minutes among feasible carriers; equal 100 mL samples, comparable start temperatures and room conditions. Set a fixed material allowance, not a grading cutoff.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Agree on success measures, allowed materials and size limits.
2. Sketch two different carriers and label their expected thermal effects.
3. Compare them in one criteria table, including cost/reuse and accessibility.
4. Choose one design with a reason and record a concern to test.
5. Each learner independently identifies a controlled variable and a possible unfair comparison.

**Individual work and assessment:** Two sketches, shared criteria table and private control-variable note.

**Quality criteria:** Requirements are measurable; both designs face the same constraints; choice refers to a transfer mechanism and a tradeoff.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If a criterion is “looks good,” replace it with an observable measure and recheck a new example. Offer a labeled sketch frame; extension compares reusable-material waste.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare material cards and inspect kit access for every learner; bring selected sketch into investigation planning.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Turn the cargo need into measurable requirements before building.  
Practice: Compare two designs using common criteria.  
Check: Two sketches, shared criteria table and private control-variable note.  
Builds on: System/transfer model from W1-I.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.A; ETS1.B. Practice: Define problems and evaluate solutions. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

### S1-W2-I — Plan a fair thermal investigation

**Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Separate material and mass effects from uncontrolled changes.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). A two-part investigation card and small labeled data table; about 150 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Demonstrate how equal temperature does not imply equal stored energy for different masses. Plan matched comparisons: same material/different masses and same mass/different materials under measured equal energy input. Explain sensors, measurement intervals, replicates and controls; equal heating time alone does not guarantee equal energy transfer.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Write a question and prediction for each comparison.
2. Specify input energy, material, mass and temperature measurements without calculating joules.
3. Identify controls and at least two repeats; sketch the apparatus.
4. Critique a deliberately confounded example and repair it.
5. Use a supplied source-verified table to explain what result would support or challenge the prediction.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual investigation plan addressing material, mass, transfer and temperature change; a corrected unfair-test example.

**Quality criteria:** Vary one factor at a time; match energy input where claimed; connect temperature change to average particle motion. Name measurement limits.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If a student equates equal minutes with equal transfer, show different heaters and ask what must be measured; recheck the plan. Provide a variable grid without choosing the variables for them.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Produce keyed empirical or clearly labeled illustrative tables and the measurement diagram. Carry control checks into prototype tests.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Separate material and mass effects from uncontrolled changes.  
Practice: Repair and explain a controlled plan.  
Check: Individual investigation plan addressing material, mass, transfer and temperature change; a corrected unfair-test example.  
Builds on: Thermometer reading, controlled variables.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: PS3.A/B. Practice: Plan investigations. Crosscutting reasoning: Scale, proportion and quantity.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-PS3-4. Supporting: MS-PS3-3. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S1-W2-G — Build and measure the first carrier

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Construct the proposed device and collect comparable evidence.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). Illustrated setup and safety checklist; no new article. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Show equal water volumes, matched starting temperatures, thermometer placement and reading at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 minutes. Model recording an unexpected result without changing it. Keep the comparison cup exposed to the same surroundings.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Assemble the carrier with pre-cut permitted materials.
2. Use trays and sealed/secured sample cups; assign operator, recorder, timer and observer roles that rotate.
3. Run a 10-minute trial beside a bare control and repeat after resetting samples.
4. During intervals, label the prototype energy pathways and calculate temperature changes.
5. Clean up and each student explains one measurement and a limitation.

**Individual work and assessment:** Actual prototype, two-trial table with units, individual feature-to-mechanism explanation.

**Quality criteria:** The device exists and was tested; data include controls and honest anomalies. Explain a feature’s effect rather than claiming proof from one favorable reading.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If start conditions differ, mark the comparison limited and reset the next run; recheck with a matched pair. Offer an accessible recording role plus direct individual design/test participation.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Verify thermometers and kits; no flames, mains wiring, sharp construction or unsupervised hot liquids. Preserve data for class comparison.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Construct the proposed device and collect comparable evidence.  
Practice: Construct, measure and explain a device.  
Check: Actual prototype, two-trial table with units, individual feature-to-mechanism explanation.  
Builds on: Fair-test plan and selected sketch.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.B/C; PS3.A/B; ETS1.A/B. Practice: Design/test solutions. Crosscutting reasoning: Energy and matter.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-PS3-3, MS-ETS1-4. Supporting: MS-PS3-4. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S1-W3-I — Compare results across three designs

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Identify useful features from several tested carriers.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). Three class-design records and graph labels; no extra reading. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a comparison using different example data: examine start temperature, control, repeats and material use before judging warming rate. A small difference within measurement variation may be inconclusive.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Plot or annotate the two trials from your carrier.
2. Compare at least three class designs using the agreed criteria.
3. Identify a useful feature in two different designs and explain its mechanism.
4. Sketch one combined revision and predict an observable change.
5. Independently justify the combination and identify one uncertainty.

**Individual work and assessment:** Comparison of three designs, combined-feature sketch and individual evidence-based justification.

**Quality criteria:** Use test data and common criteria; select features for scientific reasons; distinguish a prediction from an observed improvement.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If “best” means lowest final temperature despite unequal starts, compare changes and controls then recheck another pair. Extension identifies whether combining features could add unwanted mass.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Compile actual class data with provenance; if unavailable, clearly label a practice dataset and retain the real-test requirement for W3-G.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Identify useful features from several tested carriers.  
Practice: Compare multiple designs and plan a combined revision.  
Check: Comparison of three designs, combined-feature sketch and individual evidence-based justification.  
Builds on: Tables, temperature differences, controls.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.B; ETS1.B/C. Practice: Analyze data. Crosscutting reasoning: Patterns.

### S1-W3-G — Improve and retest the carrier

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Use the first evidence to revise a feature and measure again.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). Previous plans and concise reset checklist. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a revision record: what changed, why, expected effect, what remained controlled. Explain why no improvement is still useful evidence.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Modify the selected feature combination within the same constraints.
2. Run two reset trials with the unchanged control procedure.
3. Compare original/revised temperature changes and repeat variation.
4. Refine the energy model to explain supported or unsupported predictions.
5. Each student chooses retain, modify or reject with a data-based reason.

**Individual work and assessment:** Revised physical carrier, new trial records and private revision decision.

**Quality criteria:** Modification responds to evidence; testing is comparable; conclusions match results without invented success.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If teams alter several features without a rationale, identify the inference limit and plan a next isolating comparison. Recheck with a single-feature cause/effect prompt.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Provide spare materials/reset samples; carry any unobserved physical participation into W4-I supervised support.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Use the first evidence to revise a feature and measure again.  
Practice: Modify and retest a physical model.  
Check: Revised physical carrier, new trial records and private revision decision.  
Builds on: Three-design comparison and revision hypothesis.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.B/C; PS3.A/B; ETS1.A/B. Practice: Develop/use models. Crosscutting reasoning: Systems and system models.

### S1-W4-I — Explain the final design independently

**Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Turn the tested work into a clear scientific explanation.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). Own design record and one new investigation prompt. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Compare an evidence-based explanation with “the fabric keeps cold in.” Model claim, measurements, mechanism and limitation using a different device. Revisit material/mass controls briefly.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Use opening support to repair the named unresolved component.
2. Complete the design record using existing sketches/data rather than recopying.
3. Individually explain energy sources and transfer mechanisms for the carrier.
4. Plan a new controlled comparison involving a different mass or material.
5. Revise the explanation after precise feedback and mark supported versus solo evidence.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual explanation and fresh investigation plan attached to the shared design record.

**Quality criteria:** Explain conservation and mechanisms; use actual data; retain controlled comparison logic across changed values/materials.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If evidence is listed but not explained, connect one temperature change to one mechanism orally then recheck a fresh case. Offer a scribe/audio response while retaining individual reasoning.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare fresh prompt and key; identify who still needs direct test evidence before W4-G.

Teach / revisit: Turn the tested work into a clear scientific explanation.  
Practice: Explain and transfer investigation reasoning.  
Check: Individual explanation and fresh investigation plan attached to the shared design record.

### S1-W4-G — Demonstrate the carrier and transfer the learning

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Share the tested solution and respond to an unfamiliar cargo case.

**Resources:** [U.S. Department of Energy — Heat Flow](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/heat-flow). Final record and short new-case card. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a concise demonstration using already collected data and a safe setup; distinguish retelling procedures from explaining a result. State that the new case asks for reasoning, not an unplanned rebuild.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Finish a targeted supervised check if physical construction/testing evidence is missing.
2. Teams demonstrate the carrier and explain one improvement using the graph.
3. Each member explains a mechanism or test decision; peers record a useful question.
4. Individually recommend changes for keeping a warm sample warm and justify energy direction.
5. Archive the unresolved learning record for S2 energy models and S3 convection.

**Individual work and assessment:** Team demonstration quality, individual oral contribution and fresh thermal-transfer response kept separately.

**Quality criteria:** Claims refer to measured evidence; explain a useful feature combination and limits; correctly transfer direction to a warm-cargo case.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If a student repeats the team answer without a mechanism, use a brief different-material prompt and record a targeted next check. Do not infer competence from attendance.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Assume six teams of four: 30 minutes for six 5-minute demonstrations; verify class size. Teacher records contributions across earlier lessons and today.
