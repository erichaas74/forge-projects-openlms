# S2 — Tennessee Ecosystem Detectives

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning drafted and reviewed; materials, access arrangements and classroom timing remain to verify in Step 6. This is a 16-week sequence, not a verified full-year course or student mastery claim.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_SCIENCE_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Science workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_Science_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

## Project overview

**Question:** What evidence explains an ecosystem change, and which response could help?

**What students do:** Investigate changing populations, trace food-web effects and recommend a response to an invasive species using evidence.

**Final product:** One case file with an ecosystem model, annotated data, competing explanations and a revised invasive-species response; each student writes a short independent case conclusion.

**Lasting understandings:**
- Resource changes and organism interactions can alter populations.
- Matter cycles while energy flows through ecosystems.
- Several indicators and credible evidence are needed to evaluate ecosystem health and conservation choices.

**Schedule:** Four weeks; eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group each week. No required homework. Minimum 30 minutes of actual project work per block. Class-size assumption: 24 students/six groups of four; confirm before teaching.

**Evidence and access:** Keep individual reasoning and support records separate from team product quality. Use the lesson-specific criteria below; no invented weights or cutoffs. Provide readable diagrams, vocabulary previews, chunked text, captions and equivalent oral/text responses while preserving the scientific task. Physical construction/test expectations still require direct accessible participation. Extend with an alternate explanation or constraint, not an extra polished product.

### S2-W1-I — Read the ecosystem evidence

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Use living and nonliving resource data to frame the case.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). One paired forest/pond data sheet; about 150 words plus graphs. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model axis reading with an unrelated garden example. Compare organism growth and population count; show that co-occurring trends suggest a hypothesis rather than proving a cause.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Observe contrasting forest and freshwater photos and define each system.
2. Annotate a resource series and population series with units/time.
3. Separate living/nonliving variables; record two patterns.
4. Propose two possible explanations and a missing measurement.
5. Start a case file and revise one unsupported statement.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual annotated resource/population graph and two explanations with evidence limits.

**Quality criteria:** Distinguish growth from number, name a resource, use a data pattern and avoid unsupported causation.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If a student reads higher bars as healthier, label what the bars measure and recheck a different graph. Offer axis-reading scaffold; extend by identifying a confound.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Select empirical data with provenance; synthetic practice must be labeled. Carry graph difficulties into W1-G.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Use living and nonliving resource data to frame the case.  
Practice: Interpret resource/population patterns.  
Check: Individual annotated resource/population graph and two explanations with evidence limits.  
Builds on: Read tables/axes and identify organism needs.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.A. Practice: Analyze data. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

### S2-W1-G — Predict interactions across habitats

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Compare competition, predation and symbiosis in forest and pond systems.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). Six short organism-pair cards, maximum 200 words total. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a predator/prey explanation in grassland, then mutual benefit and competition. Include parasitism/commensalism as symbiotic patterns without assuming all symbiosis helps both organisms.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Sort organism relationships using effects on each organism.
2. Build small forest and pond interaction diagrams.
3. Predict changes if food becomes scarce; explain the mechanism.
4. Challenge one prediction using a competing interpretation.
5. Each student predicts an unfamiliar pair and revises a diagram.

**Individual work and assessment:** Two-habitat comparison and individual new-interaction prediction.

**Quality criteria:** Explain effects on both organisms and carry a pattern across ecosystems; distinguish competition from predation.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If every interaction is labeled predation, ask what is consumed and recheck two organisms sharing food. Provide relation arrows; extension examines indirect effects.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare checked organism facts; keep interaction diagrams for food webs.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Compare competition, predation and symbiosis in forest and pond systems.  
Practice: Compare and predict relationships.  
Check: Two-habitat comparison and individual new-interaction prediction.  
Builds on: Resource limitations and population patterns.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.A. Practice: Construct explanations. Crosscutting reasoning: Patterns.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-LS2-2. Supporting: MS-LS2-1. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S2-W2-I — Trace matter and energy through the web

**Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Model living/nonliving connections and energy availability.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). One food-web diagram and energy-pyramid caption; about 120 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Use a meadow to model Sun-to-producer energy and matter moving through organisms, air, water, soil and decomposers. Energy is transferred/dispersed rather than cycling like matter. Avoid a universal 10-percent rule.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Draw a bounded freshwater food web using verified organism cards.
2. Use distinct arrow styles for feeding/energy and matter transfers.
3. Add decomposers and nonliving reservoirs.
4. Build an energy pyramid explaining less energy available at higher levels without claiming disappearance.
5. Individually trace one matter path and one energy path, then revise an arrow.

**Individual work and assessment:** Student ecosystem model with boundary, nonliving reservoirs, food web and energy pyramid.

**Quality criteria:** Arrows have meaning; matter cycles; energy enters/leaves; decomposers and heat transfer are accounted for.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If energy loops to the Sun, trace one energy unit through the model and redraw; fresh check follows a different organism. Provide icons; extend by changing system boundaries.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare keyed card relationships and an accessible tactile/text model option; carry models into case analysis.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Model living/nonliving connections and energy availability.  
Practice: Develop a two-pathway ecosystem model.  
Check: Student ecosystem model with boundary, nonliving reservoirs, food web and energy pyramid.  
Builds on: Interactions and S1 conservation.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.B. Practice: Develop/use models. Crosscutting reasoning: Energy and matter.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-LS2-3. Supporting: MS-LS2-2. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S2-W2-G — Compare signs of ecosystem health

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Evaluate multiple indicators in land and water ecosystems.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). Paired monitoring tables and brief biome cards; about 200 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model why desert precipitation should not be judged by rainforest expectations. Compare appropriate reference conditions, biotic diversity/population evidence and abiotic temperature/water/soil measures.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Compare forest and freshwater indicator data to appropriate baselines.
2. Use biome reference cards for tundra, taiga, desert, grassland, rainforest and marine settings to avoid one universal health threshold.
3. Explain one physical and one biological change affecting a population.
4. Evaluate an alternative cause and identify additional evidence needed.
5. Each learner writes a bounded health conclusion for both terrestrial and aquatic cases.

**Individual work and assessment:** Paired individual health explanations supported by indicators and a competing-cause note.

**Quality criteria:** Use both biotic and abiotic patterns; comparisons suit the ecosystem; distinguish biodiversity from sheer abundance.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If one high count is treated as proof of health, compare dominance versus varied species and recheck another case. Provide a baseline/changed table; extension considers sampling effort.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare small source-verified tables and biome cards; retain case conclusions for the invasive-species investigation.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Evaluate multiple indicators in land and water ecosystems.  
Practice: Compare ecosystem indicators and argue from data.  
Check: Paired individual health explanations supported by indicators and a competing-cause note.  
Builds on: Graphs, system boundaries and interaction explanations.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.A; LS2.C. Practice: Analyze data and argue from evidence. Crosscutting reasoning: Stability and change.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-LS2-1, MS-LS2-4. Supporting: MS-LS2-3. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S2-W3-I — Investigate invasive carp evidence

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Connect a specific Tennessee species to native-population effects.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). TWRA Bighead Carp selection and a dated empirical evidence table; about 200 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model tracing an ecological claim back to its evidence. Distinguish invasive from merely nonnative and separate competition evidence from an unsupported assertion that every decline has one cause.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Locate the Tennessee waterway and identify bighead carp and native competitors.
2. Read source statements and annotate a verified population/resource record.
3. Trace a plausible effect through the food web.
4. Compare a competing explanation such as changed water conditions.
5. Individually write claim, measured evidence, mechanism and limitation.

**Individual work and assessment:** Source-linked individual invasive-species argument and revised food web.

**Quality criteria:** Name the species/place; use existing empirical evidence; explain competition and distinguish evidence strength from certainty.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If evidence is only a quoted conclusion, locate the supporting observation and recheck another claim. Offer a source/evidence organizer; extend by evaluating sampling bias.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Verify an empirical dataset/report supporting the case before material readiness; no fish collection or handling. Carry uncertainties to response design.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Connect a specific Tennessee species to native-population effects.  
Practice: Evaluate a named invasive-species case.  
Check: Source-linked individual invasive-species argument and revised food web.  
Builds on: Population evidence, food webs and alternate explanations.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.C. Practice: Argue from evidence. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

### S2-W3-G — Compare responses and ecosystem services

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Evaluate two possible responses to invasive-species impacts.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). Two management summaries and ecosystem-service cards; about 200 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model common comparison criteria: impact on native organisms, feasibility, unintended effects and monitoring. Use a non-carp example. Explain food, clean water, medicine potential, pollination, decomposition and climate-related services without claiming each is supplied by one species.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Compare two evidence-based carp-management approaches, such as targeted removal and movement barriers, using the same criteria.
2. Explain tradeoffs and uncertainty; distinguish mitigation from eradication.
3. Connect biodiversity changes to two human resources/services.
4. Draft a bounded response proposal with a monitoring indicator.
5. Each student explains why the rejected alternative may still have a useful feature.

**Individual work and assessment:** Comparison table, response proposal and private biodiversity/service explanation.

**Quality criteria:** Evaluate both solutions fairly, cite evidence and identify an unintended effect; explain a causal service connection.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If “remove every fish” is proposed, trace native food-web effects and revise; recheck another intervention. Extend by changing a constraint.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare source-checked management cards and limitations; bring response proposal to case revision.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Evaluate two possible responses to invasive-species impacts.  
Practice: Compare conservation solutions.  
Check: Comparison table, response proposal and private biodiversity/service explanation.  
Builds on: Carp evidence and ecosystem-health indicators.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.B; LS2.C; LS4.D; ETS1.B. Practice: Evaluate solutions. Crosscutting reasoning: Stability and change.

### S2-W4-I — Close the case with an individual argument

**Week 4 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Revise the explanation using all the case evidence.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). Existing case file and short fresh evidence note. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Show how a new observation can narrow a claim without erasing useful evidence. Model integrating one graph and one model rather than copying the entire packet.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Use a targeted opening clinic for unresolved data/model errors.
2. Assemble one concise case conclusion using existing annotations.
3. Revise the proposed response after feedback about a risk or missing indicator.
4. Individually explain an ecosystem model and apply it to a new resource change.
5. Mark evidence versus predictions and retain a question for the case conference.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual 150–250-word or equivalent explanation, model annotation and revised response.

**Quality criteria:** Reasoning links population change to evidence/mechanisms; revised response addresses a specific limitation; matter/energy remain distinct.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If a conclusion overstates cause, add the alternative and a discriminating measurement, then recheck a new claim. Oral response may replace writing length while preserving reasoning.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare fresh evidence and feedback examples; identify individual gaps before the conference.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Revise the explanation using all the case evidence.  
Practice: Synthesize and revise a causal argument.  
Check: Individual 150–250-word or equivalent explanation, model annotation and revised response.  
Builds on: Complete evidence file and response comparison.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.B; LS2.C. Practice: Argue from evidence. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-LS2-4, MS-LS2-3. Supporting: MS-LS2-5. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S2-W4-G — Explain the case and transfer to a new ecosystem

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Defend a response and interpret a different ecosystem case.

**Resources:** [Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency — Invasive Carp](https://www.tn.gov/content/tn/twra/wildlife/fish/invasive-carp.html). One new land/aquatic case with short source note and graph. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a concise case conference: claim, evidence, limitation, response. Explain that a new context requires checking its facts rather than copying the Tennessee conclusion.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Teams share case conclusions and answer an evidence question.
2. Each member explains one indicator, interaction or response tradeoff.
3. Individually interpret a new resource graph and predict an interaction.
4. Compare two responses for the new case and justify a choice.
5. Archive the case file; carry conservation criteria to S4.

**Individual work and assessment:** Observed individual contribution, new data interpretation and response comparison.

**Quality criteria:** Transfer a scientific pattern with appropriate evidence; avoid assuming the same intervention suits every ecosystem.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If the old solution is copied, identify a changed condition and re-evaluate; record an S4 opening recheck if needed. Extension asks what cannot be inferred.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Assume six 5-minute team conferences; prepare teacher observation roster and accessible individual response options.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Defend a response and interpret a different ecosystem case.  
Practice: Defend and transfer reasoning.  
Check: Observed individual contribution, new data interpretation and response comparison.  
Builds on: Case argument and ecological comparison criteria.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: LS2.A; LS2.C; LS4.D; ETS1.B. Practice: Communicate explanations. Crosscutting reasoning: Patterns.
