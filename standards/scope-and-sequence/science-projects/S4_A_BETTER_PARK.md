# S4 — A Better Park: Report and Presentation

Updated 2026-10-03. Steps 1–5 planning drafted and reviewed; materials, access arrangements and classroom timing remain to verify in Step 6. This is a 16-week sequence, not a verified full-year course or student mastery claim.

[Course map](../GRADE_6_SCIENCE_SCOPE_AND_SEQUENCE.md) · [Science workbook](<../../6th Grade/Grade_6_Science_Curriculum_Standards.xlsx>)

## Project overview

**Question:** What change would make a neighborhood park better for people and the environment?

**What students do:** Research one park problem, compare two improvements, and revise a scientific recommendation into a concise report used for a presentation.

**Final product:** One six- to eight-slide illustrated report (or equivalent accessible document) and a four- to six-minute team presentation. Include the problem, evidence, options, recommendation, tradeoffs, monitoring and sources; each student explains individual reasoning. No simulation or separate long paper.

**Lasting understandings:**
- Resource use and human actions affect Earth systems and biodiversity.
- A justified recommendation compares benefits, constraints and unintended effects.
- Monitoring and evidence-based revision help evaluate whether an improvement works.

**Schedule:** Four weeks; eight 90-minute blocks, individual then group each week. No required homework. Minimum 30 minutes of actual project work per block. Class-size assumption: 24 students/six groups of four; confirm before teaching.

**Evidence and access:** Keep individual reasoning and support records separate from team product quality. Use the lesson-specific criteria below; no invented weights or cutoffs. Provide readable diagrams, vocabulary previews, chunked text, captions and equivalent oral/text responses while preserving the scientific task. Physical construction/test expectations still require direct accessible participation. Extend with an alternate explanation or constraint, not an extra polished product.

### S4-W1-I — Define a park problem worth changing

**Week 1 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Identify the environmental problem and measurable success criteria.

**Resources:** [EPA — Environmental Benefits of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/environmental-benefits-green-infrastructure); [USGS — The Water Cycle](https://labs.waterdata.usgs.gov/visualizations/water-cycle/index.html). Park case and annotated map; about 200 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a problem statement for a different site: observed issue, affected people/organisms, mechanism and measurable improvement. Distinguish an observation from an assumed cause and a preference from a constraint.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Inspect the shared park map, observations and a small resource-use table.
2. Choose one bounded issue; default is runoff and reduced habitat beside a path.
3. Trace possible water/soil/organism effects and define what needs investigation.
4. Write success criteria, constraints and a research question.
5. Each learner contributes an independent problem/evidence note before the team agrees.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual problem statement and evidence note; shared report outline with criteria.

**Quality criteria:** Problem is bounded and scientifically investigable; criteria measure impact, not decoration; include people and other organisms.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If the goal is “make it nicer,” identify a measurable environmental change and recheck a different proposal. Offer a map glossary; extension identifies a conflicting stakeholder need.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare accessible map/data and choose verified source cases. Different topic choices must retain the same required learning through short in-class checks.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Identify the environmental problem and measurable success criteria.  
Practice: Define a problem and monitoring question.  
Check: Individual problem statement and evidence note; shared report outline with criteria.  
Builds on: S2 ecosystem health and S3 runoff mechanisms.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ESS3.C; ETS1.B; ETS1.A. Practice: Define problems. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-ESS3-3, MS-ETS1-1. Supporting: MS-LS2-4. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S4-W1-G — Explain resource use with data

**Week 1 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Separate population growth, individual consumption and environmental impact.

**Resources:** [U.S. Energy Information Administration — Renewable energy explained](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/renewable-sources/); [USGS — The Water Cycle](https://labs.waterdata.usgs.gov/visualizations/water-cycle/index.html). Two dated population/per-person consumption tables and source notes; about 150 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Use simple hypothetical practice values: 100 users at 2 L each versus 200 at 2 L; then 100 at 4 L. Label the examples invented, then model an argument from the source-verified dataset. Renewable does not mean unlimited or impact-free.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Interpret population and per-capita consumption separately.
2. Calculate small totals with units and annotate a supplied measured trend.
3. Connect the resource demand to a documented Earth-system effect.
4. Compare a resource-saving change and its possible tradeoff.
5. Each student writes an evidence-based argument; save one relevant chart for the report.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual argument addressing both population and per-person use, with source-linked evidence and a mechanism.

**Quality criteria:** Do not confuse totals and rates; distinguish renewable/nonrenewable resources and support the impact claim with evidence.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If per-person use is mistaken for total, use equal groups to separate the variables then recheck new values. Provide calculator/table support; extension considers rebound in use.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare dated empirical dataset and separate clearly labeled arithmetic examples. Carry resource findings into options research.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Separate population growth, individual consumption and environmental impact.  
Practice: Interpret two resource-demand drivers.  
Check: Individual argument addressing both population and per-person use, with source-linked evidence and a mechanism.  
Builds on: Read tables; multiply simple quantities; distinguish claim/evidence.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ESS3.C. Practice: Argue from evidence. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-ESS3-4. Supporting: MS-ESS3-3. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S4-W2-I — Compare energy technologies

**Week 2 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Evaluate existing and developing energy options without a sales pitch.

**Resources:** [U.S. Energy Information Administration — Renewable energy explained](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/renewable-sources/); [NASA — Causes of climate change](https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/causes/); [U.S. Department of Energy — Perovskite Research Directions](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/perovskite-research-directions). Three bounded technology cards, about 250 words total. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Trace source-to-electricity-to-use energy transformations. Compare an existing solar-PV lighting application, another renewable technology such as wind, and perovskite-silicon tandem solar cells in DOE’s dated April 2024 research case. Compare reliability, storage/material needs and impacts using dated evidence; do not imply the electric grid is a single energy source.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Extract comparable facts from the technology cards.
2. Explain one thermal/energy principle from S1 and connect it to energy use or efficiency.
3. Compare benefits, limitations and a practical park need using common criteria.
4. Identify which claim is measured, predicted or still under development.
5. Individually justify an energy recommendation or explain why the park problem does not require new lighting.

**Individual work and assessment:** Individual existing/developing technology comparison and source-quality note; use relevant evidence in the same report.

**Quality criteria:** Investigate more than one technology; distinguish current evidence from proposed performance; explain an energy mechanism and tradeoff.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If renewable is treated as zero impact, trace materials/land/storage needs and recheck another option. Offer energy-flow icons; extension separates energy source from delivery system.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare the DOE 2024 perovskite-silicon case alongside established silicon-PV and wind cards. Compare durability and scale limitations, not unverified current market claims; keep the student adaptation brief.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Evaluate existing and developing energy options without a sales pitch.  
Practice: Compare technology evidence.  
Check: Individual existing/developing technology comparison and source-quality note; use relevant evidence in the same report.  
Builds on: S1 transfer/conservation and resource demand.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ESS3.C; ETS1.B. Practice: Obtain/evaluate information. Crosscutting reasoning: Energy and matter.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-ESS3-3. Supporting: MS-ETS1-2. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S4-W2-G — Compare two improvements for wildlife and people

**Week 2 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Evaluate biodiversity solutions using the same evidence criteria.

**Resources:** [EPA — Environmental Benefits of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/environmental-benefits-green-infrastructure); [EPA — Types of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/types-green-infrastructure). EPA option selections, habitat indicators and brief conservation cases; about 250 words. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Compare a planted rain garden with a permeable-path/habitat-edge option on the default site. Use scientific, feasibility and social criteria; explain why local soils, maintenance and habitat context matter. Include short endangerment/extinction and invasive-species cases without claiming all apply to the fictional park.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Review the same criteria for both options.
2. Trace effects on resources, food-web relationships and at least two ecosystem services.
3. Evaluate relevant empirical evidence and local implementation limits.
4. Use a comparison table to choose a provisional improvement and identify a weakness.
5. Individually justify a biodiversity tradeoff and one way to improve the proposal.

**Individual work and assessment:** Two-option evaluation and each learner’s biodiversity/service explanation and proposed improvement.

**Quality criteria:** Both options receive fair consideration; explanation connects habitat change to organisms/services; conservation/endangerment/extinction concepts are distinguished.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If flower count is treated as biodiversity proof, consider native species/habitat interactions and a monitoring indicator; recheck another claim. Extension evaluates an unintended effect.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare local-condition evidence and short source-checked conservation cards. Keep evaluation tied to the same report, with individual component notes attached.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Evaluate biodiversity solutions using the same evidence criteria.  
Practice: Evaluate and improve a biodiversity proposal.  
Check: Two-option evaluation and each learner’s biodiversity/service explanation and proposed improvement.  
Builds on: S2 interactions, invasive-species reasoning and ecosystem services.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.B; LS2.C; LS4.D; ETS1.B. Practice: Evaluate solutions. Crosscutting reasoning: Stability and change.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-LS2-5, MS-ETS1-2. Supporting: MS-ESS3-3. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S4-W3-I — Draft the report and monitoring plan

**Week 3 / Individual / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Turn the recommendation into a testable, evidence-based proposal.

**Resources:** [EPA — Environmental Benefits of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/environmental-benefits-green-infrastructure); [USGS — The Water Cycle](https://labs.waterdata.usgs.gov/visualizations/water-cycle/index.html). Existing source notes plus one monitoring example; no new long reading. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a six-panel report on a different problem: problem, mechanism/evidence, options, recommendation, monitoring/limits, sources. Specify baseline, indicator, method, frequency, comparison and a criterion for revising the recommendation.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Draft the report using existing maps, notes and comparison table.
2. Add an annotated visual explaining the science and a relevant data display.
3. Describe how implementation success could be monitored without inventing future results.
4. Include costs/maintenance as constraints and identify a possible harm.
5. Each student writes or records a private justification of the monitoring design.

**Individual work and assessment:** Six- to eight-slide draft (or equivalent), individual monitoring method and a source trail.

**Quality criteria:** The recommendation follows evidence; monitoring can detect the intended impact and confounds; predictions are labeled and sources are traceable.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If “check whether it worked” lacks a measure, choose an indicator/time/comparison and recheck with a changed condition. Offer a report frame; extension addresses seasonal variation.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Prepare an accessible report template and different-topic example; bring draft and exact feedback question to W3-G.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Turn the recommendation into a testable, evidence-based proposal.  
Practice: Design monitoring and draft a report.  
Check: Six- to eight-slide draft (or equivalent), individual monitoring method and a source trail.  
Builds on: Evidence comparison and measurable criteria.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ESS3.C; ETS1.B; ETS1.A. Practice: Design solutions. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.

**National standards:** Primary: MS-ESS3-3, MS-ETS1-1. Supporting: MS-LS2-5. Confirmed in plan for the named contribution; whole performance expectation is evidenced across its mapped route. Materials and observed learning remain separate.

### S4-W3-G — Review and strengthen the proposed change

**Week 3 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Use critique to improve the science and biodiversity proposal.

**Resources:** [EPA — Environmental Benefits of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/environmental-benefits-green-infrastructure); [EPA — Types of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/types-green-infrastructure). Drafts, original sources and a concise review checklist. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model useful critique: name a claim, locate supporting evidence and ask about a constraint or unintended effect. Demonstrate a substantive revision, such as relocating a habitat feature, rather than changing colors.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Partners review the same criteria for alternatives and recommended change.
2. Check one source-to-claim connection and one monitoring limitation.
3. Revise the biodiversity strategy and report in response to evidence-based critique.
4. Rehearse each learner’s explanation without scripts replacing understanding.
5. Individually record before/after reasoning and what evidence prompted the change.

**Individual work and assessment:** Revised report, individual revision note and teacher observation of evidence discussion.

**Quality criteria:** Revision changes the proposal or reasoning for a scientific reason; evaluate alternatives and preserve honest uncertainty.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If feedback is only praise, give a claim/evidence/constraint prompt and recheck a new slide. Extension explains when the rejected option would become preferable.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Allocate teacher conferences across six teams during report revision; bring only the unresolved component list to W4-I.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Use critique to improve the science and biodiversity proposal.  
Practice: Critique and revise a solution.  
Check: Revised report, individual revision note and teacher observation of evidence discussion.  
Builds on: Draft report and monitoring proposal.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ETS1.B; LS2.C; LS4.D; ETS1.B. Practice: Argue from evidence. Crosscutting reasoning: Stability and change.

### S4-W4-I — Finish the report and explain your own reasoning

Own report plus short fresh resource/monitoring case. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Show a clear evidence caption and a statement of a limit; use a different park problem. Revisit the weakest resource, technology or biodiversity component in a targeted clinic.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Finish the report without adding a separate long paper.
2. Audit visual labels, claims, source credits and predicted versus observed benefits.
3. Individually explain one resource/energy choice and one biodiversity effect.

### S4-W4-G — Present a change worth making

**Week 4 / Group / 90 minutes.**

**Focus and project connection:** Present the report and answer evidence-based questions.

**Resources:** [EPA — Environmental Benefits of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/environmental-benefits-green-infrastructure); [EPA — Types of Green Infrastructure](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/types-green-infrastructure). Final report and individual question slips; no added research. Teacher-created tasks named below are specifications pending Step 6, not finished handouts.

**Instruction and modeling:** Model a clear recommendation with an evidence reference and honest limit. Explain that the audience is reviewing a proposal, not hearing invented implementation results.

**Activity sequence:**
1. Use a short final clinic for named gaps.
2. Each team gives a four- to six-minute presentation using the report itself.
3. Every student explains part of the science and answers an individual question.
4. Reviewers identify an evidence strength and a question about impact or feasibility.
5. Each learner submits a final note about one justified improvement and one unresolved uncertainty.

**Individual work and assessment:** Presented report, individual oral/question evidence and a final revision/limit note.

**Quality criteria:** Explain the problem, compare alternatives fairly, justify the change and monitoring; assess scientific understanding separately from presentation polish.

**Feedback, support and fresh recheck:** If an answer only repeats a slide, ask for the causal connection using a new example; record a targeted recheck. Allow equivalent live audio/text responses with the same individual criteria.

**Preparation and next handoff:** Assume 24 students in six teams: 36 minutes for six 6-minute slots; collect individual evidence throughout the project. Larger enrollment needs a revised observation schedule before teaching.

**Skills taught and checked:**
Teach / revisit: Present the report and answer evidence-based questions.  
Practice: Present and defend scientific reasoning.  
Check: Presented report, individual oral/question evidence and a final revision/limit note.  
Builds on: Completed report and independently explained recommendation.

**Three-dimensional focus:** DCI contributions: ESS3.C; ETS1.B; LS2.C; LS4.D; ETS1.B. Practice: Communicate information. Crosscutting reasoning: Cause and effect.
